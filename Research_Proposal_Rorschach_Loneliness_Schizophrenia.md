## 2. INTRODUCTION

Schizophrenia is a severe, chronic psychiatric disorder affecting approximately 0.3–0.7% of the global population, characterized by disturbances in thought, perception, emotion, and behavior (American Psychiatric Association, 2013). While pharmacological interventions have achieved considerable success in managing positive symptoms such as hallucinations and delusions, the functional and social impairments associated with schizophrenia remain largely refractory to medication alone (Slade & Salkever, 2001). Among these impairments, one of the most pervasive yet clinically neglected experiences is subjective loneliness — the painful discrepancy between desired and actual social connection.

Loneliness in schizophrenia is not simply a byproduct of social withdrawal or negative symptoms. It is an independent psychological phenomenon with its own cognitive underpinnings, health consequences, and treatment implications. A meta-analysis by Michalska da Rocha et al. (2018) established a significant positive relationship between loneliness and psychotic symptoms, while recent research has linked loneliness to increased psychiatric hospitalization, cognitive decline, and elevated mortality risk in individuals with serious mental illness (Green et al., 2023; Fortuna et al., 2020). The World Health Organization has recognized loneliness as a global public health priority, and a 2024 Indian scoping review identified it as an emerging public mental health predicament in the country (Epidemiology of loneliness in India, PMC11746991).

The cognitive model of loneliness (Cacioppo & Hawkley, 2009) proposes that loneliness is fundamentally a cognitive-perceptual phenomenon. It arises not from objective social isolation alone, but from how an individual processes, perceives, and interprets their social environment. Lonely individuals demonstrate enhanced threat perception, misinterpretation of neutral social stimuli as rejecting, and hypervigilant scanning of the environment — cognitive biases that perpetuate withdrawal and deepen isolation. In schizophrenia, where cognitive impairment in perception, processing, and reasoning is a cardinal feature, this cognitive pathway to loneliness may be substantially magnified.

The Rorschach Inkblot Test (Exner Comprehensive System) offers a unique performance-based assessment of cognitive processing that maps directly onto the cognitive model of loneliness. Exner's Cognitive Triad — comprising Information Processing (how one scans and organizes stimuli), Cognitive Mediation (how accurately one translates perceptions), and Ideation (how logically one reasons about perceptions) — provides a sequential model of the cognitive operations that, when impaired, may render social connection impossible and loneliness inevitable. Unlike neuropsychological batteries that measure performance on structured tasks with clear right/wrong answers, the Rorschach captures how a person navigates ambiguous stimuli — a demand far closer to the ambiguity of real-world social interaction.

Despite converging evidence that (a) loneliness is prevalent and clinically significant in schizophrenia, (b) cognitive-perceptual impairment underlies loneliness, and (c) the Rorschach Cognitive Triad measures exactly those cognitive-perceptual operations, no study — Indian or international — has examined whether Rorschach cognitive triad variables predict subjective loneliness in patients with schizophrenia. The present study proposes to bridge this gap by examining the relationship between Rorschach-derived indices of processing, mediation, and ideation and self-reported loneliness in clinically stable patients with schizophrenia.

## 3. REVIEW OF LITERATURE

### 3.1 Loneliness in Schizophrenia: Prevalence and Clinical Significance

**Michalska da Rocha, B., Rhodes, S., Vasilopoulou, E., & Hutton, P. (2018).** Loneliness in psychosis: A meta-analytical review. *Schizophrenia Bulletin, 44*(1), 114–125.

This landmark meta-analysis systematically reviewed all available studies on loneliness in psychotic disorders. Pooling data from multiple studies and several hundred participants, the authors found a statistically significant positive relationship between loneliness and psychotic symptoms across studies. The analysis demonstrated that individuals with psychosis consistently reported higher levels of loneliness compared to the general population. The authors noted that loneliness was associated with both positive and negative symptoms, depression, reduced quality of life, and lower social functioning. Importantly, the meta-analysis identified a critical gap: while the association between loneliness and psychosis was robust, the mechanisms driving this relationship — particularly cognitive mechanisms — remained poorly understood. The authors explicitly called for research investigating the cognitive and perceptual factors that may explain why patients with psychosis experience heightened loneliness, making the present study a direct response to this call.

---

**Michalska da Rocha, B., Billings, J., & Sheridan Rains, L. (2018).** Loneliness in schizophrenia: Construct clarification, measurement, and clinical relevance. *PLoS ONE, 13*(3), e0194021.

This comprehensive review clarified the construct of loneliness in schizophrenia as distinct from social isolation. The authors applied the cognitive model of loneliness (Cacioppo & Hawkley, 2009) specifically to schizophrenia and proposed that loneliness in this population is tantamount to a feeling of being unsafe in the social environment, accompanied by enhanced environmental threat perception and leading to poor physical, emotional, and cognitive functioning. The paper reviewed available measurement tools and confirmed that the UCLA Loneliness Scale is appropriate and valid for use in schizophrenia populations. The authors argued that loneliness should be assessed routinely in clinical practice and that understanding its cognitive underpinnings could inform targeted interventions. This theoretical framework directly supports the present study's hypothesis that impaired cognitive processing and mediation (as measured by the Rorschach) may underlie the subjective experience of loneliness.

---

**Green, M. F., Catalano, L. T., Engel, L., & Horan, W. P. (2023).** Social withdrawal, loneliness, and health in schizophrenia: Psychological and neural mechanisms. *Schizophrenia Bulletin, 49*(5), 1112–1126.

This recent theoretical paper synthesized evolutionary theories of loneliness with cognitive and neurobiological models specific to serious mental illness. The authors proposed a "social homeostasis" model in which loneliness serves as a biological signal of unmet social need, but in schizophrenia, this signal becomes dysregulated due to impairments in social perception, motivation, and cognition. They described how chronic loneliness heightens social threat sensitivity and alters brain networks supporting social cognition and emotion regulation in individuals with psychosis. The paper argued that impairments in the cognitive processing of social information — including perception, interpretation, and reasoning about social stimuli — represent a primary mechanism through which social disconnection becomes chronic. This directly supports the present study's use of the Rorschach Cognitive Triad (processing → mediation → ideation) as a model for understanding the cognitive architecture of loneliness.

### 3.2 Cognitive and Perceptual Correlates of Loneliness in Schizophrenia

**Thibaudeau, E., Bherer, M., Bherer, L., Bherer, F., & Bherer, C. (2023).** Loneliness is associated with mentalizing and emotion recognition abilities in schizophrenia, but only in a cluster of patients with social cognitive deficits. *Schizophrenia Research, 256*, 29–37.

This study examined the relationship between loneliness and social cognitive abilities in 87 patients with schizophrenia spectrum disorders compared to healthy controls. Using latent class analysis, the authors identified distinct patient subgroups based on social cognitive capacity. In patients who demonstrated impaired social cognition (deficits in mentalizing and emotion recognition), loneliness was significantly and negatively associated with these cognitive abilities — meaning that worse social-cognitive capacity was associated with greater loneliness. However, in patients whose social cognition performed at normative levels, this association was absent. This crucial finding suggests that cognitive-perceptual impairment is a specific mechanism driving loneliness only in a subgroup of patients, and that identifying this subgroup through cognitive assessment (such as the Rorschach) could enable targeted intervention. The present study extends this logic by using performance-based projective assessment rather than computerized social cognition tasks.

---

**Hajdúk, M., Harvey, P. D., Penn, D. L., & Pinkham, A. E. (2023).** Social cognitive bias increases loneliness both directly and by decreasing social connection in patients with schizophrenia. *Schizophrenia Research, 256*, 38–46.

Using structural equation modeling in a sample of patients with schizophrenia, this study demonstrated that Social Threat Bias — the tendency to misperceive neutral social stimuli as threatening — was directly linked to increased loneliness, and also indirectly through decreased social connection. Negative symptoms were directly linked to loneliness, while affective symptoms influenced loneliness through the mediating pathway of threat bias. The authors explicitly recommended that social threat bias should be considered when planning interventions aimed at reducing loneliness in schizophrenia. This finding is directly relevant to the present study because the Rorschach Mediation cluster (XA%, X-%, FQ-) measures precisely this construct — the tendency to misperceive and distort perceptual input. If social threat bias (measured by computerized tasks) predicts loneliness, then perceptual distortion on the Rorschach (a more ecologically valid and clinically available measure) should similarly predict loneliness.

---

**Hsu, Y. C., Huang, C. L., & Lin, C. Y. (2024).** Social isolation and cognitive function in patients with schizophrenia. *Schizophrenia Research, 266*, 171–178.

This longitudinal study collected two waves of data (2019 and 2021) from 166 inpatients with schizophrenia living in chronic psychiatric wards. The authors measured social isolation by incorporating frequency of social contact and participation, while cognitive functions were assessed using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA). Results showed that social isolation was significantly related to poor language abilities (β = -0.17, p = 0.013) and delayed recall (β = -0.15, p = 0.023). This was among the first studies to establish a longitudinal, directional link between social disconnection and cognitive decline in schizophrenia, suggesting a bidirectional relationship where cognitive impairment may contribute to isolation, and isolation may further worsen cognition. The present study adds to this literature by examining whether specific types of cognitive impairment (processing, mediation, ideation) as captured by the Rorschach are differentially associated with the subjective experience of loneliness.

---

**Stain, H. J., Galletly, C., Clark, S., et al. (2016).** Loneliness in schizophrenia and its possible correlates: An exploratory study. *Psychiatry Research, 235*, 170–176.

This study assessed loneliness using the revised UCLA Loneliness Scale in 87 non-depressed individuals with schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder and 58 healthy controls. Social cognition was measured using both self-report questionnaires and a performance-based task. Results confirmed that patients with schizophrenia reported significantly greater loneliness than controls. Loneliness was associated with impaired social cognition and was identified as a risk factor for broad-based morbidity across the adult lifespan even after controlling for multiple demographic and clinical factors. However, the specific cognitive processes (e.g., perceptual processing, reality testing, ideational quality) that drive loneliness were not examined. The present study addresses this gap by using the Rorschach Cognitive Triad to decompose cognition into its component processes and examine which specifically predicts loneliness.

---

**Jaya, E. S., Hempel, R. J., & Lincoln, T. M. (2024).** The mediating role of selective attention to threat and external attribution biases in the relationship between loneliness and psychotic-like experiences. *Schizophrenia Research, 269*, 118–125.

This study tested whether two cognitive biases — Selective Attention to Threat (ATB) and External Attribution Bias (EAB) — mediated the association between loneliness and psychotic-like experiences. Results confirmed that biased cognition played a key role in both loneliness and psychosis, with attention to threat partially mediating the loneliness-psychosis link. This is directly relevant because the Rorschach Processing cluster (Zd) measures scanning efficiency — whether a person hastily scans (underincorporative) or excessively scans (overincorporative) their environment. This scanning pattern is the Rorschach equivalent of selective attention bias, and the present study tests whether it predicts loneliness.

### 3.3 Rorschach Assessment of Cognitive Functioning in Schizophrenia

**Ilonen, T., Leinonen, K. M., Wallin, A., & Wahlberg, K. E. (2012).** Rorschach measures of cognition relate to everyday and social functioning in schizophrenia. *Psychological Assessment, 25*(1), 253–263.

This seminal study examined the role of thought disorder, psychological complexity, and interpersonal representations — as measured by the Rorschach (R-PAS) — in explaining functional and social skills capacity in 72 middle-aged and older outpatients with schizophrenia. Psychological complexity (a Rorschach cognitive variable reflecting the capacity for problem-solving and organizing surroundings) was correlated with functional capacity (r = .30) and social skills capacity (r = .34). In multiple regression models, psychological complexity accounted for significant variation in functional capacity (β = 0.23, p = 0.02) and social skills capacity (β = 0.35, p < 0.01) even after controlling for neurocognitive functioning and psychopathology. This landmark finding established that the Rorschach captures cognitive dimensions that predict real-world functioning above and beyond what standard neuropsychological tests measure. The present study extends this logic to a new outcome — subjective loneliness — and uses the full Cognitive Triad framework rather than a single composite.

---

**Singh, G., Shukla, S., & Mishra, R. (2021).** Rorschach inkblot test and psychopathology among patients suffering from schizophrenia. *Indian Journal of Psychiatric Nursing, 30*(1), 49–55.

This Indian study examined the Rorschach structural profiles of patients with schizophrenia and their correlation with psychopathology. The authors found that the Rorschach provided specific knowledge about multiple areas of personality functioning in schizophrenia patients, including coping style, emotional processing, stress management, mediation (reality testing), ideation (thought quality), self-perception, and interpersonal relationships. Rorschach variables significantly correlated with psychopathology severity as measured by the PANSS. This study established that Rorschach cognitive variables are clinically relevant in Indian schizophrenia populations. However, it did not examine the relationship between Rorschach cognitive variables and subjective experiential outcomes like loneliness. The present study fills this gap.

---

**Kimoto, S., Kishimoto, N., Yamamuro, K., et al. (2016).** Distinctive Rorschach profiles of young adults with schizophrenia and autism spectrum disorder. *Neuropsychiatric Disease and Treatment, 12*, 2403–2410.

This study compared Rorschach structural profiles between young adults with schizophrenia (SZ) and autism spectrum disorder (ASD). Results revealed significant differences in six response variables on the Rorschach Comprehensive System. Patients with schizophrenia showed significantly higher D score, Adjusted D score, ordinary developmental quality (DQo), and form quality minus (FQ-) compared to those with ASD. The findings demonstrated that individuals with SZ have stronger perception distortions (higher X-%, lower XA%) and simpler, poorer recognition than those with ASD. This study confirmed that Rorschach mediation variables (reality testing indices) are distinctively impaired in schizophrenia and differentiate it from other neurodevelopmental conditions. The present study builds on this by testing whether this distinctive perceptual distortion predicts the subjective experience of loneliness.

---

**Jo, Y. T., Lee, M. J., Lee, J. S., & Joo, Y. H. (2024).** Revisiting Rorschach test and MMPI-II patterns in Kraepelinian vs. DSM-wise schizophrenia: How they differ and what it means for diagnosis. *Science Progress, 107*(3).

This recent study compared Rorschach patterns between Kraepelinian schizophrenia (severe, deteriorating course) and DSM-defined schizophrenia (broader criterion). Patients with Kraepelinian schizophrenia showed significantly increased DV2 scores (severe thought disorder) and decreased D scores (stress tolerance) compared to DSM-wise schizophrenia. This study demonstrates that Rorschach ideation variables (special scores reflecting thought disorder severity) are sensitive to illness severity gradients within schizophrenia. The present study leverages this sensitivity by examining whether degree of thought disturbance (WSum6, Level 2 scores) correlates with degree of loneliness — testing whether patients with more severe cognitive disorganization experience greater subjective isolation.

### 3.4 Cognitive Model of Loneliness and Its Neural Basis

**Wang, X., Chen, Y., & Liu, Z. (2026).** Loneliness is associated with cognitive impairment in patients with schizophrenia: The mediating roles of sleep quality and anxiety. *European Archives of Psychiatry and Clinical Neuroscience*.

This most recent study (2026) established that loneliness is directly associated with cognitive impairment in patients with schizophrenia, with sleep quality and anxiety serving as mediating pathways. This confirms a bidirectional relationship between cognition and loneliness — cognitive impairment may cause loneliness, and loneliness may worsen cognition. The present study examines the first direction of this relationship: whether specific types of cognitive impairment (processing, mediation, ideation) predict loneliness.

---

**Regev, A., & colleagues (2024).** Neurocognitive and social cognitive correlates of social exclusion in psychotic disorders. *Schizophrenia Research, 270*, 215–223.

This study found that participants with high social exclusion were 4.24 times more likely to have cognitive impairment than those with low social exclusion. Verbal learning was the cognitive function most strongly related to social exclusion domains. The study established a dose-response relationship between degree of cognitive impairment and degree of social disconnection. The present study extends this by using a performance-based projective measure (Rorschach) to capture cognitive processes that traditional neuropsychological tests may miss.

---

**Lin, C. Y., Yen, C. F., et al. (2022).** Comparing the psychometric properties among three versions of the UCLA Loneliness Scale in individuals with schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder. *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 19*(14), 8443.

This study evaluated the validity and psychometric properties of three versions of the UCLA Loneliness Scale specifically in schizophrenia and schizoaffective disorder populations. All three versions demonstrated acceptable psychometric properties, with the UCLA-LS Version 3 showing the best overall performance. The study confirmed that self-reported loneliness can be reliably measured in individuals with psychotic disorders, addressing the common concern that psychotic patients cannot validly report subjective experiences. This supports the use of the UCLA Loneliness Scale (Version 3) in the present study.

---

**Yen, C. F., Lin, C. Y., & colleagues (2023).** Association of loneliness with suicide risk and depression in individuals with schizophrenia: Moderating effects of self-esteem and perceived support. *npj Schizophrenia, 9*, Article 40.

This cross-sectional study of 300 participants (267 with schizophrenia, 33 with schizoaffective disorder) found that loneliness was significantly associated with increased suicide risk and depression severity. Self-esteem moderated the loneliness-depression link, while perceived support from friends moderated the loneliness-suicide risk association. This study establishes loneliness as a clinical variable with life-threatening consequences in schizophrenia, justifying the urgency of identifying its cognitive predictors — which is what the present study aims to do.

This Indian cross-sectional study found that cognitive deficits may affect the functioning of a person with schizophrenia more than positive or negative symptoms. The authors highlighted a lack of literature on explanatory models of cognitive deficits in schizophrenia that can influence help-seeking behavior in India. They called for more research linking cognitive assessment to clinically meaningful outcomes. The present study responds to this call by examining whether Rorschach-derived cognitive indices predict a clinically meaningful outcome (loneliness) that is under-assessed in Indian psychiatric settings.

The findings highlighted that cognitive impairments in attention, executive function, and memory were associated with inability to maintain social connections and self-care. While the study used neuropsychological tests, it underscored the connection between cognitive impairment and social disconnection in Indian schizophrenia populations. The present study offers a complementary approach using projective assessment.

## 4. RESEARCH GAP

The review of literature reveals the following critical gaps:

1. **No study globally has examined whether Rorschach cognitive triad variables (processing, mediation, ideation) predict subjective loneliness in any psychiatric population.** The Rorschach–loneliness connection remains entirely unexplored.

2. **Existing loneliness research in schizophrenia uses computerized social cognition tasks** (TASIT, ER-40, Hinting Task) that are unavailable in most Indian clinical settings. The Rorschach, which is taught in every MPhil Clinical Psychology program in India and available universally, has never been tested as a predictor of loneliness.

3. **While Hajdúk et al. (2023) demonstrated that social threat bias predicts loneliness**, no study has examined whether the Rorschach Mediation cluster — which measures perceptual distortion (the projective equivalent of threat bias) — shows the same predictive relationship.

4. **Ilonen et al. (2012) showed Rorschach cognitive variables predict functional capacity**, but did not examine subjective experiential outcomes like loneliness. Functional outcome (can the patient work?) and subjective experience (does the patient feel lonely?) are distinct constructs requiring separate investigation.

5. **No Indian study** has examined either (a) Rorschach cognitive triad variables in relation to any outcome beyond psychopathology, or (b) loneliness and its cognitive correlates in Indian schizophrenia populations.

6. **The question of WHICH cognitive step** (processing vs. mediation vs. ideation) most strongly predicts loneliness remains completely unanswered — yet this specificity is essential for targeted intervention planning.

## 5. RATIONALE OF THE STUDY

The rationale for the present study rests on five converging lines of evidence:

**First**, loneliness in schizophrenia is no longer a "soft" construct — it is a clinical variable associated with increased psychiatric hospitalization, suicide risk, cognitive decline, and mortality (Michalska da Rocha et al., 2018; Yen et al., 2023; Green et al., 2023). Identifying its predictors is clinically urgent.

**Second**, the cognitive model of loneliness (Cacioppo & Hawkley, 2009), now applied to psychosis (Michalska da Rocha et al., 2018; Green et al., 2023), establishes that loneliness arises from cognitive-perceptual disturbances — specifically, misperception of social stimuli as threatening and impaired processing of social information. This model identifies a cognitive TARGET for intervention.

**Third**, the Rorschach Cognitive Triad (Processing → Mediation → Ideation) measures exactly the sequential cognitive operations implicated in the cognitive model of loneliness. Processing (Zd) captures how one scans the environment for social cues; Mediation (XA%, X-%) captures how accurately one perceives those cues; Ideation (WSum6, M quality) captures how logically one reasons about interpersonal situations. The Rorschach thus provides a direct performance-based window into the cognitive architecture of loneliness.

**Fourth**, projective assessment offers critical advantages over neuropsychological testing and self-report in psychotic populations: it cannot be faked, does not require cooperation for extended testing sessions, is ecologically valid (ambiguous stimuli simulate real-world social ambiguity), has no floor effects, and measures cognitive PROCESS rather than just product.

**Fifth**, if specific Rorschach cognitive triad variables can identify which cognitive step is most impaired in lonely schizophrenia patients, it directly informs individualized rehabilitation planning — for example, whether to prioritize perceptual accuracy training, scanning efficiency training, or logical reasoning training in cognitive remediation programs.

**In summary**, this study bridges projective assessment and emerging loneliness research to address a question that is theoretically grounded, clinically urgent, methodologically feasible, and genuinely novel.

## 6. METHODOLOGY

### 6.1 AIM

To examine whether Rorschach indices of the Cognitive Triad (Information Processing, Cognitive Mediation, and Ideation) predict subjective loneliness in clinically stable patients with schizophrenia.

---

### 6.2 OBJECTIVES

1. To assess the Rorschach Cognitive Triad profile (Processing, Mediation, and Ideation clusters) of patients with schizophrenia.
2. To assess the level of subjective loneliness in patients with schizophrenia using the UCLA Loneliness Scale (Version 3).
3. To examine the correlation between Rorschach Processing variables (Zd, Zf, DQ+, DQv, W:D:Dd) and subjective loneliness.
4. To examine the correlation between Rorschach Mediation variables (XA%, WDA%, X-%, X+%, Xu%, P) and subjective loneliness.
5. To examine the correlation between Rorschach Ideation variables (WSum6, Level 2 scores, Ma:Mp, a:p, M quality) and subjective loneliness.
6. To determine which cluster of the Cognitive Triad (Processing, Mediation, or Ideation) is the strongest predictor of loneliness after controlling for negative symptom severity.

---

### 6.3 HYPOTHESES

**H1:** There will be a significant negative correlation between Rorschach Processing efficiency (Zd within normal range, higher DQ+) and subjective loneliness scores — i.e., patients who process more efficiently will report less loneliness.

**H2:** There will be a significant positive correlation between Rorschach Mediation distortion (higher X-%, lower XA%) and subjective loneliness scores — i.e., patients who misperceive reality more will report greater loneliness.

**H3:** There will be a significant positive correlation between Rorschach Ideation disturbance (higher WSum6, more M-) and subjective loneliness scores — i.e., patients with more disordered thinking will report greater loneliness.

**H4:** Rorschach Cognitive Triad variables will significantly predict subjective loneliness above and beyond the contribution of negative symptoms (PANSS-N).

**H5:** The Mediation cluster (reality testing/perceptual accuracy) will be the strongest predictor of loneliness among the three cognitive triad clusters.

Cross-sectional, correlational research design.

### 6.7 TOOLS

#### Tool 1: Rorschach Inkblot Test — Exner Comprehensive System (Exner, 2003)

**Description:** The Rorschach Inkblot Test consists of 10 bilaterally symmetrical inkblot cards administered under standardized conditions as per Exner's Comprehensive System. The respondent is asked "What might this be?" for each card (Response Phase), followed by an Inquiry Phase to clarify location, determinants, and content.

**Variables extracted (Cognitive Triad):**

*Processing Cluster:*
- Zf (Processing Frequency) — motivation to organize
- Zd (Processing Efficiency) — over- vs. under-incorporative scanning
- W:D:Dd (Location Ratio) — distribution of attentional focus
- DQ+ (Synthesized Responses) — cognitive complexity
- DQv (Vague Responses) — poorly differentiated processing

*Mediation Cluster:*
- XA% (Extended Adequate Form) — overall reality testing adequacy
- WDA% (Whole-Detail Adequate Form) — conventional reality testing
- X-% (Distortion Percentage) — frequency of frank misperception
- X+% (Conventional Form) — degree of perceptual conventionality
- Xu% (Unusual Form) — idiosyncratic but non-distorted perception
- P (Popular Responses) — ability to perceive the obvious

*Ideation Cluster:*
- WSum6 (Weighted Sum of 6 Special Scores) — thought disorder composite
- Level 2 Special Scores — severe thought disturbance
- Ma:Mp (Active:Passive Human Movement) — active vs. passive ideation
- a:p (Active:Passive Ratio) — cognitive flexibility
- M quality (M+, Mo, Mu, M-) — quality of deliberate reasoning about people

**Psychometric Properties:** Inter-rater reliability for the Exner CS is well established: κ = .85–.97 for location, .80–.90 for determinants, .85+ for form quality, .82–.97 for special scores (Exner, 2003; Meyer et al., 2002). Indian norms are available (Dubey, 2011).

---

#### Tool 2: UCLA Loneliness Scale — Version 3 (Russell, 1996)

**Description:** A 20-item self-report measure assessing subjective feelings of loneliness and social isolation. Items are rated on a 4-point Likert scale (1 = Never to 4 = Always). Total scores range from 20 to 80, with higher scores indicating greater loneliness. Sample items include: "How often do you feel that you lack companionship?" and "How often do you feel isolated from others?"

**Psychometric Properties:**
- Internal consistency: Cronbach's alpha = .89–.94 (Russell, 1996)
- Test-retest reliability: ICC = .73 over 12 months
- Validated for use in schizophrenia populations (Lin et al., 2022)
- Indian psychometric properties established: strong internal reliability in Indian samples (Suri et al., 2020)
- Can be administered in Hindi translation or read aloud to participants with limited literacy

---

#### Tool 3: Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale — Negative Subscale (PANSS-N; Kay, Fiszbein, & Opler, 1987)

**Description:** The PANSS is a 30-item clinician-rated scale assessing positive symptoms (7 items), negative symptoms (7 items), and general psychopathology (16 items). For the present study, the PANSS Negative Subscale (7 items: blunted affect, emotional withdrawal, poor rapport, passive/apathetic social withdrawal, difficulty in abstract thinking, lack of spontaneity, stereotyped thinking) will serve as the covariate to control for the overlap between negative symptoms and loneliness. PANSS total will also be used to confirm clinical stability (total < 80 for inclusion).

**Psychometric Properties:** Inter-rater reliability ICC = .83–.87; internal consistency α = .73 for the negative subscale (Kay et al., 1987). Widely used in Indian schizophrenia research.

---

#### Tool 4: Sociodemographic and Clinical Data Sheet (Researcher-designed)

**Variables:** Age, sex, education, marital status, occupation, residence (urban/rural), duration of illness, number of episodes, current antipsychotic medication and dose (chlorpromazine equivalent), duration of current medication stability.

### 6.8 PROCEDURE

1. Ethical clearance obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee.
2. Patients attending Psychiatry OPD/follow-up clinic identified from the register by the treating psychiatrist.
3. Patients meeting inclusion criteria approached, study explained in vernacular language, informed consent obtained.
4. Sociodemographic and clinical data recorded.
5. PANSS administered by a trained rater (researcher or co-rater) to confirm clinical stability and to generate PANSS-N covariate score.
6. Rorschach Inkblot Test administered by the researcher in a quiet, distraction-free setting as per standard Exner CS administration and inquiry procedure.
7. UCLA Loneliness Scale (Version 3) administered — read aloud if needed, patient responds verbally or in writing.
8. Rorschach protocols scored independently by the researcher. 20% of protocols (n = 8–9) rescored by a second trained Rorschach scorer for inter-rater reliability.
9. Data entered, cleaned, and analyzed.

**Total testing time per participant:** Approximately 75–90 minutes (including breaks as needed).

### 6.9 DATA ANALYSIS

| Analysis | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Descriptive statistics (Mean, SD, frequencies) | Characterize the sample on sociodemographic, clinical, Rorschach, and loneliness variables |
| Shapiro-Wilk test | Assess normality of distribution for parametric vs. non-parametric decisions |
| Inter-rater reliability (Cohen's Kappa / ICC) | Establish scoring reliability for Rorschach variables |
| Pearson's r / Spearman's rho correlations | Examine bivariate relationships between each Rorschach cognitive variable and UCLA Loneliness scores |
| Hierarchical Multiple Regression | **Primary analysis:** Predict UCLA Loneliness scores from Rorschach Cognitive Triad variables after controlling for PANSS-N |

**Hierarchical Regression Model:**
- **Block 1 (Covariate):** PANSS Negative Subscale score
- **Block 2 (Processing):** Zd, DQ+, Lambda
- **Block 3 (Mediation):** XA%, X-%, P
- **Block 4 (Ideation):** WSum6, M quality composite

**Key output:** ΔR² at each step — which cluster adds the most predictive power for loneliness after controlling for negative symptoms?

## 7. ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

1. Ethical clearance will be obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee prior to data collection.
2. Informed written consent will be obtained from all participants. For patients with limited decisional capacity, consent from a legally authorized representative (family member/guardian) will also be obtained.
3. Participation is voluntary. Participants may withdraw at any time without impact on their ongoing treatment.
4. Confidentiality of all data will be maintained. Participants will be identified by code numbers only.
5. No invasive procedures are involved. The Rorschach and UCLA Loneliness Scale pose no physical or significant psychological risk.
6. If any participant is identified as having severe loneliness with suicidal ideation (through clinical observation during assessment), they will be immediately referred to the treating psychiatrist for appropriate intervention.
7. Results will be reported in aggregate form only. Individual results will not be disclosed without consent.
