Comprehensive Guide for Independent Learning & Viva Preparation

1 INTRODUCTION TO THEMATIC APPERCEPTION TESTS

TAT (Thematic Apperception Test)

  • Developed by: Henry A. Murray and Christiana D. Morgan (1935) at Harvard Psychological Clinic
  • Type: Projective personality assessment technique
  • Method: Storytelling about ambiguous pictures to reveal personality dynamics
  • Total cards: 31 pictures (typically 10-12 administered per session)
  • Theoretical basis: Based on the principle of APPERCEPTION — how a person perceives and interprets ambiguous stimuli reveals their inner personality, needs, conflicts, and defenses
  • Murray's Need-Press Theory: Stories reveal the subject's dominant needs (internal forces) and perceived environmental presses (external forces)

CAT-A (Children's Apperception Test — Animal Version)

  • Developed by: Leopold Bellak and Sonya Sorel Bellak (1949)
  • Type: Projective technique for children using animal figures
  • Rationale: Young children identify more readily with animals than with human figures
  • Total cards: 10 cards (ALL administered in every case)
  • Themes explored: Feeding, sibling rivalry, aggression, toilet training, primal scene, loneliness, punishment

CAT-H (Children's Apperception Test — Human Version)

  • Developed by: Leopold Bellak and Sonya Sorel Bellak (1965)
  • Same situations as CAT-A but with human figures instead of animals
  • Rationale: Some children (especially older ones, ages 7-10) relate better to human figures
  • Total cards: 10 cards
TAT ek projective personality test hai jo Henry Murray aur Christiana Morgan ne 1935 mein banaya tha. Isme subject ko ambiguous (aspashtit) tasveerein dikhayi jaati hain aur unhe kahani banana hoti hai. Yeh kahani unke personality dynamics ko reveal karti hai — unki needs, conflicts, defenses, aur relationships.

Kis vajah se kahani se personality pata chalti hai? Is vajah se ki jab hum koi ambiguous cheez dekhte hain, toh hum apne andar ke thoughts aur feelings ko uspe project karte hain. Isi ko APPERCEPTION kehte hain — matlab perception + personal interpretation.

CAT-A (1949) aur CAT-H (1965) Bellak ne bacchon ke liye banaya. CAT-A mein animals hain (kis vajah se? Is vajah se ki chhote bacche animals se zyada identify karte hain). CAT-H mein wohi situations hain par human figures ke saath (kuch clinicians ko lagta hai bade bacche human figures se behtar relate karte hain).
TAT reveals personality through STORYTELLING about ambiguous pictures. The key principle is APPERCEPTION = Perception + Personal Interpretation of stimuli.

2 AGE CRITERIA & APPLICABILITY

TAT Applicability Details:

  • Form A instructions: For adolescents and adults of average intelligence and education
  • Form B instructions: For children (10-14 years approximate), less educated adults, and psychotic patients
  • Can be used with elderly populations (no upper age limit)
  • NOT suitable for very young children below 10 years — use CAT instead

CAT-A Developmental Considerations:

  • Ages 3-5: Stories are shorter, more descriptive, less complex; child may simply describe what they see
  • Below age 6: Perceptual inaccuracies are common (child may not recognize all animal figures correctly)
  • Ages 6-10: More structured stories, internalization of morals visible, better narrative quality
  • Children identify more readily with animal figures — less threatening than human figures

CAT-H Considerations:

  • Same age range as CAT-A (3-10 years)
  • More cultural specificity than animal version (less culture-free)
  • Ambiguity of sex/age of human figures varies across cultures
  • Some research suggests older children (7-10) give richer stories with human figures
TAT: 14 saal aur upar ke logon ke liye hai (adolescents aur adults). Koi upper age limit nahin hai — elderly ke saath bhi use ho sakta hai. 14 se chhote bacchon ke liye TAT use NAHIN karna — unke liye CAT use karein.

CAT-A aur CAT-H: 3 se 10 saal ke bacchon ke liye hai. CAT-A mein animals hain — kis vajah se animals? Is vajah se ki chhote bacche (especially 3-5 saal ke) animals se zyada easily identify karte hain, unhe kam threatening lagta hai. CAT-H mein human figures hain — kuch clinicians ise prefer karte hain jab baccha 7-10 saal ka ho aur human figures se behtar relate kare.

Developmental differences: 3-5 saal ke bacche chhoti aur simple kahaniyaan dete hain, sirf describe karte hain. 6-10 saal ke bacche zyada structured kahaniyaan dete hain jisme morals aur outcomes dikhte hain.
NEVER administer TAT to children below 14 years. Use CAT-A or CAT-H for ages 3-10. NEVER administer CAT to adults — use TAT instead.

3 MATERIALS REQUIRED

TAT Materials:

  • 31 TAT picture cards (select 10-12 for administration based on subject's gender and referral question)
  • Recording forms OR blank paper for verbatim recording
  • Bellak T.A.T. Blank — Long Form (detailed analysis) or Short Form (brief analysis)
  • Pen/pencil for recording
  • Quiet, private room with comfortable seating
  • Stopwatch (optional but recommended for noting reaction times)
  • Referral information/case history reviewed beforehand

CAT-A Materials:

  • 10 CAT-A picture cards (animal figures) — ALL cards used every time
  • Recording forms for verbatim recording
  • Child-friendly, comfortable environment
  • Low table and comfortable seating appropriate for child's size
  • Toys may be available in room but PUT AWAY during testing
  • Cards kept OUT OF SIGHT until presented one at a time

CAT-H Materials:

  • 10 CAT-H picture cards (human figures) — ALL cards used
  • Same setup materials as CAT-A
  • Recording forms for verbatim recording
TAT ke liye: 31 cards mein se 10-12 select karo (gender aur referral ke hisaab se), recording forms ya blank paper, pen, ek quiet private room, comfortable chair, aur agar ho sake toh stopwatch bhi rakhein reaction time note karne ke liye.

CAT ke liye: 10 cards (saare use hote hain har baar), recording forms, child-friendly mahaul, low table, comfortable seating. Toys room mein ho sakte hain par test ke time hata dein. Cards ko chhupa ke rakhein — sirf ek-ek dikhaana hai.

5 PRE-ADMINISTRATION PREPARATION

  1. Review referral information: Understand the referral question and presenting problems
  2. Select appropriate cards: For TAT, choose cards based on gender, age, and specific areas to explore
  3. Arrange cards in correct order: Place selected cards in the sequence they will be presented
  4. Prepare the room: Quiet, private, comfortable; free from distractions and interruptions
  5. For CAT: Make environment child-friendly; put away distracting toys; keep cards hidden until use
  6. Recording materials ready: Forms, blank paper, pen all within reach
  7. Seating arranged: According to standard positioning (see Section 6)
  8. Ensure adequate time: TAT needs approximately 1 hour; CAT needs 20-45 minutes depending on child's age
Test se pehle ki tayyaari: Referral information padho, sahi cards select karo (TAT mein gender ke hisaab se), cards ko sahi order mein rakho, room quiet aur comfortable hona chahiye, recording materials ready rakhein. CAT ke liye room child-friendly banao, toys hata do, cards chhupa ke rakho. TAT ke liye lagbhag 1 ghanta chahiye, CAT ke liye 20-45 minutes (bacche ki umar pe depend karta hai).

6 SEATING ARRANGEMENT

TAT Seating (Standard):

  • Examiner seated BEHIND and to the SIDE of the subject
  • Subject CANNOT see examiner's face or reactions
  • Examiner CAN observe subject's facial expressions and gestures
  • Rationale: Prevents examiner's facial reactions from influencing subject's stories
  • Modification for disturbed/suspicious patients: May sit where patient can see you (to reduce anxiety)

CAT Seating:

  • MORE FLEXIBLE than TAT — can sit face-to-face at a low table
  • Comfortable, game-like atmosphere is priority
  • Low table and child-sized seating preferred
  • Floor seating also acceptable if child is more comfortable
  • Child should feel at ease and relaxed
TAT mein: Examiner subject ke PEECHE aur BAGAL mein baithta hai — taaki subject examiner ka chehra na dekh sake. Kis vajah se? Is vajah se ki examiner ke facial expressions subject ki stories ko influence kar sakte hain. Par agar patient bahut suspicious ya disturbed hai, toh saamne baith sakte hain taaki unki anxiety kam ho.

CAT mein: Zyada flexible hai — bacche ke saamne low table pe baith sakte hain. Mahaul comfortable aur game jaisa hona chahiye. Floor pe baithna bhi theek hai agar baccha comfortable hai.
For TAT: The behind-and-to-the-side position is IDEAL but not rigid. Clinical judgment guides modifications. For CAT: Comfort of the child is paramount.

7 TAT ADMINISTRATION — DETAILED PROCEDURE

Step-by-Step Procedure:

  1. Step 1: Rapport Building
    • Put subject at ease with brief conversation
    • Explain the general nature of the activity (storytelling)
    • Do NOT explain it as a "test of personality" or "psychological analysis"
  2. Step 2: Give Instructions

Form A Instructions (Adolescents & Adults of Average Intelligence):

"This is a test of imagination, one form of intelligence. I am going to show you some pictures, one at a time, and your task will be to make up as dramatic a story as you can for each. Tell what has led up to the event shown in the picture, describe what is happening at the moment, what the characters are feeling and thinking, and then give the outcome. Speak your thoughts as they come to your mind. Do you understand? Since you have fifty minutes for ten pictures, you can devote about five minutes to each story. Here is the first picture."

Form B Instructions (Children 10-14, Less Educated, Psychotics):

"This is a storytelling test. I have some pictures here that I am going to show you, and for each picture I want you to make up a story. Tell what has happened before and what is happening now. Say what the people are feeling and thinking and how it will come out. You can make up any kind of story you please. Do you understand? Well, then, here's the first picture. You have five minutes to make up a story. See how well you can do."

Bellak's Important Modification:

In CLINICAL settings, Bellak recommends OMITTING the phrase "This is a test of imagination, one form of intelligence" — it is inappropriate and may cause anxiety. Instead, add: "Let yourself go freely" or simply say it is a storytelling exercise.
  1. Step 3: Present cards one at a time
    • Hand card to subject with picture facing them
    • Subject holds the card themselves
    • Present in pre-determined sequence
  2. Step 4: Record responses VERBATIM
    • Write down EVERY word the subject says
    • Include pauses, hesitations, self-corrections
    • Note reaction time (time from receiving card to beginning of story)
    • Note total time per card
  3. Step 5: Note behavioral observations
    • Facial expressions, tone of voice, posture changes
    • Emotional reactions (blushing, nervousness, laughter)
    • Card handling (turning card over, holding at distance)
  4. Step 6: After FIRST story only — brief encouragement
    • May give ONE encouraging remark: "That's fine" or "Good"
    • If first story was too short: gently remind about story elements
    • Do NOT give encouragement after subsequent stories
  5. Step 7: Remain NEUTRAL for rest of test
    • Say nothing unless absolutely necessary
    • Do NOT comment on story content
    • Do NOT show approval or disapproval
    • Do NOT interpret or react to themes
  6. Step 8: If stories are too long/rambling
    • Gently remind about story structure and time limit
    • "Remember, try to include what led up to this, what's happening, feelings, and the outcome"
  7. Step 9: If stories are too brief/descriptive
    • Prompt: "What might have led up to this?"
    • "What do you think the characters are feeling?"
    • "How might it turn out?"

INQUIRY (Critical Procedure):

The inquiry is conducted AFTER ALL STORIES ARE COMPLETE — NOT after each individual card. This is one of the most common administration errors. Wait until all 10-12 stories have been told, then go back and ask questions.

Inquiry Questions Include:

  • Clarification of proper names used — "You mentioned 'Rahul' — is this anyone you know?"
  • Clarification of places and dates mentioned
  • Specific unusual or idiosyncratic content
  • Free associations may be requested for specific elements
  • Clarification of unclear story elements
  • What made them think of a particular theme
TAT Administration ke Steps (Hinglish mein):
1. Pehle rapport build karo — subject ko comfortable karo
2. Instructions do (Form A ya Form B — subject ke education level ke hisaab se)
3. Ek-ek card dikhao — subject ke haath mein do
4. SHABD-SHABD likho (verbatim) — har baat, har pause
5. Reaction time note karo (card milne se kahani shuru karne tak ka time)
6. Non-verbal behavior note karo (chehra, tone, gestures)
7. Sirf PEHLI kahani ke baad thoda encourage karo ("Bahut accha")
8. Baaki saari kahaniyaan ke liye CHUP raho — koi comment nahin
9. Agar kahani bahut lambi ho — story structure yaad dilao
10. Agar kahani bahut chhoti ho — prompt karo: "Pehle kya hua hoga? Log kya feel kar rahe hain? Kya hoga aage?"
11. SAARI kahaniyaan khatam hone ke BAAD hi inquiry karo
12. Inquiry mein poochho: names, places, dates, unusual content ke baare mein

Kis vajah se inquiry baad mein hoti hai? Is vajah se ki agar har card ke baad inquiry karein toh subject defensive ho jayega aur baaki ki kahaniyaan naturally nahin aayengi.

8 CAT ADMINISTRATION — DETAILED PROCEDURE (Both A and H)

Step-by-Step Procedure:

  1. Step 1: Build Rapport — Make it a GAME
    • The key difference from TAT: CAT should feel like a GAME, not a TEST
    • Never use the word "test" with young children
    • Use words like "game," "fun activity," "telling stories"
    • Spend adequate time warming up — play, chat, build comfort
  2. Step 2: Address Test Awareness
    • If child knows it is a "test" — acknowledge it openly
    • Explain: "This is NOT a hard test — there are no right or wrong answers"
    • Convey positive, warm attitude
  3. Step 3: Keep All Cards Hidden
    • ALL cards must be out of sight except the one being shown
    • Children will want to play with all cards if they see them
    • Present ONLY one card at a time
  4. Step 4: Present Cards in Order (1-10)
    • Always present in numbered order: Card 1 first, Card 10 last
    • No skipping unless absolutely necessary (attention span issues)

Standard CAT Instructions:

"We are going to play a game. I am going to show you some pictures and I want you to tell me a story about each one. Tell me what is going on, what the animals (or people, for CAT-H) are doing now."

At suitable points during the story, ask:
"What happened before?" and "What will happen later?"
CAT Instructions (Hinglish mein):
"Hum ek khel khelne waale hain. Main aapko kuch tasveerein dikhaunga aur aapko har tasveir ke baare mein ek kahani sunani hai. Batao ki kya ho raha hai, jaanwar (ya log) abhi kya kar rahe hain."

Beech mein poochho: "Isse pehle kya hua tha?" aur "Iske baad kya hoga?"
  1. Step 5: Encourage But Do NOT Suggest
    • Much encouragement and prompting may be needed with children
    • Say: "That's good! Tell me more!" or "And then what happened?"
    • NEVER suggest themes, characters, or outcomes
    • NEVER say: "Is the bear angry?" or "Is the bunny scared?" (that is leading)
  2. Step 6: Record EVERYTHING Verbatim
    • Every word the child says
    • ALL side remarks (even seemingly irrelevant ones)
    • Physical activities during storytelling
    • Gestures, facial expressions, tone changes
    • Restlessness, fidgeting, avoidance
  3. Step 7: Note Non-Verbal Behavior Carefully
    • Children communicate MORE through behavior than adults
    • Card handling: holding close, pushing away, turning over
    • Emotional reactions: excitement, fear, withdrawal
    • Attention span: when child gets distracted or bored

INQUIRY for CAT:

  • Conducted AFTER ALL 10 stories have been told
  • Go over each story asking for elaboration
  • "You said the bear's name was Raju — why that name?"
  • Ask about proper names of places, ages of characters
  • Questions about the type of outcome (happy/sad ending)
  • If child's attention span doesn't permit full inquiry later: Do inquiry as soon after the last card as possible, or in a brief follow-up session

Cain's Dream Technique (Supplementary — Cards 5, 6, 9):

For Cards 5, 6, and 9 (where characters are often seen as sleeping), you can use Cain's Dream Technique: Ask "What did the bear/rabbit dream about?" Dreams children report in this context often contain MORE unconscious material than the stories themselves. This is a supplementary technique, not mandatory.
CAT Administration Steps (Hinglish mein):
1. Sabse pehle RAPPORT banao — bacche ko comfortable karo. Ye ek KHEL hai, TEST nahin.
2. "Test" shabd use MAT karo — "game" ya "khel" bolo
3. Saare cards chhupa ke rakho — sirf ek-ek dikhao
4. Order mein dikhao: Card 1 se Card 10 tak
5. Instructions do: "Hum ek khel khelenge — main tasveer dikhaunga, tum kahani sunao. Jaanwar kya kar rahe hain? Pehle kya hua? Baad mein kya hoga?"
6. Bahut encourage karo par SUGGESTIVE mat ho (ye mat poochho "kya bear gussa hai?" — ye leading hai)
7. SHABD-SHABD likho — har cheez including side remarks
8. Non-verbal behavior note karo (gestures, expressions, restlessness)
9. SAARI 10 kahaniyaan ke BAAD inquiry karo
10. Inquiry mein poochho: "Ye naam kis vajah se rakha? Ye jagah kaunsi hai? Kitne saal ke hain?"

Cain's Dream Technique: Cards 5, 6, 9 mein (jahan characters soya hua lag sakte hain), poochh sakte hain: "Bear/Rabbit ne kya sapna dekha?" Ye dreams zyada unconscious material dete hain. Ye optional supplementary technique hai.

9 SPECIAL SITUATIONS & MODIFICATIONS

If Child Wants Examiner to Tell a Story:

  • This is a common request — indicates desire to be GIVEN rather than to GIVE
  • Clinically meaningful: may indicate dependency needs or insecurity
  • Explain warmly: "I really want to hear YOUR story — you make such good ones!"
  • May promise to tell a story AFTER the test is done
  • If child refuses entirely: postpone testing, build more rapport first

If Child is Restless or Has Short Attention Span:

  • Can restrict test to cards MOST RELEVANT to the child's presenting issues
  • Example: Sibling rivalry problems → focus on Cards 1 and 4
  • Example: Aggression issues → focus on Cards 3 and 7
  • Example: Fear/anxiety → focus on Cards 5, 6, 9
  • Minimum: try to get at least 5-6 stories

Self-Administration of TAT (Adults Only):

  • Give selected cards with WRITTEN instructions
  • Subject writes their stories (approximately 300 words each)
  • Advantages: Time-saving; useful for group research; can be mailed
  • Disadvantages: Loss of spontaneity; no control over length; cannot intervene; miss behavioral observations; miss inquiry opportunity in real-time

Group Administration of TAT:

  • Pictures projected on screen or displayed on large board
  • Written responses collected from all participants
  • Same instructions as individual administration
  • Limitations: Less rich than individual; no behavioral observation; no individual prompting; loss of interaction data
Special Situations:
1. Agar baccha kahe "Aap sunao kahani": Ye dependency need dikhata hai. Pyaar se samjhao ki humein UNKI kahani sunni hai. Promise karo ki baad mein tum sunao ge. Agar bilkul mana kare toh test postpone karo.

2. Agar baccha restless ho: Saare 10 cards zaroori nahin — sirf relevant cards do. Jaise sibling rivalry ka issue hai toh Card 1 aur 4 pe focus karo. Kam se kam 5-6 stories lene ki koshish karo.

3. Self-Administration (sirf adults): Written instructions ke saath cards de do, subject khud likh ke stories dega. Time bachta hai par spontaneity jaati hai, behavioral observations miss hote hain.

4. Group Administration: Screen pe picture dikhao, sab log likh ke stories dein. Individual administration jitna rich nahin hota.

10 RECORDING PROCEDURES

What to Record:

ElementDescriptionImportance
Verbatim ResponseEvery single word spoken by subject/childEssential — basis of all analysis
Reaction TimeTime from receiving card to beginning of story (in seconds)Long RT may indicate conflict/anxiety about card content
Total Time per CardHow long the entire story tookVery short = avoidance; Very long = over-involvement
Behavioral ObservationsTone of voice, affect, pauses, sighs, laughterReveals emotional reactions to themes
Card RejectionsIf subject refuses to tell a story for a cardHighly significant — indicates strong conflict
Sequence DisruptionsIf subject returns to previous card or requests to skipMay indicate unresolved conflict
Physical Activity (CAT)Fidgeting, posture, card handling, eye contactChildren communicate much through behavior
Side RemarksComments not part of story ("This is boring", "What a weird picture")Reveals attitudes and defenses

Recording Forms:

  • Bellak Long Form: Detailed analysis sheet with sections for: main theme, main hero, main needs, main presses, conception of environment, significant conflicts, nature of anxieties, main defenses, severity of superego, integration of ego
  • Bellak Short Form: Brief form for quick analysis — key themes and patterns only
  • For initial recording: Use blank paper and write verbatim; transfer to analysis forms later
Recording (Likhne) ke Rules:
1. SHABD-SHABD likho — har ek word jo subject/baccha bole
2. Reaction Time note karo — card milne se kahani shuru karne mein kitna time laga (seconds mein). Agar bahut zyada time lage toh iska matlab hai ki us card ke content se anxiety ya conflict hai
3. Total Time per Card — puri kahani mein kitna time laga. Bahut kam = avoidance; Bahut zyada = over-involvement
4. Behavioral Observations — tone, expressions, pauses, laughter, nervousness
5. Card Rejections — agar kisi card pe kahani na bataaye toh bahut important hai (strong conflict ka sign)
6. Side Remarks — kahani ke alaawa jo bhi bole ("Ye boring hai", "Ajeeb tasveer hai") sab likho
7. CAT mein: Bacche ki physical activity bhi note karo (fidgeting, card ko kaise pakda, eye contact)

Kis vajah se verbatim likhna zaroori hai? Is vajah se ki analysis ke time har word important hota hai — agar kuch miss ho gaya toh interpretation galat ho sakti hai.

11 WHAT A GOOD STORY SHOULD CONTAIN

The examiner should aim to elicit stories that contain ALL FOUR of these elements:

#ElementQuestion to PromptWhat It Reveals
1PAST/ANTECEDENT — What led up to this event"What happened before this?"Perceived causes, history, background attributions
2PRESENT — What is happening now"What is going on right now?"Current perception of situations, conflicts
3FEELINGS/THOUGHTS — What characters feel and think"What are they feeling and thinking?"Emotional life, empathy, psychological mindedness
4OUTCOME/FUTURE — How it will turn out"How does it end? What happens next?"Expectations, optimism/pessimism, problem-solving

When Elements Are Missing:

  • If subject only DESCRIBES the picture: Prompt gently — "Can you make up a story about what might be happening?"
  • If no outcome given: Ask "How do you think it might end?"
  • If feelings are avoided: Ask "What might the person be feeling or thinking?"
  • If past is missing: Ask "What might have happened before this moment?"
  • For children (CAT): More prompting is acceptable, but never leading or suggestive
Ek achi kahani mein 4 cheezein honi chahiye:
1. PAST: Pehle kya hua (antecedent)
2. PRESENT: Abhi kya ho raha hai
3. FEELINGS/THOUGHTS: Characters kya soch rahe hain aur kya feel kar rahe hain
4. OUTCOME: Kya hoga aage / kahani kaise khatam hogi

Agar koi element missing hai toh gently prompt karo. Par KABHI bhi suggestive mat ho — khud se koi theme ya feeling suggest mat karo. Bacchon ko zyada prompting ki zaroorat hoti hai, par leading questions avoid karo.
A complete story = PAST + PRESENT + FEELINGS + OUTCOME. If any element is missing, prompt gently but NEVER lead or suggest specific content.

Common Galtiyan (Errors) jo avoid karni hain:
1. Verbatim na likhna — har shabd likhna ZAROORI hai
2. Har card ke baad inquiry karna — ye GALAT hai; SAARI kahaniyaan ke baad karo
3. Leading questions dena — kabhi suggest mat karo ki kahani mein kya hona chahiye
4. Rapport na banana — pehle subject/bacche ko comfortable karo
5. Reactions dikhana — chaunk jaana, has dena, brow raise karna — ye sab mat karo
6. 4 elements ke liye prompt na karna — agar past/feelings/outcome missing hai toh poochho
7. Non-verbal behavior note na karna — har expression, pause, gesture note karo
8. Galat cards dena — gender aur age ke hisaab se sahi cards check karo
9. Bacche ko rush karna — patience rakho, unka time do
10. Saare cards ek saath dikhana — ek-ek karo; baaki chhupao

Kis vajah se ye errors itni serious hain? Is vajah se ki ye test projective hai — spontaneous aur natural responses chahiye. Agar administration galat ho toh data contaminate ho jata hai aur interpretation meaningless ho jaati hai.

13 COMPLETE CAUTIONS — DO'S AND DON'TS

✅ DO's (Correct Practices):

  1. DO record verbatim — every single word, pause, and self-correction
  2. DO establish rapport thoroughly before starting
  3. DO present cards in correct predetermined order
  4. DO give ONE brief encouraging remark after the FIRST story only
  5. DO allow subject to develop story at their own pace
  6. DO note ALL behavioral observations (tone, gestures, expressions, pauses)
  7. DO conduct inquiry AFTER all stories are completed
  8. DO make it game-like and fun for children (CAT)
  9. DO keep other cards completely out of sight
  10. DO use age-appropriate and education-appropriate instructions
  11. DO maintain a neutral, warm, accepting demeanor throughout
  12. DO prompt gently for missing story elements (past, feelings, outcome)
  13. DO note reaction time and total time per card
  14. DO record card rejections as clinically significant
  15. DO sit behind/beside subject for TAT (standard position)
  16. DO ensure privacy and freedom from interruptions
  17. DO review referral information before selecting cards

❌ DON'Ts (Errors to Avoid):

  1. DO NOT conduct inquiry after each card — wait until ALL stories are done
  2. DO NOT lead the story by suggesting themes, emotions, or outcomes
  3. DO NOT suggest specific themes or interpretations during testing
  4. DO NOT show emotional reactions (surprise, shock, amusement, concern) to story content
  5. DO NOT rush the subject or child — allow natural pacing
  6. DO NOT tell the subject what the cards "mean" or what they "should" see
  7. DO NOT let the child see all cards at once (show ONE at a time)
  8. DO NOT use "test" language with young children — use "game" or "khel"
  9. DO NOT give more than one brief encouragement (only after first story)
  10. DO NOT interrupt a flowing story — let it complete naturally
  11. DO NOT administer TAT to children under 14 years — use CAT instead
  12. DO NOT administer CAT to adults or adolescents — use TAT instead
  13. DO NOT interpret stories aloud or discuss meaning with subject during testing
  14. DO NOT allow distractions or interruptions during administration
  15. DO NOT force a story if subject becomes very distressed — offer to move on
  16. DO NOT paraphrase or summarize what subject says — write their exact words
  17. DO NOT use leading prompts like "Is the person sad?" or "Is the bear angry?"
Kya KARNA hai (DO's):
✅ Verbatim likho, rapport banao, sahi order mein cards do, pehli kahani ke baad hi encourage karo, subject ka pace allow karo, behavior note karo, saari kahaniyaan ke BAAD inquiry karo, bacchon ke liye game jaisa banao, baaki cards chhupao, age-appropriate instructions do, neutral raho, gently prompt karo, timing note karo.

Kya NAHIN karna hai (DON'Ts):
❌ Har card ke baad inquiry mat karo, story lead mat karo, themes suggest mat karo, reactions mat dikhao, rush mat karo, cards ka "meaning" mat batao, saare cards ek saath mat dikhao, bacchon se "test" mat bolo, zyada encourage mat karo, story interrupt mat karo, 14 se kam umar ke liye TAT mat do, adults ko CAT mat do, stories ka meaning discuss mat karo testing ke dauran.

Kis vajah se ye sab itna important hai? Is vajah se ki projective tests ki validity aur reliability administration ki quality pe depend karti hai. Agar administration standard se hata toh results meaningless ho jaate hain.
The single MOST COMMON ERROR in TAT/CAT administration is conducting inquiry after each card instead of waiting until all stories are complete. This makes subjects defensive and contaminates subsequent stories.

TAT vs CAT-A vs CAT-H — Key Differences (Hinglish mein):

TAT: Murray ne banaya (1935), 14+ saal ke liye, 31 cards mein se 10-12 use karte hain, human figures hain, formal instructions, examiner peeche baithta hai, approximately 1 ghanta lagta hai.

CAT-A: Bellak ne banaya (1949), 3-10 saal ke liye, 10 cards saare use hote hain, ANIMAL figures hain (kis vajah se animals? is vajah se ki bacche animals se zyada identify karte hain aur kam threatened feel karte hain), game jaisa mahaul, saamne baith sakte hain, 20-45 minutes, relatively culture-free hai.

CAT-H: Bellak ne banaya (1965), 3-10 saal ke liye, 10 cards saare use hote hain, HUMAN figures hain (same situations as CAT-A par insaan ke saath), kuch clinicians ise prefer karte hain jab baccha bada ho (7-10 saal) ya animals se identify na kare, zyada culture-specific hai.

Common in ALL THREE: Inquiry SAARI kahaniyaan ke baad hoti hai, verbatim recording zaroori hai, projective technique hai, personality dynamics reveal karti hai.

15 HINGLISH QUICK REFERENCE SUMMARY — COMPLETE STEPS

📋 TAT Administration Quick Steps (Hinglish):

STEP 1: 10-12 cards select karein (gender/age ke hisaab se — BM = Boy/Male, GF = Girl/Female, MF = Male-Female)

STEP 2: Subject ke PEECHE aur BAGAL mein baithein (standard position — kis vajah se? is vajah se ki aapke facial expressions subject ko influence na karein)

STEP 3: Instructions dein — Form A (educated adults): "Ye ek imagination test hai... tasveer dekhiye aur ek dramatic kahani banayiye..." Form B (less educated/children): "Ye ek storytelling test hai... kahani banao..."

STEP 4: Bellak ki modification: Clinical setting mein "test of intelligence" wala part HATA do. Simply bolo: "Khul ke sochiye" (Let yourself go freely)

STEP 5: Ek-ek card dikhayein — subject ke haath mein dein

STEP 6: Kahein: "Ek kahani banayein — (1) kya hua PEHLE, (2) kya ho raha hai ABHI, (3) log kya SOCH rahe hain aur FEEL kar rahe hain, aur (4) kya hoga AAGE"

STEP 7: SHABD-SHABD likhein (verbatim) — har baat, har pause, har hesitation

STEP 8: Reaction Time note karein (card milne se kahani shuru karne tak ka time — seconds mein)

STEP 9: Behavioral observations note karein — tone, expressions, gestures, card handling

STEP 10: Sirf PEHLI kahani ke baad thoda encourage karein ("Bahut accha" ya "That's fine")

STEP 11: Baaki saari kahaniyaan ke liye CHUP rahein — koi comment NAHIN, koi reaction NAHIN

STEP 12: Agar kahani bahut chhoti ho — gently prompt karein: "Pehle kya hua hoga? Kya feel kar rahe hain? Kya hoga aage?"

STEP 13: Agar kahani bahut lambi ho — gently yaad dilayein story structure aur time limit ke baare mein

STEP 14: SAARI kahaniyaan khatam hone ke BAAD inquiry karein (KABHI bhi beech mein nahin)

STEP 15: Inquiry mein poochhein: "Ye naam kisne inspire kiya? Ye jagah kaunsi hai? Ye date/time kab ki hai? Is cheez ke baare mein aur kya aata hai mann mein?"

📋 CAT Administration Quick Steps (Hinglish):

STEP 1: 10 cards tayaar rakhein (order mein 1-10) — saare use hone hain

STEP 2: Comfortable, game-like mahaul banayein — "test" shabd use MAT karein, "khel" ya "game" bolein

STEP 3: Rapport banayein — thodi der bacche se baat karein, comfortable karein, playful rahein

STEP 4: Saare cards CHHUPA ke rakhein — sirf ek-ek dikhaana hai (kis vajah se? is vajah se ki bacche saare cards dekhenge toh khelne lagenge aur focus khatam ho jayega)

STEP 5: Instructions dein: "Hum ek khel khelenge — main tasveerein dikhaunga, tum kahani sunao. Batao ki jaanwar (ya log) kya kar rahe hain abhi."

STEP 6: Beech mein suitable points pe poochhein: "Isse pehle kya hua tha?" aur "Iske baad kya hoga?"

STEP 7: Bahut ENCOURAGE karein (par SUGGESTIVE mat ho!) — "Bahut accha! Aur kya hua? Phir kya hua?"

STEP 8: KABHI bhi leading questions mat poochhein — "Kya bear gussa hai?" ya "Kya bunny dar gaya?" — ye GALAT hai

STEP 9: SHABD-SHABD likhein — kahani + side remarks + physical activity sab kuch

STEP 10: Non-verbal behavior note karein — gestures, expressions, card handling, restlessness, eye contact

STEP 11: SAARI 10 kahaniyaan khatam hone ke BAAD inquiry karein

STEP 12: Inquiry mein poochhein: "Ye naam kis vajah se rakha? Ye jagah kaunsi hai? Kitne saal ke hain? Happy ending hai ya sad?"

STEP 13: Optional — Cain's Dream Technique: Cards 5, 6, 9 ke liye — "Bear/Rabbit ne kya sapna dekha?" (Ye zyada unconscious material de sakta hai)

STEP 14: Agar baccha restless ho jaaye — sirf relevant cards pe focus karein (minimum 5-6 stories lein)

STEP 15: Agar baccha bole "Aap sunao" — pyaar se samjhao ki humein UNKI kahani chahiye. Baad mein story sunane ka promise karo.
GOLDEN RULES (Sone ke Niyam) for Both TAT and CAT:
1. VERBATIM recording is NON-NEGOTIABLE — shabd-shabd likhna hai
2. Inquiry ALWAYS after ALL stories — beech mein KABHI nahin
3. NEUTRAL demeanor — koi reaction nahin dikhana
4. Never LEAD — kabhi suggest mat karo
5. For CAT: It's a GAME not a TEST — ye khel hai, test nahin
