## SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION TO IPDE

### 1.1 What is the IPDE?

The **International Personality Disorder Examination (IPDE)** is a semi-structured clinical interview developed to assess personality disorders according to the criteria of the **International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10)** and the **Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fourth Edition (DSM-IV)**. It is the first instrument of its kind designed specifically for reliable cross-cultural assessment of personality disorders worldwide.

**Full Form:** International Personality Disorder Examination

### 1.2 Authors and Development History

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Primary Author** | Dr. Armand W. Loranger (Cornell University Medical College, New York) |
| **Co-Editors** | Dr. Aleksandar Janca (WHO), Dr. Norman Sartorius (University of Geneva) |
| **Developed Under** | WHO/NIH (US National Institutes of Health) Joint Project on Diagnosis and Classification of Mental Disorders |
| **Predecessor** | Personality Disorder Examination (PDE; Loranger, 1988) |
| **Development Period** | 1985-1991 (workshops, revisions, field trial) |
| **Published** | 1997 (Cambridge University Press) |
| **Field Trial** | 1988-1990, at 14 centres in 11 countries across North America, Europe, Africa, and Asia |

#### Development Process:
1. Beginning in 1985, several international workshops were convened
2. Representatives from the international psychiatric community discussed format, wording, and scoring
3. Translations were undertaken and frequent revisions made based on interviewer experience
4. A major international field trial was conducted (1988-1990) at 14 clinical facilities
5. In August 1991, principal investigators met at WHO headquarters in Geneva to finalize revisions
6. Additional modifications were made for the DSM-III-R to DSM-IV transition


### 1.3 Purpose and Scope

The IPDE serves the following purposes:

- **Standardized diagnosis** of personality disorders according to ICD-10 and DSM-IV criteria
- **Cross-cultural assessment** of personality pathology in diverse populations
- **Research applications** requiring reliable, replicable case identification
- **Clinical assessment** providing both categorical diagnoses and dimensional scores
- **Training tool** for clinicians learning personality disorder assessment

The IPDE is **NOT** designed to:
- Survey the entire realm of normal personality
- Assess neutral, positive, or adaptive traits
- Diagnose other mental disorders (Axis I conditions)
- Replace comprehensive psychiatric evaluation

## SECTION 2: STRUCTURE OF THE TEST

### 2.1 Overview of Components

The IPDE consists of four integrated components:

```
IPDE STRUCTURE
|
|--- 1. SCREENING QUESTIONNAIRE (Self-administered)
|       |--- ICD-10 Version: 59 items
|       |--- DSM-IV Version: 77 items
|
|--- 2. SEMI-STRUCTURED CLINICAL INTERVIEW
|       |--- 67 items (ICD-10 Module)
|       |--- 99 items (DSM-IV Module)
|       |--- Organized into 6 domains
|
|--- 3. ANSWER SHEET
|       |--- Subject scores
|       |--- Informant scores (optional)
|
|--- 4. SCORING MATERIALS
        |--- Handscoring algorithms
        |--- Summary scoresheet
        |--- Computer scoring option
```

### 2.2 Screening Questionnaire

#### ICD-10 Screening Questionnaire:
- **Number of Items:** 59 True/False statements
- **Purpose:** To identify subjects who are unlikely to have a personality disorder, thereby reducing the number requiring the full interview
- **Format:** Self-administered paper-and-pencil questionnaire
- **Response Options:** TRUE (T) or FALSE (F)
- **Time to Complete:** Approximately 10-15 minutes

**Instructions to Subject:**
> "The purpose of this questionnaire is to learn what type of person you have been during the past five years. Please do not skip any items. If you are not sure of an answer, select the one - TRUE or FALSE - which is more likely to be correct. There is no time limit, but do not spend too much time thinking about the answer to any single statement."

#### Screening Questionnaire Scoring:
- Items are allocated to specific personality disorders
- Each disorder has a designated set of screening items
- **Threshold:** If 3 or more items from a disorder are endorsed in the keyed direction, the subject has "failed" the screen for that disorder and should be interviewed
- Clinicians may adopt lower (more sensitive) or higher (more specific) screening standards depending on the sample characteristics

**Critical Note:** The screening questionnaire should NEVER be used to:
- Make a psychiatric diagnosis
- Calculate dimensional scores
- Replace the semi-structured interview


### 2.3 Semi-Structured Clinical Interview

The interview is organized into **six thematic domains** that provide a natural clinical flow:

#### Domain 1: WORK
- Occupational functioning and productivity
- Perfectionism and task completion
- Preoccupation with details, rules, organization
- Avoidance of occupational activities involving interpersonal contact
- Items assessed: Anankastic criteria (productivity, perfectionism, details), Anxious criteria (occupational avoidance)

#### Domain 2: SELF
- Self-image and identity
- Goals and aims in life
- Internal preferences (values, morality, sexual orientation)
- Decision-making capacity
- Doubt and caution
- Sense of autonomy vs. dependence
- Items assessed: Emotionally Unstable-Borderline (identity disturbance, aims, preferences), Dependent (decision-making, autonomy), Anankastic (doubt, conscientiousness)

#### Domain 3: INTERPERSONAL RELATIONSHIPS
- Quality and stability of relationships
- Trust and suspiciousness
- Social avoidance and anxiety
- Attachment patterns
- Assertiveness and compliance
- Manipulation and exploitation of others
- Items assessed: Paranoid (suspiciousness, grudges, sensitivity), Anxious (social avoidance, fear of rejection), Dependent (subordination, compliance), Dissocial (relationship instability, callousness), Histrionic (attention-seeking, suggestibility), Schizoid (preference for solitary activities, lack of close friends)

#### Domain 4: AFFECTS
- Emotional range and expression
- Mood stability
- Anxiety and tension
- Emptiness and abandonment fears
- Anger and irritability
- Items assessed: Emotionally Unstable-Impulsive (mood instability, anger outbursts), Emotionally Unstable-Borderline (chronic emptiness, abandonment fears), Anxious (persistent tension, apprehension), Histrionic (shallow/labile affect), Schizoid (emotional coldness, flattened affect)

#### Domain 5: REALITY TESTING
- Conspiratorial thinking
- Suspicions regarding sexual fidelity
- Unusual beliefs or perceptual experiences
- Items assessed: Paranoid (conspiratorial preoccupation, sexual jealousy)

#### Domain 6: IMPULSE CONTROL
- Impulsive action without consideration of consequences
- Self-harm and suicidal behaviour
- Aggression and violence
- Irresponsibility and disregard for social norms
- Capacity for guilt and remorse
- Ability to profit from experience
- Items assessed: Emotionally Unstable-Impulsive (unexpected actions), Emotionally Unstable-Borderline (self-harm), Dissocial (irresponsibility, aggression, lack of guilt, rationalization), Anankastic (pedantry - rated at interview conclusion), Schizoid (social insensitivity - rated at interview conclusion)


### 2.4 ICD-10 vs. DSM-IV Module Differences

| Feature | ICD-10 Module | DSM-IV Module |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| **Number of Interview Items** | 67 | 99 |
| **Screening Questionnaire Items** | 59 | 77 |
| **Personality Disorders Assessed** | 9 specific + 1 unspecified | 12 specific + NOS |
| **Nomenclature Differences** | Anankastic, Anxious, Dissocial, Emotionally Unstable | Obsessive-Compulsive, Avoidant, Antisocial, Borderline |
| **Unique to ICD-10** | Impulsive subtype of Emotionally Unstable | - |
| **Unique to DSM-IV** | - | Schizotypal, Narcissistic, Passive-Aggressive |
| **Borderline Placement** | Subtype of Emotionally Unstable | Separate category |
| **Schizotypal Placement** | With schizophrenia spectrum (not assessed) | Full personality disorder |

### 2.5 Interview Format Features

The IPDE interview incorporates several structural features designed to optimize assessment quality:

1. **Open-ended introductory questions** at the beginning of each section to establish rapport and set context
2. **Specific criterion-targeted questions** with standardized wording
3. **Follow-up probes** to elicit examples and anecdotes
4. **Scoring guidelines** printed directly beneath each question in the interview schedule
5. **Criterion identification** above each question (disorder name and criterion number)
6. **"Partial" designation** for items that assess only part of a multi-faceted criterion
7. **Dual scoring columns** (Subject and Informant) on the answer sheet

## SECTION 3: ADMINISTRATION PROCEDURE (STEP-BY-STEP)

### 3.1 Prerequisites Before Administration

Before administering the IPDE, the examiner should:

1. **Obtain psychiatric history** - Ideally through a prior clinical interview (e.g., SCAN, SCID-I) or medical records
2. **Assess current mental state** - Rule out severe depression, psychosis, or cognitive impairment that would invalidate the assessment
3. **Gather background information** - Age, marital status, education, occupation, treatment history
4. **Avoid detailed personality inquiries** - Do NOT extensively discuss personality traits with the subject beforehand
5. **Prepare materials** - Interview schedule, answer sheet, screening questionnaire (if used), scoring materials

### 3.2 Step 1: Rapport Building

**Procedure:**
- Introduce yourself and explain the purpose of the assessment
- Provide a comfortable, private setting
- Establish a collaborative, non-judgmental tone
- Explain that the interview concerns long-standing patterns of behaviour, not just recent experiences
- Clarify that there are no "right" or "wrong" answers
- Inform the subject about approximate duration (60-90 minutes)
- Address any concerns or questions

**Clinical Tips for Rapport:**
- Adopt a conversational, unhurried tone
- Show genuine interest in the subject's experiences
- Avoid appearing judgmental about socially undesirable behaviours
- Maintain appropriate eye contact and open body language
- Remember that personality-disordered individuals may be especially sensitive to perceived criticism

### 3.3 Step 2: Instructions to Client

The standardized opening instruction is:

> *"The questions I am going to ask concern what you are like most of the time. I'm interested in what has been typical of you throughout your life, and not just recently. If you have changed and your answers might have been different at some time in the past, be sure to let me know."*

**Key points to emphasize:**
- Focus on long-standing patterns (at least 5 years)
- Not about current symptoms or recent changes
- Encourage honesty and openness
- Reassure about confidentiality


### 3.4 Step 3: Administering the Screening Questionnaire (Optional)

**When to Use:**
- When time constraints prevent full interview administration for all subjects
- In research settings with large samples where many subjects may not have personality disorders
- As a first-stage screening tool in epidemiological studies

**Procedure:**
1. Provide the self-administered questionnaire to the subject
2. Read the printed instructions aloud if necessary
3. Ensure the subject understands the True/False format
4. Allow approximately 10-15 minutes for completion
5. Remain available to clarify any items if asked
6. Collect the completed questionnaire

### 3.5 Step 4: Scoring the Screener

**Scoring Procedure:**
1. Circle item numbers NOT followed by "F" if they were answered TRUE
2. Circle remaining item numbers (those followed by "F") if they were answered FALSE
3. Count the number of circled items for each disorder
4. **Decision Rule:** If 3 or more items from a disorder are circled, the subject has "failed" the screen for that disorder

**Scoring Summary Sheet Organization by Disorder:**

| Disorder | Items Assessed |
|----------|---------------|
| F60.0 Paranoid | Specific allocated items |
| F60.1 Schizoid | Specific allocated items |
| F60.2 Dissocial | Specific allocated items |
| F60.30 Impulsive | Specific allocated items |
| F60.31 Borderline | Specific allocated items |
| F60.4 Histrionic | Specific allocated items |
| F60.5 Anankastic | Specific allocated items |
| F60.6 Anxious | Specific allocated items |
| F60.7 Dependent | Specific allocated items |

### 3.6 Step 5: Deciding Whether to Proceed to Interview

**Decision Matrix:**

| Screening Result | Action |
|-----------------|--------|
| Failed screen for one or more disorders | Administer full interview (or relevant sections) |
| Passed all screens | Generally no interview needed; clinical judgment may override |
| No screening used | Administer full interview to all subjects |

**Important Considerations:**
- The screener produces many **false positives** but few **false negatives**
- Clinical judgment may override screening results in either direction
- In clinical settings where personality disorder is suspected, the full interview is always preferred
- Never rely solely on the screener for diagnosis


### 3.7 Step 6: Conducting the Semi-Structured Interview

#### General Procedure:

1. **Begin with Background Information** (if not already known):
   - Age, marital status, children
   - Parents' status (living/deceased)
   - Siblings
   - Living arrangements
   - Educational history
   - Occupational history
   - Reason for current treatment
   - Previous psychiatric history

2. **Deliver the standardized opening instruction** (Section 3.3)

3. **Proceed through the six domains sequentially:**
   - I. Work
   - II. Self
   - III. Interpersonal Relationships
   - IV. Affects
   - V. Reality Testing
   - VI. Impulse Control

4. **For each domain:**
   - Begin with open-ended introductory questions
   - Follow with specific criterion-targeted questions
   - Probe for examples and documentation
   - Score each item before moving to the next (or immediately after)

5. **At the conclusion of the interview:**
   - Rate the observational criteria (items 65-67: pedantry, social insensitivity, emotional coldness) based on behaviour during the entire interview

#### Interview Flow Example:

```
Domain I: WORK
  -> Open-ended: "How well do you usually function in your work?"
  -> "What annoyances or problems keep occurring?"
  -> Item 1: Undue preoccupation with productivity
  -> Item 2: Perfectionism
  -> Item 3: Preoccupation with details
  -> Item 4: Avoidance of occupational activities

Domain II: SELF
  -> Open-ended: "How would you describe your personality?"
  -> "Have you always been like that?"
  -> Item 5: Disturbances in self-image
  -> Item 6: Uncertainty about aims
  -> Item 7: Uncertainty about internal preferences
  ...and so on
```

### 3.8 Step 7: Use of Probes and Follow-Up Questions

**Purpose of Probing:**
- To obtain specific behavioural examples
- To establish frequency, duration, and severity
- To distinguish personality traits from episodic illness
- To document the criterion has been met

**Types of Probes Used:**

| Probe Type | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| **Clarification** | "Tell me more about that." |
| **Example-seeking** | "Give me some examples." |
| **Frequency** | "How often does that happen?" |
| **Duration** | "How long has that been going on?" |
| **Impact** | "How does that affect your life?" |
| **Onset** | "When did you first notice that about yourself?" |
| **Verification** | "Have others told you that you're like that?" |
| **Confrontation** | "Why do you think they've said that?" |

**Rules for Probing:**
- Always require behavioural examples for a positive score
- Mere endorsement without documentation is insufficient
- Probe further when responses are vague or contradictory
- Do not lead the subject toward a particular answer
- When subjects deny a trait, selected follow-up with "Have people told you that you're like that?" is appropriate


### 3.9 Step 8: Handling Resistance and Vague Answers

**Common Challenges and Solutions:**

| Challenge | Strategy |
|-----------|----------|
| Subject denies all traits | Use "Have people told you..." follow-up; consider informant data |
| Vague or general responses | Request specific examples: "Can you give me a specific situation?" |
| Subject provides only recent examples | Redirect: "What about before this current situation? Has this been a pattern for many years?" |
| Exaggeration or drama | Cross-reference with other items; seek concrete documentation |
| Minimization | Gently probe: "Your spouse/friend/doctor has mentioned...?" |
| Defensiveness | Normalize the question; move on and return later if needed |
| Subject is tangential | Gently redirect: "That's interesting. Let me ask specifically about..." |
| Cultural misunderstanding | Rephrase in culturally appropriate terms while preserving criterion meaning |
| Subject endorses trait but cannot provide examples | Do NOT score positively; request examples again; if none forthcoming, score 0 |

**Critical Rule:** If subjects endorse a trait or behaviour but say they are unable to provide examples, a positive score should NOT be given. If they truly have the trait to a clinically meaningful degree, they should be able to provide anecdotes or examples.

### 3.10 Step 9: Duration and Pacing

| Aspect | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| **Total Interview Duration** | 60-90 minutes typical; may extend to 2+ hours with complex cases |
| **Pacing** | Maintain steady but unhurried pace |
| **Breaks** | Brief breaks permitted if subject fatigues; note this |
| **Splitting Sessions** | Avoid if possible; if necessary, complete within 1-2 weeks |
| **Time per Domain** | Varies; Work and Self typically shorter; Interpersonal and Impulse Control often longer |
| **Recording** | Score during interview or immediately after each item |

### 3.11 Step 10: Clinical Judgment Rules

The IPDE relies heavily on clinical judgment in several areas:

1. **Distinguishing traits from states:** The examiner must differentiate longstanding personality characteristics from transient symptoms of Axis I disorders
2. **Duration requirement:** Behaviours must have been present for at least 5 years
3. **Onset requirement:** At least one criterion of a disorder must have been fulfilled before age 25
4. **Past 12 months:** Unless specifically exempt, the criterion must have manifested during the past year
5. **Cultural considerations:** Behaviours must be evaluated within the subject's cultural context
6. **Informant data:** When informant information contradicts subject self-report, the examiner uses clinical judgment to determine which source is more valid
7. **Overall clinical significance:** A score of 2 (pathological/criterion level) requires not just endorsement but convincing clinical documentation

## SECTION 4: SCORING SYSTEM

### 4.1 Item Scoring Categories

Each IPDE item is scored using a standardized rating scale:

| Score | Meaning | Description |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **0** | Absent or Normal | Behaviour is denied, rare, insignificant, or not supported by convincing examples |
| **1** | Exaggerated or Accentuated | Trait is present to an exaggerated degree OR occasionally present at pathological level, but does NOT fully meet criterion threshold |
| **2** | Criterion Level (Pathological) | Behaviour is clearly present, well-documented with examples, meets the full diagnostic criterion |
| **?** | Inadequate Information | Subject could not or would not provide adequate information to score the item |
| **NA** | Not Applicable | The item does not apply to this subject (e.g., occupational items for those who never worked) |

### 4.2 Detailed Scoring Guidelines

#### Score of 2 (Pathological/Criterion Level):
- The behaviour is **frequently** present (unless the criterion specifies otherwise)
- It is supported by **convincing examples, anecdotes, and descriptions**
- It causes **subjective distress** or **problems in social or occupational functioning**
- It has been present for **at least 5 years**
- It has manifested **during the past 12 months** (unless specifically exempt)
- At least one criterion must have onset **before age 25**

#### Score of 1 (Exaggerated/Accentuated):
A score of 1 is assigned when:
- The behaviour is **occasionally** rather than frequently present at criterion level
- The behaviour is present frequently but is **less than convincing** in documentation
- The behaviour is present but its clinical significance is **uncertain**
- The behaviour almost always meets one part of a criterion but not the full criterion

#### Score of 0 (Absent/Normal):
- The behaviour is denied
- The behaviour is rare or insignificant
- The behaviour is not supported by the subject's description
- Examples provided are unconvincing
- The behaviour is explained by other factors (illness, medications, cultural norms)

#### Score of ? (Inadequate Information):
- Used when the subject cannot or will not provide sufficient information
- Should be used sparingly
- The examiner should first attempt all reasonable probes before resorting to this score
- Items scored ? are treated as 0 in diagnostic algorithms

#### Score of NA (Not Applicable):
- The item genuinely does not apply to the subject's life situation
- Example: Occupational items for someone who has never worked and is not a homemaker/student
- NA items are excluded from the diagnostic algorithm for that criterion


### 4.3 Temporal Scoring Conventions

The IPDE employs specific temporal requirements:

#### Duration Requirement:
- **Minimum 5 years** of the behaviour being present
- A 3-year requirement may optionally be used for adolescents (but must be specified as non-standard)
- Less than 5 years for subjects over 20 is discouraged

#### Onset Requirement:
- At least ONE criterion of the disorder must have been fulfilled **before age 25 years**
- This is recorded in the "<25" column on the scoring sheet
- Behaviours with onset at age 25 or later are recorded in the ">=25" column

#### Past 12 Months Requirement:
- The behaviour must have been present (at least to some degree) during the past 12 months
- Some criteria are specifically exempt from this requirement (e.g., arrests, suicide attempts - these need only have occurred within the past 5 years)
- Items exempt from the 12-month requirement are marked with an asterisk (*) in the interview schedule

#### Recording Temporal Information:

| Column | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **<25** (Column 1) | Score applies; onset before age 25, present in past 12 months |
| **>=25** (Column 2) | Score applies; onset at age 25 or later, present in past 12 months |

### 4.4 Scoring During the Interview

**Best Practice Procedure:**

1. Ask the standardized question
2. Listen to the subject's initial response
3. Probe for examples and documentation as needed
4. Determine temporal parameters (duration, onset, recency)
5. Assign the score based on scoring guidelines printed beneath the item
6. Record the score on the interview schedule or answer sheet
7. Move to the next item

**Important Scoring Principles:**
- The score is based on the **behaviour itself**, not merely on the subject's endorsement
- **Behavioural examples** are essential for scores of 1 or 2
- A subject saying "yes" without documentation does NOT warrant a positive score
- Rationalizations, ambition, or cultural factors must be considered as potential explanations
- When behaviour during the interview contradicts self-report, ask the subject to reconcile; ultimately score the response, not the interview behaviour (except for observational items 65-67)


### 4.5 Informant Scoring

The IPDE provides a second scoring column for informant data:

**When to Use Informant Data:**
- When information from family, friends, mental health professionals, or records clearly contradicts the subject's responses
- When the examiner has MORE confidence in the informant data than in the subject's self-report
- When the informant source satisfies the identical scoring guidelines that apply to the subject

**Informant Scoring Rules:**
1. Record informant-based scores in brackets [ ] on the scoresheet
2. When transcribing scores, informant scores ALWAYS override subject scores
3. Both the examiner's confidence and the quality of informant documentation must meet threshold
4. Do NOT confront subjects with informant discrepancies during the interview

### 4.6 Diagnostic Algorithms (Handscoring)

The handscoring algorithms follow a standardized decision tree for each disorder:

#### Algorithm Steps (Example: F60.0 Paranoid):

```
Step 1: Count informant-based scores [in brackets]
        -> Enter total after "Number of Criteria Based on Informant(s)"

Step 2: Check if ANY positive score (1 or 2) exists in Column 1 (<25)
        -> If NO positive score: Enter 0 for Number of Criteria Met
           and Dimensional Score. Check "Negative." Move to next disorder.

Step 3: Add ALL scores in Columns 1 and 2
        -> Enter sum as "Dimensional Score"

Step 4: Check if any score of 2 exists in Column 1 (<25)
        -> If NO score of 2: Enter 0 for Number of Criteria Met.
           Check "Negative." Move to next disorder.

Step 5: Count the number of 2s in BOTH Columns 1 and 2
        -> Enter as "Number of Criteria Met"

Step 6: Apply diagnostic threshold:
        -> If Number of Criteria Met < threshold-1: Check "Negative"
        -> If Number of Criteria Met >= threshold: Check "Definite"
        -> If Number of Criteria Met = threshold-1: Check "Probable"
```

### 4.7 Diagnostic Thresholds by Disorder (ICD-10)

| Disorder | Total Criteria | Required for DEFINITE | Required for PROBABLE |
|----------|---------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| F60.0 Paranoid | 7 | >= 4 | 3 |
| F60.1 Schizoid | 9 | >= 4 | 3 |
| F60.2 Dissocial | 6 | >= 3 | 2 |
| F60.30 Emotionally Unstable - Impulsive | 5 (+2 required) | >= 3 (including Item 30) | 2 |
| F60.31 Emotionally Unstable - Borderline | 5 (+2 required) | >= 3 (including Item 50) | 2 |
| F60.4 Histrionic | 6 | >= 4 | 3 |
| F60.5 Anankastic | 8 | >= 4 | 3 |
| F60.6 Anxious | 7 (partial criteria combined) | >= 4 | 3 |
| F60.7 Dependent | 6 | >= 4 | 3 |
| F60.9 Unspecified | N/A | >= 10 total criteria (no specific disorder met) | 9 total criteria |

**Special Rules:**
- **Emotionally Unstable - Impulsive Type:** Item 30 (quarrelsome when thwarted) MUST be scored 2 for a Definite diagnosis
- **Emotionally Unstable - Borderline Type:** Item 50 (unstable mood) MUST be scored 2, AND criteria for Impulsive type must also be met
- **Partial criteria:** Some criteria are combined from two items (e.g., Dissocial criterion 4 combines items 15+60)


### 4.8 Dimensional Scoring

In addition to categorical diagnoses, the IPDE produces a **dimensional score** for each disorder:

**Calculation:**
- Sum of ALL scores (0, 1, and 2) for all items pertaining to that disorder
- Includes scores from both Column 1 (<25) and Column 2 (>=25)
- Provides a continuous measure of the degree to which a personality disorder is present
- Higher dimensional scores indicate greater pathology in that domain

**Clinical Utility of Dimensional Scores:**
- More reliable than categorical diagnoses (less affected by threshold effects)
- Useful for tracking treatment progress over time
- Captures sub-threshold pathology that may still be clinically significant
- Provides greater versatility in research data analysis
- Recognizes that personality traits are continuously distributed in the population

### 4.9 Computer Scoring vs. Handscoring

| Feature | Computer Scoring | Handscoring |
|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Speed** | Faster for multiple subjects | Adequate for individual cases |
| **Accuracy** | Eliminates arithmetic errors | Subject to human error |
| **Flexibility** | Can generate multiple output formats | Limited to scoresheet format |
| **Partial Criteria** | Automatically combines partial items | Requires manual combination tables |
| **Output** | Diagnoses, dimensional scores, profiles | Same, but manually derived |
| **Requirement** | Specific software | Manual algorithms + scoresheet |

### 4.10 Summary of Scoring Process

```
SCORING WORKFLOW:

1. Score each item during/immediately after administration (0, 1, 2, ?, NA)
   |
2. Record on Interview Schedule or Answer Sheet
   |
3. Transcribe to Scoresheet (following specific rules):
   - Informant scores override subject scores
   - Enter in appropriate onset column (<25 or >=25)
   - Scores of ? treated as 0
   |
4. Apply Diagnostic Algorithm for each disorder:
   - Calculate Dimensional Score (sum of all scores)
   - Determine Number of Criteria Met (count of 2s)
   - Apply threshold rules
   |
5. Assign Diagnosis:
   - DEFINITE: Meets full threshold
   - PROBABLE: One criterion below threshold
   - NEGATIVE: Below probable threshold
   |
6. Rate Confidence Level (1=High, 2=Moderate, 3=Low)
   |
7. Complete Summary Scoresheet
```

### 5.2 Criteria Fulfillment Rules

For a criterion to be considered "met" (scored 2), ALL of the following must apply:

1. **Behavioural documentation:** Supported by convincing examples, anecdotes, or descriptions
2. **Duration:** Present for at least 5 years
3. **Recency:** Present during the past 12 months (unless specifically exempt)
4. **Onset:** For at least ONE criterion per disorder, onset must be before age 25
5. **Clinical significance:** The behaviour causes distress or impairment (where specified)
6. **Not better explained by:** Episodic mental illness, medication effects, or specific life circumstances

### 5.3 Diagnostic Categories: Definite, Probable, and Absent (Negative)

| Diagnosis Level | Meaning | Clinical Implication |
|----------------|---------|---------------------|
| **DEFINITE** | Meets the full ICD-10 threshold for the disorder | Formal personality disorder diagnosis can be assigned |
| **PROBABLE** | Meets one criterion fewer than required for Definite | Significant personality pathology present; warrants clinical attention; may represent sub-threshold disorder |
| **NEGATIVE** | Below probable threshold | Disorder is not present to a clinically significant degree |

### 5.4 Time Requirement and Stability

The IPDE enforces strict temporal criteria to ensure that diagnoses reflect stable personality patterns rather than transient states:

- **5-year minimum duration:** Distinguishes personality traits from episodic illness or situational reactions
- **Onset before age 25:** Ensures the pattern began early in development (as per ICD-10 general criteria)
- **Not episodic:** Behaviours must be pervasive across situations, not confined to episodes of illness
- **Stable pattern:** The pattern should be relatively consistent over time (not characterized by periods of complete remission)

### 5.5 Optional Diagnostic Modifiers

The IPDE provides optional scoring for:

| Modifier | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **Late Onset** | Criteria fulfilled, but all onset at age 25 or later |
| **Past Diagnosis** | Previously met criteria, but has NOT done so during the past year (12 months) |
| **Past Late Onset** | Both modifiers apply |

### 5.6 Differential Diagnosis Considerations

When interpreting IPDE results, the examiner must carefully distinguish personality disorders from:

| Condition | Differentiation Strategy |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Major Depression** | Personality traits should be present when NOT in a depressive episode; chronic emptiness differs from episodic depression |
| **Anxiety Disorders** | Distinguish trait anxiety (lifelong avoidance) from episodic/situational anxiety |
| **Bipolar Disorder** | Mood instability in personality disorders is typically within hours/days, not weeks; not accompanied by full manic syndrome |
| **PTSD** | Personality changes secondary to trauma should be distinguished from pre-existing personality disorder |
| **Substance Use** | Disinhibited behaviour while intoxicated does not equal personality disorder; assess behaviour when sober |
| **Psychotic Disorders** | Residual symptoms of schizophrenia may mimic schizoid traits; assess pre-morbid personality |
| **Organic/Medical Conditions** | Rule out personality changes due to brain injury, endocrine disorders, etc. |
| **Normal Personality Variation** | Traits must cause distress or impairment to be considered disordered |
| **Cultural/Subcultural Norms** | Behaviours normative within a culture should not be pathologized |

### 5.7 Comorbidity

Multiple personality disorder diagnoses are common. The IPDE field trial demonstrated significant co-occurrence among disorders:
- Comorbidity rates were highest within DSM-IV clusters (e.g., avoidant with dependent)
- Cross-cluster comorbidity also occurs (e.g., borderline with paranoid)
- When multiple disorders are diagnosed, all should be reported
- The dimensional scores provide additional information about the pattern of comorbidity

## SECTION 6: QUESTIONS AND ITEMS

### 6.1 Nature of IPDE Questions

The IPDE interview questions have several distinctive characteristics:

1. **Direct and specific:** Questions target specific diagnostic criteria
2. **Behaviourally anchored:** Focus on observable behaviours and verifiable experiences
3. **Open to probing:** Initial questions are followed by standardized probes
4. **Culturally sensitive:** Designed for use across different cultural contexts
5. **Self-report based:** Rely primarily on the subject's account of their own behaviour
6. **Documentation-requiring:** Demand examples and anecdotes for positive scoring

### 6.2 Types of Item Formats

The IPDE uses several question formats (paraphrased descriptions, not verbatim items):

#### Type 1: Direct Self-Assessment Questions
- Ask subjects directly whether they have a particular trait or behaviour
- Example type: Questions about whether the subject is perfectionistic, cautious, or impulsive

#### Type 2: Behavioural Frequency Questions
- Inquire about how often specific behaviours occur
- Example type: Questions about frequency of anger outbursts, mood changes, or social avoidance

#### Type 3: Impact/Consequence Questions
- Assess whether a behaviour causes problems or distress
- Example type: Follow-up questions about whether a trait creates difficulties at work or in relationships

#### Type 4: Relationship/Interpersonal Pattern Questions
- Explore patterns in how the subject relates to others
- Example type: Questions about trust, dependence, closeness, and conflict in relationships

#### Type 5: Historical/Developmental Questions
- Assess onset and duration of behaviours
- Example type: Questions about when a pattern began and whether it has been consistent

#### Type 6: Observational Ratings (Examiner-Rated)
- Scored based on subject's behaviour during the interview itself
- Three items at interview conclusion: pedantry/formality, social insensitivity, emotional coldness/detachment

### 6.3 How Items Relate to Diagnostic Criteria

Each IPDE item is explicitly linked to one (or occasionally more) ICD-10 diagnostic criteria:

```
ITEM STRUCTURE (as printed in interview schedule):

  Item Number:    [e.g., "1."]
  Scoring Grid:  [0 1 2 ? NA]  [0 1 2]
  Criterion:     [e.g., "Undue preoccupation with productivity..."]
  Disorder:      [e.g., "Anankastic: 5"]
  
  Questions:     [Standardized interview questions]
  
  Scoring Notes: [Detailed guidance for the examiner on how to rate]
  
  Score of 2:    [Specific definition of what constitutes a 2]
  Score of 1:    [Specific definition of what constitutes a 1]
  Score of 0:    [Specific definition of what constitutes a 0]
```

**Key Feature:** The criterion and its disorder are printed above the questions, so the examiner always knows exactly what diagnostic criterion they are assessing.


### 6.4 Difference Between Screener and Interview Questions

| Feature | Screening Questionnaire | Interview Questions |
|---------|------------------------|---------------------|
| **Format** | True/False self-report | Open-ended with probes |
| **Depth** | Surface-level endorsement | In-depth with examples |
| **Scoring** | Dichotomous (T/F) | Graded (0, 1, 2, ?, NA) |
| **Documentation** | No examples required | Examples essential |
| **Clinical Judgment** | Minimal | Extensive |
| **Time Frame** | "Past five years" (stated in instructions) | 5-year duration + onset before 25 + past 12 months |
| **Purpose** | Identify who needs full interview | Make diagnostic decisions |
| **Diagnostic Use** | NEVER for diagnosis | Basis for diagnosis |
| **Example** | "I'm a very cautious person" (T/F) | "Do you have a lot of doubts about things?" + probes + examples + scoring |

### 6.5 Illustrative Examples of Item Content (Paraphrased)

**Note:** The following are paraphrased descriptions of the types of content assessed. They are NOT verbatim reproductions of copyrighted test items.

#### Work Domain Examples:
- Assessing whether excessive devotion to work excludes leisure and relationships
- Determining if perfectionism prevents task completion
- Evaluating preoccupation with organizational details at the expense of the main task

#### Self Domain Examples:
- Exploring uncertainty about personal identity across different situations
- Assessing fluctuation in life goals and career direction
- Evaluating uncertainty about moral values or sexual orientation

#### Interpersonal Domain Examples:
- Investigating patterns of intense but unstable close relationships
- Assessing chronic suspiciousness and distrust of others' motives
- Evaluating avoidance of social situations due to fear of rejection
- Exploring patterns of excessive dependence on others for decisions

#### Affect Domain Examples:
- Assessing chronic feelings of emptiness
- Evaluating rapid shifts in mood (within hours to days)
- Determining persistent tension and worry as personality features
- Exploring emotional coldness or inability to express feelings

#### Reality Testing Examples:
- Investigating beliefs about conspiracies or hidden meanings
- Assessing unfounded suspicions about partner's fidelity

#### Impulse Control Examples:
- Evaluating history of impulsive actions with harmful consequences
- Assessing patterns of physical aggression or violence
- Investigating self-harm behaviours and suicidal gestures
- Exploring patterns of irresponsibility and disregard for social norms

### 6.6 Abbreviated Interview Option

For time-constrained situations, two abbreviation options exist:

**Option 1: Selective Administration**
- Omit items not pertaining to disorders of interest
- Always include the open-ended introductory questions for any section containing relevant items

**Option 2: Screen-Based Abbreviation**
- Administer screening questionnaire first
- Interview only for disorders where the screen was "failed"
- Maintains full interview integrity for assessed disorders

### 7.2 Temporal Stability

The temporal stability of the IPDE was assessed by re-examining 243 subjects after an average interval of 6 months:

#### Item-Level Stability (Intraclass Correlations):

| ICC Range | Number of Items (Uncorrected) | Number of Items (Corrected for Attenuation) |
|-----------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| 0.80-0.89 | 0 | 2 |
| 0.70-0.79 | 2 | 13 |
| 0.60-0.69 | 14 | 42 |
| 0.50-0.59 | 62 | 62 |
| 0.40-0.49 | 53 | 27 |

#### Diagnostic-Level Stability:
- Kappa for "any personality disorder" (Definite): 0.62 (DSM-III-R), 0.59 (ICD-10)
- These values compare favourably with temporal stability of Axis I instruments (e.g., SADS-L)
- Stability is influenced by imperfect single-administration reliability (correction for attenuation applied)


### 7.3 Validity Evidence

#### Procedural Validity:
- The IPDE has "procedural validity" - its standardized administration ensures that conclusions are more readily exportable and less susceptible to institutional and regional biases
- Provides clinicians with a more uniform method of case identification
- Facilitates comparison and replication of research findings across centres

#### Clinical Validity:
- Most clinicians participating in the field trial considered the IPDE a "useful and essentially valid method of assessing personality disorders for research purposes"
- Clinical acceptability was rated highly across diverse international settings
- The interview was considered culturally relevant at most centres

#### Convergent Validity:
- Correspondence with independent clinical diagnoses (LEAD standard: Longitudinal, Expert, All Data)
- Agreement with other structured instruments for personality disorder assessment
- Dimensional scores show expected patterns of correlation with related constructs

#### Cross-Cultural Validity:
- Field-tested across 14 centres in 11 countries (Bangalore, Geneva, Leiden, London, Luxembourg, Munich, Nairobi, New York, Nottingham, Oslo, Tokyo, Vienna)
- Demonstrated utility across diverse cultural, linguistic, and socioeconomic contexts
- Interviewers from all centres reported the instrument as culturally acceptable

### 7.4 Strengths of the IPDE

1. **International standardization:** The only personality disorder interview designed and tested for worldwide cross-cultural use
2. **Dual classification system:** Covers both ICD-10 and DSM-IV in modular format
3. **Detailed scoring manual:** Item-by-item scoring guidelines minimize ambiguity
4. **Both categorical and dimensional output:** Provides diagnostic categories AND continuous dimensional scores
5. **Strong psychometric properties:** Demonstrated reliability comparable to Axis I instruments
6. **Temporal safeguards:** 5-year duration and onset requirements protect against state-trait confusion
7. **Documentation requirement:** Demands behavioural examples, reducing false-positive rates
8. **Informant integration:** Systematic provision for incorporating collateral information
9. **Multiple language translations:** Available in numerous languages with back-translation verification
10. **Training structure:** Clear qualifications and training requirements specified
11. **Screening option:** Self-administered questionnaire allows efficient pre-screening
12. **Abbreviation options:** Can be shortened for specific research or clinical needs

### 7.5 Limitations of the IPDE

1. **Self-report dependency:** Assumes subjects can provide valid descriptions of their personality; individuals may lack insight into their own traits
2. **Social desirability bias:** Subjects may deny socially undesirable traits or exaggerate distress
3. **Time-consuming:** Full administration requires 60-90+ minutes, which may be impractical in some clinical settings
4. **Examiner qualifications:** Requires highly trained clinicians (psychiatrists or clinical psychologists); cannot be administered by lay interviewers
5. **State-trait confusion:** Despite safeguards, current mental state (severe depression, psychosis) may contaminate personality assessment
6. **Cultural limitations:** Despite cross-cultural testing, personality disorder concepts remain predominantly Western in origin
7. **No gold standard:** Validity assessment is complicated by the absence of an accepted gold standard for personality disorder diagnosis
8. **Informant challenges:** Integrating discrepant subject and informant data remains procedurally complex
9. **Literacy requirement:** Screening questionnaire requires reading ability; interview requires adequate verbal ability
10. **Not suitable for:** Severe psychosis, significant cognitive impairment, below-normal intelligence, very young subjects (<18)
11. **Comorbidity complexity:** High rates of diagnostic overlap raise questions about discriminant validity of individual categories
12. **Threshold arbitrariness:** Diagnostic cut-points (number of criteria required) are somewhat arbitrary

### IX. INTERPRETATION

Ms. Sharma's IPDE profile is consistent with a diagnosis of **Emotionally Unstable Personality Disorder, Borderline Type (F60.31)** according to ICD-10 criteria. This diagnosis captures the core features of her presentation:

**Identity and Self-Concept:** Ms. Sharma demonstrates a pervasive pattern of identity disturbance, with marked uncertainty about her self-image, long-term goals, and internal values. She describes feeling like "a chameleon" who takes on the identity of those around her. This has contributed to an unstable career trajectory and chronic dissatisfaction.

**Relationship Patterns:** Her interpersonal relationships are characterized by a pattern of intense involvement followed by dramatic dissolution. She oscillates between idealization ("This person is perfect for me") and devaluation ("They're just like everyone else who has let me down"). Frantic efforts to avoid abandonment—including threatening self-harm—are a consistent feature.

**Affective Instability:** The most prominent feature is marked instability of mood, with rapid shifts between dysphoria, irritability, and anxiety, typically lasting hours rather than days. This mood reactivity is predominantly triggered by interpersonal events and perceived rejection or abandonment. Chronic feelings of emptiness further compound her emotional distress.

**Impulsivity and Self-Harm:** Ms. Sharma demonstrates a long-standing pattern (8+ years) of impulsive and self-destructive behaviours, including recurrent self-harm (wrist cutting), one serious suicide attempt, impulsive spending, and angry outbursts with property destruction. These behaviours typically occur in the context of perceived abandonment or interpersonal conflict.

**Sub-threshold Anxious and Dependent Features:** The probable diagnoses of Anxious and Dependent personality disorders reflect secondary personality features—persistent tension, fear of rejection, difficulty making independent decisions, and subordination of needs—that are partially embedded within the broader borderline pathology.

The dimensional scores indicate that while Paranoid and Histrionic features are elevated compared to normative expectations, they do not reach diagnostic threshold. The elevated Paranoid score reflects interpersonal suspiciousness that appears stress-related rather than pervasive.


### X. DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS

**Personality Disorder vs. Recurrent Depressive Disorder:** While Ms. Sharma has a comorbid diagnosis of Recurrent Depressive Disorder, her personality pathology is clearly distinguishable. The self-harm, identity disturbance, relational instability, and impulsivity have been consistently present for over 10 years, persisting during euthymic periods. These features preceded her first depressive episode and are not confined to depressive episodes.

**Emotionally Unstable (Borderline) vs. Histrionic:** Although Ms. Sharma displays some dramatic self-expression and emotional lability consistent with histrionic features, the depth of identity disturbance, self-destructive behaviour, and chronic emptiness are more consistent with borderline pathology. Histrionic personality typically involves attention-seeking without the self-destructive component.

**Emotionally Unstable (Borderline) vs. Dissocial:** Despite some impulsive aggression, Ms. Sharma demonstrates capacity for guilt and remorse, does not show callous disregard for others' feelings, and her aggression is reactive (interpersonally triggered) rather than instrumental. Dissocial personality disorder is not supported.

**Personality Disorder vs. Complex PTSD:** Given her history of childhood emotional neglect, complex PTSD was considered. However, the pervasive nature of the personality disturbance across all life domains, its early onset (adolescence), and the specific pattern of identity disturbance and interpersonal instability are most consistent with a personality disorder diagnosis.

### XI. CONCLUSION

Based on comprehensive IPDE assessment, Ms. Priya Sharma meets ICD-10 criteria for **Emotionally Unstable Personality Disorder, Borderline Type (F60.31) - Definite diagnosis** with high confidence. Sub-threshold anxious and dependent personality features are also present. This personality disorder is of moderate-to-severe clinical significance and is the primary driver of her recurrent self-harm presentations, relationship instability, and occupational difficulties. The disorder has been present for a minimum of 10 years with onset in adolescence.

### XII. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Psychotherapy:** Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT) is recommended as the primary treatment modality, given strong evidence for its efficacy in Borderline Personality Disorder. A minimum of 12-18 months of structured treatment is advised.

2. **Treatment Focus Areas:**
   - Emotion regulation skills training
   - Distress tolerance (alternative to self-harm)
   - Interpersonal effectiveness
   - Identity consolidation work (longer-term)

3. **Risk Management:**
   - Safety planning with clear crisis protocols
   - Regular monitoring of self-harm urges
   - Collaborative development of a "life worth living" plan
   - Crisis contact information reviewed

4. **Medication:** Continue current antidepressant under psychiatric supervision. Consider mood stabilizer if affective instability does not improve with DBT.

5. **Further Assessment:** Consider periodic re-assessment (6-12 months) to track dimensional score changes with treatment.

6. **Prognosis:** With structured, evidence-based treatment (DBT), moderate improvement in self-harm behaviours and interpersonal functioning is expected within 12-18 months. Complete remission of personality disorder diagnosis is possible with sustained treatment (3-5 years).

## SECTION 9: PRACTICAL CLINICAL TIPS

### 9.1 Common Mistakes in IPDE Administration

| Mistake | Consequence | Correction |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Scoring based on endorsement alone without examples | False positives (over-diagnosis) | Always require behavioural documentation |
| Confusing current symptoms with longstanding traits | State-trait confusion | Verify 5-year duration for every positive score |
| Accepting vague generalities as evidence | Unreliable scoring | Probe for specific, concrete examples |
| Skipping temporal probes (onset, duration, recency) | Invalid scoring | Systematically assess all temporal parameters |
| Confronting subjects with informant data during interview | Damaged rapport | Record discrepancies; reconcile after interview |
| Scoring observational items (65-67) based on brief impressions | Unreliable scores | Base on entire interview observation |
| Administering to severely depressed/psychotic patients | Invalid assessment | Postpone until acute symptoms remit |
| Using screening questionnaire as diagnostic tool | Misdiagnosis | Screen only identifies who needs interview |
| Rushing through the interview | Missed pathology, poor documentation | Maintain unhurried pace; allow 60-90 minutes |
| Failing to distinguish cultural norms from pathology | Cultural bias in diagnosis | Evaluate behaviour within cultural context |
| Scoring partial criteria items without combining properly | Scoring errors | Follow manual algorithms for combined items |
| Leading questions or suggesting answers | Examiner bias | Use standardized questions; probe neutrally |

### 9.2 How to Probe Effectively

#### Probing Strategies by Situation:

**When the subject endorses a trait but provides no examples:**
```
"You mentioned that you [trait]. Can you give me a specific situation 
where that happened?"

"What did you actually do in that situation?"

"How did that affect your work/relationships/daily life?"
```

**When the subject denies a trait that may be present:**
```
"Have people ever told you that you're like that?"

"Why do you think they've said that?"

"Has anyone - a family member, friend, or therapist - ever 
commented on this aspect of your behaviour?"
```

**When responses are vague or superficial:**
```
"Can you be more specific about what happened?"

"Give me an example from the last year."

"What exactly did you say/do in that situation?"

"How often would you say this happens - daily, weekly, monthly?"
```

**When distinguishing trait from state:**
```
"Has this been the way you are most of the time, or mainly when 
you're feeling [depressed/anxious/stressed]?"

"Were you like this before your [current illness] started?"

"If you think back 5 years or more, was this already a pattern?"

"Has this been consistent over many years, or does it come and go?"
```

**When assessing clinical significance:**
```
"Does this cause any problems for you?"

"How does this affect your relationships/work/daily life?"

"Do other people find this difficult or problematic?"

"Would you say this has caused you significant distress?"
```


### 9.3 Cultural Considerations

The IPDE was designed for cross-cultural use, but examiners must be aware of several cultural factors:

#### Areas Requiring Cultural Sensitivity:

| Domain | Cultural Consideration |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Interpersonal dependence** | In collectivist cultures, reliance on family for decisions may be normative, not pathological |
| **Emotional expression** | Cultures vary in norms for emotional display; "emotional coldness" must be judged against cultural baseline |
| **Sexual behaviour** | Questions about sexuality may be inappropriate or highly sensitive in some cultures; use ? score when inquiry is culturally inappropriate |
| **Aggressiveness** | What constitutes "appropriate" assertiveness varies by culture and gender |
| **Suspiciousness** | In settings with genuine persecution (political, ethnic), vigilance may be adaptive |
| **Social avoidance** | Cultural norms regarding social interaction vary widely |
| **Gender roles** | Dependence, submissiveness, or assertiveness must be evaluated within gender-role expectations of the culture |
| **Work devotion** | What constitutes "excessive" work dedication varies by cultural and economic context |
| **Conscientiousness** | Religious and cultural traditions may prescribe high levels of rule adherence |

#### Cultural Adaptation Guidelines:

1. **Judge behaviour against cultural norms** of the subject's own community
2. **Religious individuals** should be compared to others of the same religious background
3. **Gender-role behaviour** should be evaluated within the cultural context
4. **When cultural appropriateness is unclear**, err on the side of NOT scoring positively
5. **Regional dialects and literacy** may require adaptation of question wording while preserving criterion meaning
6. **Informant data** may be especially important in cultures where self-disclosure is limited
7. **Use the ? score** when cultural factors make assessment impossible for a specific item

### 9.4 Interviewer Training Requirements

#### Minimum Qualifications (as specified by the IPDE manual):

- **Professional status:** Experienced psychiatrists, clinical psychologists, or those with comparable training
- **Diagnostic capability:** Must be capable of making independent psychiatric diagnoses WITHOUT a semi-structured interview
- **NOT appropriate for:** Early-career clinicians in training, research assistants, nurses, medical students, or graduate students (without supervision)

#### Training Steps:

| Step | Activity | Duration |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Study the IPDE interview and scoring manual thoroughly | Self-paced (several days) |
| 2 | Study the relevant classification system (ICD-10/DSM-IV) criteria for all personality disorders | Self-paced |
| 3 | Observe at least 2-3 complete IPDE administrations by an experienced examiner | 4-6 hours |
| 4 | Conduct practice interviews under supervision | 2-4 interviews |
| 5 | Score practice interviews and compare with trainer's scores | Concurrent |
| 6 | Achieve acceptable inter-rater agreement before independent use | Until criterion met |
| 7 | Periodic reliability checks and ongoing consultation | Continuous |

#### Key Training Competencies:

1. **Mastery of scoring guidelines** - Can correctly apply all scoring rules without consulting manual for routine items
2. **Temporal probing** - Systematically assesses onset, duration, and recency for every positive score
3. **State-trait differentiation** - Can reliably distinguish personality traits from symptoms of Axis I disorders
4. **Clinical judgment** - Exercises appropriate judgment in ambiguous situations
5. **Cultural competence** - Can adapt questioning style without compromising standardization
6. **Rapport maintenance** - Conducts intensive probing while maintaining therapeutic alliance
7. **Documentation quality** - Obtains behavioural examples of sufficient quality and specificity


### 9.5 Special Populations and Situations

#### Adolescents (Age 15-18):
- Use with caution; personality is still developing
- Consider 3-year rather than 5-year duration requirement (if used, specify deviation)
- Be conservative with diagnoses; personality traits may be developmental and transient
- The standard IPDE instructions discourage use below age 18

#### Patients with Current Axis I Disorders:
- **Mild to moderate depression/anxiety:** Proceed with IPDE; evidence suggests the PDE is resistant to contamination from mild-moderate symptoms
- **Severe depression:** Postpone assessment until symptoms remit
- **Active psychosis:** Do NOT administer; postpone until stable remission
- **Substance intoxication/withdrawal:** Postpone; assess when sober and stable

#### Forensic Settings:
- Be aware of potential malingering or impression management
- Documentation requirement of the IPDE helps identify fabricated responses
- Consider that subjects may minimize antisocial traits
- Informant data especially valuable in this context

#### Inpatient vs. Outpatient Settings:
- Inpatients may be in acute distress; assess timing carefully
- Outpatients may have more stable presentations
- Neither setting is inherently superior for IPDE administration

### 9.6 Integration with Other Assessments

The IPDE is most informative when used as part of a comprehensive assessment battery:

| Assessment Component | Purpose | Timing |
|---------------------|---------|--------|
| Clinical interview (history, MSE) | Background information, current mental state | Before IPDE |
| Axis I assessment (SCAN, SCID-I, MINI) | Rule out/identify comorbid disorders | Before IPDE |
| IPDE Screening Questionnaire | Identify probable personality pathology | Before IPDE interview |
| IPDE Semi-Structured Interview | Formal personality disorder assessment | Core assessment |
| Self-report measures (optional) | Additional data point (e.g., MCMI-IV, PDQ-4) | Before or after IPDE |
| Informant interview/records review | Corroborate or challenge self-report | Before or during IPDE scoring |
| Cognitive/neuropsychological testing | Rule out cognitive factors if suspected | As indicated |

### A.3 ICD-10 General Criteria for Personality Disorder (F60)

For ANY specific personality disorder to be diagnosed, the following general criteria must ALSO be met:

1. The pattern is **not directly attributable** to gross brain damage or disease, or to another psychiatric disorder
2. The pattern is **stable and of long duration**, with onset in late childhood or adolescence
3. The pattern is **pervasive** and clearly maladaptive across a broad range of personal and social situations
4. The pattern leads to **considerable personal distress** (which may only become apparent late in the course)
5. The pattern is usually (but not always) associated with **significant problems in occupational and social performance**
6. The pattern represents **extreme or significant deviations** from the way in which the average individual perceives, thinks, feels, and relates to others
