1. What is the Rorschach

  • A performance-based (projective) personality test using 10 standardized inkblots; the person says what each blot "might be," and the perceptual–verbal response is coded and compared with norms.
  • It samples the problem-solving / perceptual process: how a person organizes ambiguous stimuli, what they emphasize, and how they translate input into meaning — yielding inferences about thinking, reality testing, affect, self-image, controls, and interpersonal perception.
  • Conceptually a study of perception + projection: response selection reflects stable psychological operations, not just "imagination."
  • Five of the ten cards are achromatic (I, IV, V, VI, VII) and five contain chromatic colour (II, III, VIII, IX, X).

2. History — key points

  • Hermann Rorschach (Swiss psychiatrist) published Psychodiagnostik in 1921; he died in 1922, so the method was elaborated by successors.
  • Posthumous 1923 paper (Rorschach & Oberholzer) introduced Popular responses (answers occurring at least once in every three records).
  • Five major American systems developed independently and diverged: Beck Klopfer Hertz Piotrowski Rapaport–Schafer. This created reliability/validity problems.
  • John E. Exner compared the systems empirically and built the Comprehensive System (CS), integrating the most defensible, reliable elements; Vol. 1 published 1974, with later editions and workbooks (1986, 1991, 1993, 2001/2003).
  • CS strengths: standardized administration, coding, normative reference data, and high inter-rater/retest reliability for stable variables.
  • Indices evolved over time: SCZI → PTI (Schizophrenia Index revised to the Perceptual-Thinking Index); plus DEPI, CDI, S-CON, HVI, OBS.
  • Later development: R-PAS (Rorschach Performance Assessment System) — an internationally normed successor approach.
  • Critiques to be aware of: debate over CS norms (possible over-identification of pathology), cultural variation in Populars.

3. Administration (essentials)

  • Seating: side-by-side (examiner beside, not facing) to reduce nonverbal cueing.
  • Two phases: (1) Response (Association) phase — "What might this be?"; record verbatim; (2) Inquiry phase — clarify where (location), what made it look that way (determinants), and what (content), without leading.
  • Minimum validity: a record with fewer than 14 responses (R < 14) is generally considered unreliable/invalid and should be re-administered.
  • Examiner notes card turning, reaction time, and exact wording.
  • Each response is then coded across several categories (below) and entered into the Sequence of Scores and the Structural Summary.

4. The three data sets (basis of interpretation)

  1. Verbiage — the actual language and elaborations the person uses.
  2. Sequence — the order of responses and codes across cards.
  3. Structural data — frequency tallies, ratios, percentages, and indices (the "hard data"); the primary source of interpretive hypotheses, cross-checked against the other two.

Interpretation proceeds by the deviation principle (compare each datum to norms) but always integrates variables in combination rather than singly.

5. Coding / Scoring categories

5.1 Location & Developmental Quality (DQ)

CodeMeaningInterpretive note
WWhole blot usedEffort to organize the whole; economy/ambition (with M, the W:M ratio reflects aspiration vs capacity).
DCommon detailPractical, conventional focus on obvious areas.
DdUnusual detailIdiosyncratic/peripheral focus; elevations suggest unusual scanning or over-attention to small features.
SWhite spaceOppositionality/negativism, autonomy; S− links negativism to misperception.

Developmental Quality (DQ): quality of the perceptual organization —

  • + synthesized (two+ objects in meaningful relationship, with form demand) — sophisticated.
  • o ordinary (single object with definite form).
  • v/+ synthesized but vague components.
  • v vague (no specific form demand) — immature/impressionistic.

Blends = responses with two or more determinants → psychological complexity. Colour-shading blend = chromatic colour + shading/achromatic (ambivalent, painful/confused affect). Shading blend = multiple shading determinants.

5.3 Form Quality (FQ) — perceptual accuracy

  • + superior-overelaborated; o ordinary (common, accurate); u unusual (uncommon but not a violation of contours); − minus (distorted, fits poorly/contours violated); none no form.
  • FQ− is the key reality-testing marker. Derived percentages: XA% (appropriate form use overall), WDA% (appropriate form in W+D areas), X−% (distortion), X+% (conventional), Xu% (unconventional but not distorted).

5.4 Contents & Populars

  • Contents (examples): H, (H), Hd, (Hd), Hx; A, (A), Ad, (Ad); An (anatomy), Art, Ay, Bl, Bt, Cg, Cl, Ex, Fd (food), Fi, Ge, Hh, Ls, Na, Sc, Sx, Xy, Id.
  • Human content (esp. Pure H) → interest in/realistic perception of people. An+Xy → bodily/somatic preoccupation. Fd → dependency. Isolation Index uses Bt, Cl, Ge, Ls, Na.
  • Popular (P): the most commonly given response to a blot/area (occurs ≥ once in 3 records). Low P = departure from convention; the CS recognizes 13 Populars (see Card section).

5.5 Organizational Activity (Z scores)

  • Zf = frequency of organized responses (processing effort). ZSum = weighted total; Zest = expected sum for that Zf.
  • Zd = ZSum − Zest: processing efficiency.
    • Zd > +3.0 = overincorporation (thorough, cautious, effortful, can be inefficient).
    • Zd < −3.0 = underincorporation (hasty, careless scanning; neglects cues → faulty input).

5.6 Special Scores

Critical Six (cognitive special scores → thought disturbance), Levels 1 & 2

CodeMeaningWeight (WSum6)
DVDeviant Verbalization (odd word/neologism/redundancy)1 / 2
INCOMIncongruous Combination (implausible fusion of features)2 / 4
DRDeviant Response (rambling/inappropriate phrase)3 / 6
FABCOMFabulized Combination (implausible relationship between objects)4 / 7

CONTAMContamination (two percepts fused into one impossible image) — most pathological7

Raw Sum6 = count of the six; WSum6 = weighted sum (higher = more thought disturbance). Level 2 responses indicate more serious slippage. CONTAM is among the most pathognomonic indicators of disordered thinking.

Other (independent) special scores

  • AB abstraction; AG aggressive movement; COP cooperative movement; CP colour projection (denial — sees colour where none exists); MOR morbid (damaged/dysphoric) content; PER personal (defensive authoritarianism); PSV perseveration.
  • GHR / PHR Good vs Poor Human Representation → quality of interpersonal/object representations.

6. Structural Summary — key ratios, percentages & derivations

Core (Controls & Stress Tolerance)

  • Lambda (L) = F / (R−F). High L (>0.99) = avoidant, simplifying style.
  • EB (Erlebnistypus) = Sum M : WSumC → coping style: Introversive (M dominant, ideational), Extratensive (colour dominant, affect-driven), Ambitent (mixed/inconsistent). Caveat: if EA < 4.0, EB style is not validly interpretable (Exception 1).
  • EA = M + WSumC (available, organized resources). EBPer = pervasiveness of the EB style (≥2.5 = pervasive/rigid).
  • eb = (FM+m) : (SumC'+SumT+SumV+SumY); es = sum of those; Adj es removes excess m and Y.
  • D Score = capacity for control/stress tolerance now; Adj D = trait-level/baseline capacity. Minus = overload & proneness to impulsiveness; D < Adj D = situational stress present.

8. Interpretation steps & search strategy

  1. Check validity (R ≥ 14) and the integrity/accuracy of coding.
  2. Review S-CON first (safety).
  3. Identify the first positive Key Variable (e.g., PTI>3, DEPI>5, CDI>3, D<AdjD, AdjD minus, Lambda>.99, Fr+rF>0, EB introversive/extratensive, p>a+1, HVI) — this sets the cluster search order.
  4. If no Key Variable is positive, use Tertiary Variables to choose the starting cluster.
  5. Work cluster-by-cluster, interpreting variables in combination (not singly); the Cognitive Triad (Processing → Mediation → Ideation) is interpreted together, as are Self- and Interpersonal-perception.
  6. Integrate findings into a coherent description; cross-check with sequence and verbatim material.

9. The 7 interpretive clusters (areas of interpretation)

  1. Controls & Stress Tolerance — EA, es, D/AdjD, CDI, L.
  2. Situationally Related Stress — D vs AdjD, m, Y, blends (reviewed when stress is indicated).
  3. Affect — DEPI, EB, FC:CF+C, Afr, SumC':WSumC, Blends, CP, S.
  4. Information Processing — Zf, W:D:Dd, W:M, Zd, DQ, PSV.
  5. Cognitive Mediation — XA%, WDA%, X−%/X+%/Xu%, P, FQ−, S−.
  6. Ideation — EB, a:p, Ma:Mp, MOR, Sum6/WSum6, M−, intellectualization.
  7. Self-Perception — Egocentricity, Fr+rF, FD, V, An+Xy, MOR, H:(H)+Hd+(Hd).
  8. Interpersonal Perception & Behaviour — COP, AG, GHR:PHR, SumT, Human content, Pure H, Isolation Index, a:p, Fd, PER.

(The first seven are routine; the situational-stress cluster is added when indicated — together commonly described as the eight review areas.)

11. High-yield viva points

  • R < 14 → invalid; readminister.
  • Three data sets: verbiage, sequence, structural.
  • EB = M : WSumC (style); EA = M + WSumC (resources); D/Adj D = control; Lambda = avoidant style.
  • Reality testing trio: XA%, WDA%, X−% (and P).
  • Thought disturbance: Sum6/WSum6; CONTAM = most serious; PTI flags perceptual-thinking trouble.
  • Affect: Afr (engagement), FC:CF+C (modulation), C' (constriction), CP (denial), colour-shading blend (ambivalent/painful affect).
  • Self: Egocentricity 3r+(2)/R; reflections → narcissism; MOR → damaged self-image; An+Xy → bodily preoccupation; Vista → painful self-scrutiny.
  • Interpersonal: COP/AG (cooperative vs hostile expectancy); GHR:PHR (quality); T=0 (distance); Pure H (realistic perception of people); Isolation Index (withdrawal).
  • Zd: +>3 overincorporative (cautious/inefficient); −<3 underincorporative (hasty/careless).
  • Six constellations: PTI, DEPI, CDI, S-CON (review first), HVI, OBS.
  • Interpretation is nomothetic (deviation from norms) + idiographic (integration); never interpret a single score in isolation.
