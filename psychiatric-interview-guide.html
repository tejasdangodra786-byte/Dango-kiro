Psychiatric Interview Guide

Detailed Questions for Obtaining a Rich & Comprehensive Case History

General Tips for Rich History Taking: Start with open-ended questions, then narrow down. Use silence effectively. Reflect and summarize. Show empathy. Observe non-verbal cues. Never rush. Let the patient tell their story first before asking structured questions.

1. Rapport Building & Opening

The first few minutes set the tone. Build trust before diving into clinical questions.

  1. "Hello, I am Dr. ___. How would you like me to address you?"
  2. "What brings you here today?" / "How can I help you?"
  3. "Tell me what has been troubling you in your own words."
  4. "How are you feeling right now, at this moment?"
  5. "Is there anything you'd like to ask me before we begin?"
  6. "I want you to know that everything you share here is confidential. Feel free to speak openly."
Tip: Allow the patient to speak uninterrupted for at least 2-3 minutes initially. Note what they say first — it reveals their priority concern.

2. Chief Complaints — In Their Own Words

Get the patient's/informant's spontaneous account. Do NOT interpret or medicalize at this stage.

  1. "What is the main problem that brought you to see me?"
  2. "When did this problem first start?"
  3. "Can you describe exactly what you experienced when it first began?"
  4. "What other problems have you been facing along with this?"
  5. "Which problem bothers you the most?"
  6. "Has anyone around you (family/friends) noticed any changes in you?"
  7. "What do THEY say is the problem?" (for informant)
  8. "In your opinion, why do you think this is happening to you?"

Probe: "Is there anything else that's been worrying you that you haven't mentioned yet?"

3. History of Present Illness (HOPI) — The Heart of Case History

This is where you build the narrative. Think of it as writing the patient's story chronologically.

3A. Onset & Precipitating Factors

  1. "When were you last completely well / your normal self?"
  2. "What was happening in your life around that time? Any stress, loss, conflict, change?"
  3. "Did anything specific trigger the problem — an event, argument, loss of job, relationship issue, financial crisis, bereavement?"
  4. "Was the onset sudden (overnight/within days) or did it build up slowly over weeks/months?"
  5. "What was the very first change you or your family noticed?"
  6. "Were there any physical illnesses, medications, or substance use around the time of onset?"
  7. "Had you recently stopped any medication or substance?"

3B. Symptom Exploration — Go Deep Into Each Symptom

For EACH symptom, ask these 8 dimensions: When did it start? How often does it occur? How severe is it (mild/moderate/severe)? What triggers it? What makes it worse? What makes it better? How does it affect daily life? Has it changed over time (better/worse/same)?

If patient reports Low Mood / Sadness:

  1. "How would you describe your mood on most days?"
  2. "Is the sadness there all the time, or does it come and go?"
  3. "Is there any time of day when you feel worse — morning or evening?"
  4. "Can anything make you feel better temporarily — talking to someone, watching TV, good news?"
  5. "Do you still enjoy things you used to enjoy? Music, food, hobbies, spending time with loved ones?"
  6. "Do you feel like crying? How often? Do tears actually come?"
  7. "Do you feel empty, numb, or like nothing matters anymore?"
  8. "Do you feel hopeless about the future? Like things will never get better?"
  9. "Do you feel guilty about anything? Do you blame yourself for things?"
  10. "Do you feel like a burden to your family?"
  11. "Do you feel you deserve punishment for something?"

If patient reports Anxiety / Worry / Fear:

  1. "What exactly do you worry about? Can you give me examples?"
  2. "Is the worry about something specific, or is it a general feeling of dread?"
  3. "How much of your day is spent worrying?"
  4. "Can you control the worry once it starts, or does it spiral?"
  5. "Do you get physical symptoms with the anxiety — racing heart, sweating, trembling, breathlessness, chest tightness, dizziness, tingling, dry mouth, nausea?"
  6. "Do you avoid certain places, people, or situations because of fear?"
  7. "Do you get sudden episodes of intense fear that come out of nowhere (panic attacks)? How long do they last? What do you feel during them?"
  8. "Do you fear you are going to die, go crazy, or lose control during these episodes?"
  9. "Is the anxiety related to social situations — meeting people, speaking in public, being watched?"
  10. "Do you have any specific fears — heights, animals, closed spaces, blood, injections, darkness?"
  11. "Do you worry excessively about your health? Do you keep checking your body for signs of illness?"

If patient reports Abnormal Beliefs / Suspiciousness:

  1. "Do you feel that people are against you or trying to harm you?"
  2. "Who do you think is doing this? Why would they do it?"
  3. "How did you come to know about this? What evidence do you have?"
  4. "Do you feel people are talking about you, laughing at you, or watching you?"
  5. "Do you feel you have special powers, abilities, or a special mission?"
  6. "Do you believe something is being done to your body without your consent?"
  7. "Do you feel someone is controlling your thoughts or actions from outside?"
  8. "Do you feel your thoughts are being read by others, taken away, or put into your head?"
  9. "Do you feel your thoughts are being broadcast so others can hear them?"
  10. "Are these experiences happening only to you, or do others experience them too?"
  11. "Has anyone tried to convince you that these beliefs might not be true? What was your reaction?"

If patient reports Hearing Voices / Seeing Things (Hallucinations):

  1. "Do you ever hear sounds or voices when no one is around?"
  2. "What do the voices say? Can you make out the words clearly?"
  3. "Is it one voice or many? Male or female? Familiar or unknown?"
  4. "Do they talk to you directly (2nd person) or talk about you amongst themselves (3rd person)?"
  5. "Do they comment on what you are doing in real time?"
  6. "Do they give you commands or instructions? If yes, have you ever followed them?"
  7. "Where do the voices come from — inside your head or from outside (like from the wall, ceiling, a device)?"
  8. "When are they loudest — at night, when alone, when stressed?"
  9. "Do you ever see things that others cannot see? Shadows, figures, animals, lights?"
  10. "Do you feel things crawling on or under your skin (tactile)?"
  11. "Do you smell or taste things that others don't notice?"
  12. "How do these experiences make you feel — scared, comforted, angry, confused?"
  13. "Do you believe these experiences are real, or could they be part of your illness?"

If patient reports Elevated Mood / Overactivity (Mania):

  1. "Have you been feeling unusually happy, energetic, or on top of the world?"
  2. "Do you feel like you need less sleep than usual but still feel energetic?"
  3. "Are your thoughts racing? Do ideas come faster than you can speak them?"
  4. "Have you been talking more than usual? Do people tell you to slow down?"
  5. "Have you been spending more money than usual? Any risky financial decisions?"
  6. "Have you started many new projects or activities all at once?"
  7. "Has your sexual drive increased significantly?"
  8. "Do you feel like you have special abilities, great plans, or are destined for something big?"
  9. "Have you been getting into arguments or physical fights more than usual?"
  10. "Have you been using alcohol or drugs more during this time?"
  11. "Do you feel irritable or angry if someone disagrees with you or tries to stop you?"
  12. "How long has this been going on? Has this happened before?"

If patient reports Obsessive Thoughts / Repetitive Behaviours (OCD):

  1. "Do you get unwanted, intrusive thoughts that keep coming back even though you try to stop them?"
  2. "What are these thoughts about? (contamination, harm, religion, sex, symmetry, doubt)"
  3. "Do these thoughts feel like your own, or do they feel foreign/inserted?"
  4. "Do you recognize these thoughts as excessive or irrational?"
  5. "Do you do anything to reduce the anxiety caused by these thoughts — washing, checking, counting, arranging, repeating, seeking reassurance?"
  6. "How much time per day do these thoughts/rituals take up?"
  7. "What happens if you try to resist doing the ritual? How does that make you feel?"
  8. "Do these thoughts/behaviours interfere with your work, relationships, or daily routine?"
  9. "Does anyone in your family have similar habits?"

If patient reports Bodily Complaints without Medical Cause (Somatization):

  1. "Tell me about the physical symptoms you've been experiencing."
  2. "Have you consulted other doctors for these symptoms? What did they find?"
  3. "Do the symptoms move around the body or stay in one place?"
  4. "Are the symptoms constant or do they come and go? What triggers them?"
  5. "Do you notice the symptoms are worse when you are stressed or upset?"
  6. "How convinced are you that there is a serious physical illness despite normal test results?"
  7. "Do you feel the doctors have missed something?"
  8. "How much do these symptoms affect your daily activities and work?"
  9. "Is there anything happening in your personal life that is stressing you?"

3C. Biological Functions (Neurovegetative Symptoms)

Sleep:

  1. "How is your sleep? What time do you go to bed and wake up?"
  2. "Do you have difficulty falling asleep (initial insomnia)? How long does it take?"
  3. "Do you wake up in the middle of the night (middle insomnia)? Can you go back to sleep?"
  4. "Do you wake up very early in the morning and can't sleep again (terminal/early morning insomnia)?"
  5. "Do you feel rested when you wake up, or still tired?"
  6. "Are you sleeping much more than usual (hypersomnia)?"
  7. "Do you have nightmares, night terrors, or disturbing dreams?"
  8. "Do you walk, talk, or do things in your sleep?"

Appetite & Weight:

  1. "How is your appetite? Has it changed recently?"
  2. "Are you eating more or less than usual?"
  3. "Do you still enjoy the taste of food?"
  4. "Have you lost or gained weight recently? How much, over what period?"
  5. "Do you have to force yourself to eat?"
  6. "Do you binge eat or eat in secret? Do you make yourself vomit after eating?"

Sexual Function:

  1. "Has there been any change in your interest in sex (libido)?"
  2. "Are you able to perform sexually as before?" (ask sensitively)
  3. "Is this change causing problems in your relationship?"

Energy & Motivation:

  1. "How is your energy level compared to before?"
  2. "Do you feel tired all the time even without doing much?"
  3. "Do you find it hard to start tasks or get out of bed?"
  4. "Have you stopped doing things you used to do — bathing, cooking, going to work?"
  5. "Do you feel like everything requires too much effort?"

3D. Suicidal Assessment — CRITICAL (Ask Every Patient)

IMPORTANT: Asking about suicide does NOT plant the idea. It shows you care. Always ask in a stepwise, empathic manner — from passive to active ideation to plan to attempt.
  1. "With everything you've been going through, have you ever felt that life is not worth living?"
  2. "Have you wished you could just go to sleep and not wake up?"
  3. "Have you had thoughts of ending your life or harming yourself?"
  4. "How often do these thoughts come? How long do they last?"
  5. "Have you thought about HOW you would do it? Do you have a plan?"
  6. "Have you done anything to prepare — writing notes, giving away belongings, collecting pills/rope?"
  7. "Have you ever attempted to harm yourself or end your life in the past? What happened? How were you saved?"
  8. "What stopped you from acting on these thoughts? (protective factors — family, religion, fear, children)"
  9. "Right now, on a scale of 1-10, how strong is the urge to harm yourself?"
  10. "Do you feel safe right now? Can you promise me you will reach out if the urge becomes strong?"
  11. "Have you had thoughts of harming anyone else?"

3E. Course & Progression

  1. "Since the problem started, has it been getting worse, better, or staying the same?"
  2. "Has it been continuous, or are there periods when you feel better (episodes)?"
  3. "If episodic — how long does each episode last? How many episodes have you had? What happens between episodes?"
  4. "Have any new symptoms developed over time?"
  5. "How has the illness affected your work, studies, relationships, and daily functioning?"
  6. "What is the worst point you have reached during this illness?"

3F. Treatment History

  1. "Have you seen any doctor or healer for this problem before? Who? When?"
  2. "Were you given any medications? Do you remember the names?"
  3. "Did the medications help? How much? Any side effects?"
  4. "Were you taking them regularly, or did you stop? Why did you stop?"
  5. "Have you ever been admitted to a hospital for this problem?"
  6. "Have you tried any alternative treatments — ayurveda, homeopathy, faith healing, jhaad-phoonk, temple/dargah visits?"
  7. "Have you ever received ECT (shock therapy)? How many sessions? Did it help?"
  8. "Have you had any psychotherapy/counselling before? What type? Did it help?"

4. Past Psychiatric History

  1. "Have you ever had a similar episode in the past?"
  2. "At what age did the first episode occur?"
  3. "How many episodes have you had in total?"
  4. "What was the diagnosis given each time?"
  5. "How long did each episode last?"
  6. "What treatment was given? Did you recover fully between episodes?"
  7. "Were there periods of completely normal functioning between episodes?"
  8. "Were there ever episodes of the OPPOSITE kind — for example, if you had depression, were there periods of extreme happiness/overactivity?"

5. Past Medical & Surgical History

  1. "Do you have any medical illnesses — diabetes, blood pressure, thyroid problems, epilepsy/seizures, asthma, heart disease?"
  2. "Have you ever had a head injury, even a mild one? Were you unconscious? For how long?"
  3. "Have you ever had seizures/fits/convulsions?"
  4. "Have you had any surgeries or been hospitalized for any physical illness?"
  5. "Are you currently taking any medications for physical health? What are they?"
  6. "Do you have any known allergies to medications?"
  7. "Have you ever been diagnosed with HIV, hepatitis, tuberculosis, or any infections?"
  8. "For women: Any pregnancy-related complications? Post-partum symptoms?"

6. Family History

Understanding the family helps with genetic risk, psychosocial stressors, and support systems.

6A. Family Structure & Dynamics

  1. "Tell me about your family — who all live with you?"
  2. "How many siblings do you have? What is your birth order?"
  3. "Are your parents alive? What are their ages and health status?"
  4. "What do your family members do (occupation)?"
  5. "How is the overall atmosphere at home — peaceful, tense, conflictual?"
  6. "Who are you closest to in the family? Who do you go to when you're upset?"
  7. "Are there any ongoing conflicts in the family — between parents, siblings, in-laws?"
  8. "How does your family react to your illness? Are they supportive, dismissive, blaming, or overprotective?"
  9. "Has your illness put any financial burden on the family?"
  10. "Is there domestic violence in the family — physical, verbal, or emotional?"

6B. Family Psychiatric & Medical History

  1. "Has anyone in your family (parents, siblings, grandparents, uncles/aunts, cousins) ever had mental health problems?"
  2. "Has anyone in the family been admitted to a psychiatric hospital?"
  3. "Has anyone taken psychiatric medications?"
  4. "Has anyone in the family attempted or died by suicide?"
  5. "Is there a history of alcohol or drug problems in the family?"
  6. "Has anyone in the family had epilepsy, intellectual disability, or neurological illness?"
  7. "Are the parents blood-related (consanguineous marriage)?"

7. Personal History — Building the Life Narrative

This tells you who the patient was BEFORE the illness. It helps understand vulnerabilities, strengths, and context.

7A. Birth & Early Development

  1. "Were you born in a hospital or at home? Normal delivery or any complications?"
  2. "Do your parents mention any problems during pregnancy — illness, falls, medications, stress?"
  3. "Were you born on time, premature, or late?"
  4. "Was there any difficulty at birth — delayed cry, blue baby, cord around neck, low birth weight?"
  5. "Were developmental milestones reached on time — when did you first sit, stand, walk, talk?"
  6. "Were there any childhood illnesses — high fevers, seizures, head injuries?"
  7. "Were you breastfed or bottle-fed? Were there feeding difficulties?"

7B. Childhood & Adolescence

  1. "How was your childhood overall — happy, difficult, lonely?"
  2. "How was the relationship with your mother? Father? Were they warm, strict, absent, abusive?"
  3. "Did you have childhood problems like bedwetting, thumb sucking, head banging, sleep walking, night terrors, fear of dark, stammering?"
  4. "Were you a shy child or outgoing? Did you make friends easily?"
  5. "Were you ever bullied? Did you bully others?"
  6. "Were there any traumatic experiences in childhood — physical abuse, sexual abuse, emotional neglect, witnessing violence, death of a loved one, separation from parents?"
  7. "At what age did you first learn about sex? Was it age-appropriate or traumatic?"
  8. "At what age did puberty begin? How did you feel about the body changes?"
  9. "Were there any conduct problems — lying, stealing, truancy, fire-setting, cruelty to animals, running away from home?"

7C. Educational History

  1. "Up to what class/level have you studied?"
  2. "How were you as a student — good, average, or struggling?"
  3. "Did you ever fail a class? How did that affect you?"
  4. "Did you ever refuse to go to school? Why?"
  5. "Were you punished at school? How?"
  6. "Did you participate in sports, arts, or other activities?"
  7. "How were your relationships with classmates and teachers?"
  8. "Was there any learning difficulty — reading, writing, mathematics?"
  9. "Why did you stop studying (if discontinued)? Was it your choice, financial, or due to illness?"

7D. Occupational History

  1. "What work do you do currently? How long have you been doing it?"
  2. "How many jobs have you had? Why did you leave previous jobs?"
  3. "Are you satisfied with your work? Is there stress at work?"
  4. "How are your relationships with colleagues and bosses?"
  5. "Has your illness affected your work performance or attendance?"
  6. "Have you been fired, demoted, or had conflicts at work?"
  7. "Are there financial difficulties because of job loss or reduced income?"
  8. "If unemployed — how long? What is the reason? How does it make you feel?"

7E. Sexual & Marital History

Approach sensitively: "I need to ask some personal questions about your relationships and sexual life. This helps me understand your overall well-being. You can skip any question you're not comfortable with."

Sexual History:

  1. "When did you first become aware of sexual feelings?"
  2. "Are you attracted to the same sex, opposite sex, or both?"
  3. "Have you had sexual relationships? At what age did they begin?"
  4. "Are you satisfied with your sexual life currently?"
  5. "Are there any difficulties — low desire, difficulty with arousal, premature ejaculation, pain during intercourse, inability to reach orgasm?"
  6. "Were there any unwanted sexual experiences — harassment, abuse, or assault at any age?"
  7. "Do you have any concerns or guilt about your sexuality?"

Marital History:

  1. "Are you married? At what age? Was it arranged or love marriage?"
  2. "How is your relationship with your spouse — supportive, conflictual, distant?"
  3. "Do you communicate openly with each other?"
  4. "Are there frequent arguments? About what? Does it ever become physical?"
  5. "Is your spouse supportive of your treatment?"
  6. "Have there been separations or divorce proceedings?"
  7. "Do you have children? How many? Their ages and health?"
  8. "How is your relationship with your children?"
  9. "If extramarital affairs — do you want to talk about them?"

7F. Menstrual History (for females)

  1. "At what age did your periods start?"
  2. "Are they regular? What is the cycle length and duration?"
  3. "When was your last menstrual period (LMP)?"
  4. "Do you experience mood changes around your periods — irritability, sadness, bloating (premenstrual symptoms)?"
  5. "Have you reached menopause? At what age? Any symptoms?"
  6. "How many pregnancies? Any miscarriages, abortions, or stillbirths?"
  7. "Did you experience depression or psychosis after any delivery (postpartum)?"

7G. Substance Use History — Detailed Exploration

Approach: "Many people use substances to cope. I'd like to ask about your use of alcohol, tobacco, and other substances — there's no judgement here."

For EACH substance (Alcohol, Tobacco, Cannabis, Opioids, Sedatives, Inhalants, etc.):

  1. "Do you use ___? Since when? At what age did you first try it?"
  2. "How often do you use it — daily, weekly, occasionally, in binges?"
  3. "How much do you consume in a typical day/session?"
  4. "Has the amount increased over time (tolerance)?"
  5. "Do you get withdrawal symptoms when you stop or reduce — tremors, sweating, anxiety, seizures, body aches, inability to sleep?"
  6. "Have you tried to quit? How many times? What happened?"
  7. "Have you ever needed medical help to stop (detox)?"
  8. "Why do you use it — stress relief, peer pressure, to feel good, to avoid withdrawal, to sleep?"
  9. "Has the substance caused problems in your life — health, family, work, financial, legal?"
  10. "Do you use it alone or with company?"
  11. "When did you last use it?"
  12. "Do you feel you have a problem with the substance, or do you feel it's under control?"
  13. "Has anyone in your life expressed concern about your substance use?"
  14. "Have you ever done things under the influence that you regret — violence, accidents, risky sex?"

7H. Forensic / Legal History

  1. "Have you ever been in trouble with the law — arrested, charged with a crime, imprisoned?"
  2. "Were any offences committed under the influence of substances or during an episode of illness?"
  3. "Have you been violent towards others? In what context?"
  4. "Are there any ongoing legal cases?"
  5. "Has your illness ever led to involuntary admission or restraint?"

8. Premorbid Personality — Who Was the Patient Before Illness?

Ask about the patient's personality AT LEAST 2 years before illness onset. Best confirmed with informant.

8A. Social Relationships & Temperament

  1. "Before you became unwell, what kind of person were you?"
  2. "Were you an outgoing person who enjoyed socializing, or did you prefer being alone?"
  3. "How many close friends did you have? Could you trust people easily?"
  4. "Were you generally cheerful, serious, moody, or anxious by nature?"
  5. "How did you handle stress or difficult situations — talk it out, bottle it up, get angry, avoid?"
  6. "Were you generally optimistic or pessimistic about life?"
  7. "Were you short-tempered or patient? How did you express anger?"
  8. "Were you a sensitive person? Did you take things personally?"

8B. Interests, Habits & Moral Standards

  1. "What were your hobbies and interests?"
  2. "Were you religious or spiritual? How important was it to you?"
  3. "What were your values — honesty, discipline, ambition?"
  4. "Were you a perfectionist or easygoing?"
  5. "Were you rigid about rules and routines, or flexible?"
  6. "Were you suspicious of others, or generally trusting?"
  7. "How did you see yourself — confident, insecure, inferior, superior?"
  8. "Did you have any unusual habits or superstitions?"
  9. "How did you react to changes or unexpected events?"

8C. Fantasy Life & Coping

  1. "Did you tend to daydream a lot?"
  2. "What kind of future did you imagine for yourself?"
  3. "When stressed, what did you typically do to cope — exercise, pray, drink, talk, withdraw?"
  4. "Were you someone who planned ahead, or lived in the moment?"
  5. "How did you deal with loss or failure in the past?"

9. Mental Status Examination (MSE) — Observation-Based Questions

MSE is primarily observational, but some aspects require direct questioning:

9A. Orientation & Consciousness

  1. "Can you tell me what today's date is? Day? Month? Year?"
  2. "What time of day is it approximately?"
  3. "Where are we right now? What is this place? Which city/state?"
  4. "What is your full name? Can you tell me who I am?"
  5. "Who is the person who brought you here?"

9B. Attention & Concentration

  1. "I will say some numbers — please repeat them after me." (Digit span: start with 3 digits, go up to 7)
  2. "Now repeat them in reverse order."
  3. "Can you spell the word 'WORLD' backwards?"
  4. "Starting from 100, keep subtracting 7." (Serial 7s: 93, 86, 79...)
  5. "Name the days of the week in reverse order."
  6. "Name the months of the year in reverse order."

9C. Memory

Immediate Memory:

  1. "I will say 3 words — Apple, Table, Coin. Please repeat them now."
  2. "I will ask you to recall them in 5 minutes."

Recent Memory:

  1. "What did you eat for breakfast/lunch today?"
  2. "How did you reach the hospital today? Who brought you?"
  3. "What did you do yesterday?"
  4. "Can you recall the 3 words I told you earlier?" (after 5 min)

Remote Memory:

  1. "Where were you born? Where did you grow up?"
  2. "What school did you attend? Name your teachers/friends."
  3. "When did you get married? Name your children and their ages."
  4. "Who is the current Prime Minister/President? Who was before that?"

9D. Intelligence & Abstract Thinking

General Knowledge:

  1. "How many days are there in a week? Months in a year?"
  2. "Name the capital of our country."
  3. "Who is the current leader of the country?"
  4. "What is the distance between [two major cities]?"

Calculations:

  1. "If you buy something for ₹65 and give ₹100, how much change will you get?"
  2. "If 1 kg rice costs ₹40, how much will 3.5 kg cost?"

Abstract Thinking (Proverbs):

  1. "What does this mean: 'A stitch in time saves nine'?"
  2. "What does this mean: 'Don't cry over spilt milk'?"
  3. "What does this mean: 'People in glass houses should not throw stones'?"

Similarities & Differences:

  1. "How are a table and a chair similar?"
  2. "How are a river and a canal different?"
  3. "How are an apple and an orange similar?"

9E. Judgement

  1. "If you found a stamped, addressed, sealed envelope on the road, what would you do?"
  2. "If you noticed fire/smoke in a crowded theatre, what would you do?"
  3. "What would you do if a stranger approached you asking for money?"
  4. "How are you planning to manage your life after discharge from here?"
  5. "Do you think you need treatment? Are you willing to take medications?"

9F. Insight

  1. "Do you think you are unwell? What do you think is wrong?"
  2. "Do you think the problems you described are due to a mental illness?"
  3. "Do you think you need treatment?"
  4. "Why do you think your family brought you here?"
  5. "If a doctor prescribes medication, will you take it regularly?"
  6. "Do you think the experiences you described (voices, beliefs) could be symptoms of an illness?"

10. Closing the Interview

  1. "Is there anything important that I haven't asked about, that you'd like to tell me?"
  2. "Is there anything you wanted to ask me?"
  3. "How are you feeling now after this conversation?"
  4. "Do you have any concerns about your treatment or stay here?"
  5. "I want to thank you for being so open with me. This helps me understand your situation better and help you."
Final Tip: Always end on a supportive note. Summarize what you've understood. Give a brief reassurance that you will work together towards recovery. Schedule the next meeting. Ask if they need anything immediately.

Bonus: Questions for the Informant (Family Member)

Always interview the informant separately when possible. They often provide crucial information the patient may not share or be aware of.

  1. "What changes have YOU noticed in the patient?"
  2. "When did you first notice something was wrong?"
  3. "How was the patient before this illness — their personality, habits, relationships?"
  4. "Is the patient's account accurate, or are there things the patient doesn't recognize or denies?"
  5. "How is the patient functioning at home — self-care, eating, sleeping, interacting with others?"
  6. "Has the patient been violent, aggressive, or threatened anyone?"
  7. "Has the patient talked about death or suicide?"
  8. "Has the patient been talking to themselves, laughing without reason, staring blankly?"
  9. "Is the patient taking medications as prescribed?"
  10. "How has the illness affected the family — financially, emotionally, socially?"
  11. "What are your expectations from this treatment?"
  12. "Are you able to take care of the patient at home? Do you need support?"
  13. "Is there anything you are afraid to mention in front of the patient?"

Quick Reference: Red Flags to Never Miss

  • Suicidal ideation or plan — always assess safety
  • Homicidal ideation — duty to warn/protect
  • Command hallucinations — especially to harm self/others
  • Acute confusion/disorientation — rule out delirium (organic cause)
  • Recent head injury — may indicate organic pathology
  • Substance withdrawal — can be life-threatening (alcohol, benzodiazepines)
  • First episode psychosis in elderly — rule out organic cause
  • Refusal to eat/drink — risk of dehydration, especially in catatonia/severe depression
  • Postpartum onset — higher risk of harm to self and baby
  • Child abuse or neglect — mandatory reporting in most jurisdictions
