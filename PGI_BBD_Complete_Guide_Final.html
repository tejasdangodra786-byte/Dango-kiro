Scoring Formula:
Forward Score = Number of digits in the LONGEST series correctly repeated forward
Backward Score = Number of digits in the LONGEST series correctly repeated backward

Pass criterion per trial: ALL digits must be in correct sequence. Even ONE digit wrong = fail that trial.
Pass criterion per level: Pass at least 1 out of 2 trials = move to next level.
Discontinue: BOTH trials at a level failed → Stop. Score = previous level's digit count.
Subject's performance (Forward):
Level 1 (3 digits): Trial 1 ✓ Trial 2 ✓ → Pass, move on
Level 2 (4 digits): Trial 1 ✓ Trial 2 ✗ → Pass (1 correct is enough), move on
Level 3 (5 digits): Trial 1 ✓ Trial 2 ✗ → Pass, move on
Level 4 (6 digits): Trial 1 ✗ Trial 2 ✓ → Pass, move on
Level 5 (7 digits): Trial 1 ✗ Trial 2 ✗ → STOP
Forward Score = 6 (longest level passed)

Subject's performance (Backward):
Level 1 (2 digits): Pass → Level 2 (3 digits): Pass → Level 3 (4 digits): Pass → Level 4 (5 digits): Both fail → STOP
Backward Score = 4

SUBTEST 4: COMPREHENSION

Basic Parameters

  • Number of Items: 14 questions
  • Scoring: 0, 1, or 2 points per item (based on quality)
  • Maximum Score: 14 × 2 = 28
  • Discontinue: After 4 consecutive 0 scores
  • Time Limit: No formal time limit (but don't wait beyond 2 minutes)
  • Query: If answer is vague, say "Aur batayein" (Tell me more) ONCE

Sample Items with 2-1-0 Scoring Criteria:

Item: "Agar aapko sadak par ek stamp laga hua khat mila toh aap kya karenge?"
(What would you do if you found a stamped, addressed letter on the road?)

2 points: "Post it / Put it in a mailbox / Take it to the post office" (shows social responsibility AND practical solution)
1 point: "Return it to the person" / "Give it to someone" (partial solution; vague but shows intent)
0 points: "Open it and read it" / "Keep it" / "Throw it" / "Don't know" (no social judgment)
Item: "Kapde kyun dhoye jaate hain?" (Why do we wash clothes?)

2 points: "To remove dirt/germs AND to maintain hygiene/look clean" (two concepts: cleanliness + hygiene/appearance)
1 point: "To make them clean" / "Because they get dirty" (one concept only)
0 points: "Because mother says so" / "I don't know" (no understanding)
Item: "Vaada kyun nibhaana chahiye?" (Why should promises be kept?)

2 points: "To maintain trust" AND/OR "Society cannot function without keeping word" / "Moral obligation + practical consequence" (abstract reasoning + social understanding)
1 point: "Because it's wrong to break them" / "People won't trust you" (one reason, correct but limited)
0 points: "I don't know" / "Because God punishes" (superstitious/no reasoning)
Proverb Item: "'Lohe ko garam hai tab tak thoknaa' — iska matlab kya hai?"
(What does "strike while the iron is hot" mean?)

2 points: "Take advantage of opportunity when it arises" / "Act at the right time before it's too late" (abstract generalization)
1 point: "Don't waste time" / "Do things quickly" (correct direction but not precise)
0 points: "Iron is easier to bend when hot" (literal, concrete interpretation)
2 points = Response shows good judgment, abstract thinking, and considers multiple aspects.
1 point = Response is correct but limited, concrete, or addresses only one aspect.
0 points = Response is incorrect, irrelevant, or shows no understanding.

Query rule: If response is vague (could be 1 or 2), ask "Aur batayein?" (Tell me more) ONCE. Score based on total response (original + elaboration).

VIQ = (TQInformation + TQDigit Span + TQArithmetic + TQComprehension) ÷ 4
1

2

3

TEST 4: NAHOR-BENSON TEST — Scoring Procedure

Step-by-Step Scoring

1
Give subject the test sheet with symbol-digit key at top
2
Demonstrate first 3-5 items as practice
3
Say "Shuru karo" and start stopwatch. Time limit = 90 seconds (or 120 sec per version)
4
Say "Ruko" at time limit. Mark where subject stopped.
5
Count Total Items Attempted (how far did they get?)
6
Count Errors (wrong symbols, skipped items, incomplete symbols)
7
Net Score = Total Attempted − Total Errors
8
Compare Net Score to age-education norms → Assign DRS (0, 2, or 3)
What counts as an Error:
• Wrong symbol drawn for that digit = 1 error
• Skipped item (left blank) = 1 error
• Symbol so poorly drawn it's unidentifiable = 1 error
• Self-corrections: If corrected properly, do NOT count as error

1
Collect all 9 reproductions from the subject
2

3

Section 1: Introduction to PGI-BBD

1.1 What is PGI-BBD?

The PGI Battery of Brain Dysfunction (PGI-BBD) is a comprehensive neuropsychological test battery developed by Dr. Dwarka Pershad and Dr. N.N. Wig at the Post Graduate Institute of Medical Education and Research (PGIMER), Chandigarh, India in 1989.

It is the most widely used neuropsychological battery in Indian clinical settings for detecting cognitive dysfunction associated with brain damage/disease.

(PGI-BBD ek neuropsychological test battery hai jo India mein sabse zyada use hoti hai brain damage ya brain dysfunction detect karne ke liye. Yeh PGIMER Chandigarh mein develop hui thi. Isme intelligence, memory, aur visuo-motor functioning — teenon assess hoti hain)

1.2 Purpose of PGI-BBD

  • Screening for brain dysfunction — Identifying presence or absence of organic brain impairment
  • Differentiating organic from functional disorders — Is the cognitive impairment due to brain damage or psychiatric illness?
  • Assessing severity — Quantifying the degree of cognitive impairment
  • Lateralization — Suggesting which hemisphere may be more affected
  • Baseline and follow-up — Monitoring cognitive changes over time (post-surgery, rehabilitation, treatment)
  • Research tool — Finding differential cognitive loss across clinical populations

1.3 Indian Clinical Relevance

PGI-BBD was specifically developed for Indian populations. Its norms are based on Indian adults (age 20-59), it can be used regardless of educational level, and the content is culturally appropriate. This makes it superior to Western batteries (like Halstead-Reitan or Luria-Nebraska) for use in Indian clinical settings where literacy levels, cultural contexts, and languages differ significantly from Western norms.

Section 1: Introduction to PGI-BBD

1.1 What is PGI-BBD?

The PGI Battery of Brain Dysfunction (PGI-BBD) is a comprehensive neuropsychological test battery developed by Dr. Dwarka Pershad and Dr. N.N. Wig at the Post Graduate Institute of Medical Education and Research (PGIMER), Chandigarh, India in 1989.

It is the most widely used neuropsychological battery in Indian clinical settings for detecting cognitive dysfunction associated with brain damage/disease.

(PGI-BBD ek neuropsychological test battery hai jo India mein sabse zyada use hoti hai brain damage ya brain dysfunction detect karne ke liye. Yeh PGIMER Chandigarh mein develop hui thi. Isme intelligence, memory, aur visuo-motor functioning — teenon assess hoti hain)

1.2 Purpose of PGI-BBD

  • Screening for brain dysfunction — Identifying presence or absence of organic brain impairment
  • Differentiating organic from functional disorders — Is the cognitive impairment due to brain damage or psychiatric illness?
  • Assessing severity — Quantifying the degree of cognitive impairment
  • Lateralization — Suggesting which hemisphere may be more affected
  • Baseline and follow-up — Monitoring cognitive changes over time
  • Research tool — Finding differential cognitive loss across clinical populations

1.3 Indian Clinical Relevance

PGI-BBD was specifically developed for Indian populations. Its norms are based on Indian adults (age 20-59), it can be used regardless of educational level, and the content is culturally appropriate. This makes it superior to Western batteries (like Halstead-Reitan or Luria-Nebraska) for Indian clinical settings.

Section 2: Pre-Interpretation Checks

Before interpreting PGI-BBD results, several critical factors must be evaluated that can influence test performance and interpretation accuracy.

(Score dekhne se pehle yeh zaroori hai ki hum jaanein ki patient ki age, education, physical condition, aur cooperation kaisi thi — kyunki yeh sab factors scores ko affect karte hain)

2.1 Age and Education Norms

FactorImpact on ScoresWhat to Do
AgePerformance naturally declines with age, especially on timed tasksUse age-appropriate norms; compare within age band (20-29, 30-39, 40-49, 50-59)
EducationHigher education improves VAIS scores significantly; less impact on performance tasksVAIS scores must be interpreted with education context; Bhatia less affected
IlliteracyCannot do certain subtests (arithmetic, digit span in VAIS may be affected)Rely more heavily on performance tests and non-verbal memory subtests
A low VAIS score in an illiterate subject does NOT indicate brain dysfunction — it reflects educational opportunity. Always interpret verbal scores in the context of educational background.

2.2 Sensory and Motor Deficits

Screen for and document:

  • Visual impairment — Affects all performance tests, BVMGT, Nahor-Benson, visual memory
  • Hearing impairment — Affects all verbal tests, auditory memory subtests
  • Motor impairment (hand) — Affects BVMGT, Nahor-Benson, Block Design, timed tasks
  • Dominant hand affected? — Right-hand impairment may artificially lower performance scores
If the patient has significant sensory/motor deficits, note this in the report: "Performance scores should be interpreted with caution given the documented [visual/motor/auditory] impairment which may have adversely affected performance independent of cognitive dysfunction."

2.3 Effort and Cooperation

LevelDescriptionImpact on Interpretation
GoodEngaged, tries hard, follows instructionsScores likely valid; interpret with confidence
VariableEffort fluctuates; good on some, poor on othersInconsistent profile may reflect effort, not brain dysfunction
PoorMinimal effort, gives up easily, appears unmotivatedScores likely underestimate ability; interpret with extreme caution
Suspect malingeringWorse-than-chance performance, inconsistent with observed functioningResults may be invalid; note in report

2.4 Cultural Considerations

  • Urban vs. rural backgrounds — Rural subjects may have less exposure to test-like situations
  • Test anxiety — First-generation hospital visitors may be highly anxious, affecting timed tasks
  • Language — If testing language differs from native language, verbal scores are compromised
  • Familiarity with materials — Some subjects may never have seen blocks/puzzles before

2.5 Medical and Psychiatric Context

Before interpretation, note:

  • Current medications — Sedatives, antipsychotics, anticonvulsants can affect speed and memory
  • Active psychiatric symptoms — Severe depression slows processing; psychosis disrupts attention
  • Substance use — Recent alcohol/drug use affects performance
  • Sleep deprivation — Significantly affects attention, memory, and processing speed
  • Pain — Chronic pain reduces concentration and motivation

(Yeh sab check karna zaroori hai kyunki agar patient drowsy hai medicines ki wajah se, ya severely depressed hai, toh low scores brain damage ki wajah se nahi hain — yeh confounding factors hain)

3D: Nahor-Benson Test

What it measures: Visuo-motor speed, accuracy, sustained attention, and psychomotor functioning

(Nahor-Benson test ek speed-based test hai jismein patient ko jaldi-jaldi symbols copy karne hote hain. Yeh measure karta hai ki patient ki psychomotor speed kaisi hai — brain damage mein yeh typically slow ho jaata hai)

Test Description:

  • Subject copies symbols/substitutes as quickly and accurately as possible within time limit
  • Similar in principle to Digit-Symbol/Coding from WAIS
  • Scores based on: number completed correctly within time, errors made

Scoring:

  • Speed Score — Number of items completed in given time
  • Accuracy Score — Number of errors made
  • Combined score compared to age-education norms

Interpretation:

FindingClinical Significance
Very slow speed, good accuracyPsychomotor slowing (subcortical, depression, medication effects)
Normal speed, many errorsImpulsivity; poor sustained attention (frontal)
Slow speed AND many errorsSignificant brain dysfunction; diffuse damage
Normal performancePsychomotor functioning intact
Nahor-Benson is particularly sensitive to diffuse brain damage and subcortical pathology. It is one of the first tests to show deficits even in mild brain dysfunction because it requires sustained attention AND motor speed simultaneously.

3E: Bender Visuo-Motor Gestalt Test (BVMGT)

Developed by: Lauretta Bender (1938); within PGI-BBD, scored for brain dysfunction indicators

What it measures: Visuo-motor integration, constructional ability, spatial perception

(Bender Gestalt test mein patient ko 9 geometric figures copy karne hote hain. Jis tarah se woh copy karta hai ussey pata chalta hai ki brain damage hai ya nahi. Rotation, distortion, perseveration — yeh sab organic signs hain)

The 9 Bender Designs:

The subject copies 9 designs (labeled A, 1-8) from stimulus cards onto blank paper.

Scoring Systems Used in PGI-BBD:

The most common scoring approach in the PGI-BBD context is the modified Hutt-Briskin scoring or Lacks adaptation which identifies 12 essential discriminating signs of brain damage:

#Error TypeDescriptionClinical Significance
1RotationDesign rotated 80-180 degrees from originalStrong organic indicator; parietal dysfunction
2Overlapping DifficultyCannot properly reproduce overlapping partsSpatial processing deficit; parietal
3SimplificationDesign drawn in simplified, less complex formCognitive decline; executive dysfunction
4FragmentationDesign broken into disconnected parts; gestalt lostSevere organic; poor integration (right parietal)
5RetrogressionMore primitive, less mature reproductionRegression in constructional ability; organic
6PerseverationRepetition of a design element beyond what is requiredStrong frontal lobe indicator
7Collision/CrowdingDesigns drawn too close together or overlap each otherPlanning deficit; poor spatial organization (frontal)
8ImpotenceRecognizes error but cannot correct it despite repeated attemptsStrong organic indicator; awareness intact but motor execution impaired
9Motor IncoordinationIrregular, tremulous, uncoordinated linesMotor dysfunction; cerebellar or basal ganglia involvement
10Angulation DifficultyCannot reproduce angles correctly; angles changedParietal-occipital dysfunction
11CohesionParts of design drawn with abnormal spacing (too spread or too compressed)Spatial perception deficit
12Closure DifficultyCannot properly close figures (gaps or overlaps at junctions)Motor control and spatial integration deficit

Cutoff for Brain Dysfunction:

5 or more errors (out of 12 essential discriminators) = SUGGESTIVE of brain damage
(Hit rate: approximately 75-85% correct classification)

Key Qualitative Indicators:

  • Perseveration — Most indicative of frontal lobe damage
  • Rotation — Most indicative of parietal damage
  • Fragmentation — Most indicative of severe/diffuse damage
  • Motor tremor throughout — Consider toxic/metabolic encephalopathy, cerebellar, or subcortical

Section 4: Step-by-Step Interpretation Framework

Once all tests are scored, follow this systematic sequence to interpret the PGI-BBD profile.

Global Profile → VIQ vs. PIQ → Memory Analysis → Visuo-Motor Assessment → Integration → Lateralization → Severity

STEP 1: Assess Global Functioning

First, look at the overall profile to determine if brain dysfunction is present.

Dysfunction Rating Score (DRS):

The PGI-BBD manual provides a Dysfunction Rating Score system where scores on each component are rated as:

  • 0 = Average/Normal performance
  • 2 = Mildly impaired performance
  • 3 = Significantly impaired performance

Combined DRS across all tests provides overall brain dysfunction rating.

According to the PGI-BBD manual, the combined rating system can correctly identify approximately two-thirds of organic cases among difficult-to-diagnose patients. Higher combined DRS = greater likelihood of organic brain dysfunction.

Quick Global Check:

Overall PatternInterpretation
All scores within normal limitsNo evidence of brain dysfunction on this battery
One area mildly low, others normalMay be normal variation; or very mild/focal deficit
Multiple areas below expected levelLikely brain dysfunction; proceed to pattern analysis
All areas severely impairedSignificant diffuse brain dysfunction; or severe depression/low premorbid functioning

STEP 2: Analyze Intellectual Functioning (VIQ vs. PIQ)

Compare Verbal IQ (VAIS) with Performance IQ (Bhatia's).

(VIQ aur PIQ ka comparison bahut important hai neuropsychology mein. Agar dono mein bahut zyada difference hai — toh yeh lateralization ka clue deta hai. Left brain damage mein VIQ girta hai, right brain damage mein PIQ girta hai)

VIQ-PIQ Discrepancy Analysis:

PatternDiscrepancyClinical Significance
VIQ = PIQ (within 10 points)No significant discrepancySymmetric functioning; if both low = diffuse damage or pre-existing low ability
VIQ > PIQ (15+ points)Significant Performance deficitRIGHT hemisphere dysfunction suspected; OR non-dominant hemisphere damage
PIQ > VIQ (15+ points)Significant Verbal deficitLEFT hemisphere dysfunction suspected; OR low education effects on verbal tests
VIQ >> PIQ (20+ points)Large Performance deficitStrong right hemisphere indicator; consider right CVA, tumor, trauma
PIQ >> VIQ (20+ points)Large Verbal deficitStrong left hemisphere indicator; OR aphasic component; consider left CVA
A VIQ-PIQ discrepancy of 15 points or more is considered clinically significant. However, ALWAYS consider education level before assuming lateralized damage (low education + low VIQ ≠ left brain damage).
Case: 35-year-old male, graduate education, right-handed. VIQ = 102, PIQ = 78. Discrepancy = 24 points (PIQ significantly lower).

Interpretation: Significant Performance deficit relative to Verbal abilities. Given adequate education and normal VIQ, this suggests right hemisphere dysfunction affecting visuo-spatial processing. Consistent with right-sided brain pathology. Correlate with neuroimaging findings.

STEP 3: Analyze Memory Functioning

Evaluate PGI Memory Scale results within the context of the full battery.

Key Comparisons:

1
MQ vs. IQ Comparison
  • MQ approximately = IQ → Memory commensurate with intelligence (expected)
  • MQ significantly < IQ (10+ points) → Disproportionate memory impairment (organic amnesia likely)
  • MQ > IQ → Unusual; consider poor effort on IQ tests or memory overestimation
2
Memory Profile Analysis
  • Remote memory intact + Recent/Immediate impaired = Anterograde amnesia (new learning deficit)
  • All memory impaired including remote = Severe/global amnesia OR confabulation
  • Attention/Working memory low + Other memory OK = Pseudo-memory deficit (attention causes poor encoding)
3
Verbal vs. Visual Memory
  • Verbal memory (Immediate Recall, Paired Associates) low + Visual memory OK = Left temporal involvement
  • Visual memory (Visual Retention) low + Verbal memory OK = Right temporal involvement
  • Both low = Bilateral temporal/hippocampal involvement
IQ-MQ Discrepancy ≥ 10 points (MQ lower) = Clinically significant memory impairment disproportionate to general intellectual decline

STEP 4: Analyze Visuo-Motor & Performance Functioning

Combine findings from Bhatia's Performance tests, Nahor-Benson Test, and Bender Gestalt.

Integration of Performance Measures:

TestPrimarily AssessesIf Impaired, Suggests
Block Design (Bhatia)Visuo-spatial constructionRight parietal damage; constructional apraxia
Pass-Along (Bhatia)Planning, executive reasoningFrontal lobe involvement
Nahor-BensonPsychomotor speedDiffuse damage; subcortical pathology; processing speed deficit
BVMGTVisuo-motor integrationOrganic brain dysfunction; parietal and/or frontal damage

Constructional Ability Assessment:

If Block Design AND Bender Gestalt both show impairment → Constructional apraxia likely → Right parietal lobe dysfunction

(Agar patient blocks se design nahi bana pa raha aur Bender mein bhi figures properly copy nahi kar pa raha — toh yeh constructional apraxia hai jo right parietal lobe ki problem indicate karta hai)

STEP 5: Analyze Language & Verbal Functions

VAIS subtests provide insight into left hemisphere functioning:

VAIS FindingLeft Hemisphere Interpretation
Information intact but Comprehension lowStored knowledge OK but reasoning/judgment impaired (frontal)
Digit Span very lowWorking memory/phonological loop deficit (left frontal-parietal)
All VAIS subtests uniformly lowGeneralized left hemisphere dysfunction OR diffuse damage
Comprehension very low with other subtests intactSpecific frontal-temporal deficit in social reasoning
Cannot perform VAIS due to aphasiaSignificant left hemisphere damage; document and rely on performance tests
VAIS Information subtest is considered the MOST resistant to brain damage among verbal subtests (because it tests overlearned, crystallized knowledge). If Information is impaired, it suggests either very severe/early-onset damage or pre-morbid low functioning.

Section 5: Pattern Analysis — Organic vs. Functional

One of the most critical clinical questions PGI-BBD helps answer: Is the cognitive impairment organic (brain-based) or functional (psychiatric/psychological)?

(Sabse important clinical sawaal jo PGI-BBD answer karta hai: kya patient ki cognitive problems brain damage ki wajah se hain, ya depression/anxiety/malingering ki wajah se? Pattern analysis se yeh pata chalta hai)

5.3 Diffuse vs. Localized Brain Damage

Diffuse Brain Damage

  • ALL scores uniformly depressed
  • No VIQ-PIQ discrepancy
  • Memory, IQ, visuo-motor ALL impaired
  • Psychomotor slowing prominent
  • Nahor-Benson particularly impaired

Causes: TBI (diffuse axonal injury), toxic encephalopathy, advanced dementia, infections (encephalitis)

Focal/Localized Damage

  • Specific pattern of deficits with some areas intact
  • Significant VIQ-PIQ discrepancy
  • One domain impaired, others preserved
  • Lateralization clues present
  • Specific constructional or language deficits

Causes: Stroke (CVA), tumor, localized trauma, surgical lesion

5.4 Cortical vs. Subcortical Patterns

Key differentiator: In cortical damage, Recognition memory is as impaired as Free Recall (true encoding/storage failure). In subcortical damage, Recognition is significantly better than Recall (retrieval deficit with intact storage). Check Recognition vs. Recall subtests on PGIMS!

Section 6: Lateralization of Brain Dysfunction

PGI-BBD can provide clues about which hemisphere is more affected, though it cannot replace neuroimaging for precise localization.

(Lateralization ka matlab hai yeh pata karna ki left hemisphere zyada damage hua hai ya right hemisphere. PGI-BBD mein verbal vs. performance tests ka comparison yeh clue deta hai)

6.2 Right Hemisphere Indicators

6.3 Bilateral/Diffuse Indicators

  • VIQ and PIQ both low with no significant discrepancy
  • Both verbal and visual memory impaired
  • Psychomotor slowing on Nahor-Benson
  • Multiple BVMGT errors of different types
  • Global decline across all subtests
Lateralization from neuropsychological testing is probabilistic, not definitive. Always correlate with neuroimaging. False lateralization can occur due to: crossed dominance, bilateral damage with one side more affected, premorbid ability differences.

8.4 Psychiatric Conditions (Pseudo-Organic Patterns)

Depression:

  • Psychomotor slowing affects ALL timed tests (Nahor-Benson, Block Design time bonuses)
  • Effort-dependent tasks (free recall) more affected than recognition
  • Improves with treatment (reversible "pseudodementia")
  • No true organic signs on BVMGT (no perseveration, no rotation)
  • May give up easily ("I don't know") rather than try and fail

Schizophrenia:

  • Working memory and attention most affected
  • Executive deficits (frontal pattern)
  • Disorganized approach to Block Design and BVMGT (poor planning, not true constructional apraxia)
  • Variable performance (good on some items, poor on similar difficulty items)

Substance Use Disorders (Alcohol):

  • Memory disproportionately affected (Korsakoff pattern)
  • New learning severely impaired with relatively preserved IQ
  • Visuo-spatial deficits (cerebellar involvement)
  • Executive deficits (frontal lobe vulnerability to alcohol)

8.5 Epilepsy

  • Pattern depends on seizure focus lateralization
  • Left temporal lobe epilepsy → verbal memory deficit
  • Right temporal lobe epilepsy → visual memory deficit
  • Generalized epilepsy → diffuse mild deficits; attention most affected
  • Anti-epileptic drugs may independently cause slowing

Section 9: Integration into a Single Formulation

After analyzing all components individually and looking at patterns, the final step is creating a unified neuropsychological formulation.

(Sab components ko individually analyse karne ke baad, ek single coherent formulation banana padta hai jo poori picture dikhaye — brain dysfunction hai ya nahi, kahan hai, kitni severe hai, aur clinical context mein kya matlab hai)

9.1 Integration Template

Step-by-Step Integration

1
Premorbid Estimate: What was this person's likely cognitive level BEFORE the current problem? (Use education, occupation, Information subtest as estimates)
2
Current Level: What is their current cognitive functioning? (VIQ, PIQ, MQ overall levels)
3
Decline: Is there evidence of decline from estimated premorbid level? (Current < Expected = decline)
4
Pattern: Is the decline diffuse or focal? Lateralized or bilateral?
5
Domain Specificity: Which domains are most affected? (Memory? Visuo-spatial? Verbal? Executive? Psychomotor speed?)
6
Organic vs. Functional: Does the pattern look organic or is it better explained by psychiatric factors?
7
Severity: Mild/Moderate/Severe?
8
Clinical Correlation: Is the pattern consistent with the suspected diagnosis?

9.2 Sample Integrated Formulation

Case: 45-year-old male, B.A. graduate, right-handed, referred for cognitive assessment following right MCA stroke 3 months ago.

Results: VIQ = 98 (Average); PIQ = 72 (Borderline); MQ = 85 (Low Average); BVMGT = 7 errors with rotations and spatial disorganization; Nahor-Benson = mildly slow.

Integrated Formulation:
"Based on the patient's educational background (B.A. graduate) and intact VAIS Information subtest (TQ=105), premorbid intellectual functioning is estimated to be in the Average to High-Average range. Current assessment reveals a significant VIQ-PIQ discrepancy of 26 points, with Performance IQ markedly lower than Verbal IQ. This pattern, combined with constructional errors on BVMGT (rotations, spatial disorganization) and mild Visual Retention deficit on PGIMS, is strongly suggestive of RIGHT hemisphere dysfunction — consistent with the documented right MCA stroke. Memory functioning shows mild overall impairment (MQ=85) with visual memory relatively more affected than verbal memory. Psychomotor speed is mildly reduced. Overall, findings indicate moderate brain dysfunction lateralized to the right hemisphere, primarily affecting visuo-spatial processing, constructional ability, and psychomotor speed, consistent with the clinical history of right MCA infarction."

Section 11: Common Errors in PGI-BBD Interpretation

Error 1: Overgeneralization from Single Low Score

Concluding "brain damage" from one low subtest score.
  • Mistake: "Block Design is low — therefore right parietal damage"
  • Correction: A single low score can be normal variation. Require convergent evidence from multiple tests pointing to same conclusion.
  • Rule: Never diagnose brain dysfunction from one subtest. Require pattern confirmation.

Error 2: Ignoring IQ-Memory Relationship

Calling low MQ "impaired" without comparing to IQ.
  • Mistake: "MQ = 78, therefore memory impairment present"
  • Correction: If IQ = 75, then MQ = 78 is actually EXPECTED (commensurate). Memory impairment is only significant when MQ is disproportionately lower than IQ.
  • Rule: Always compare MQ to IQ. Only diagnose memory impairment when MQ is significantly lower than what IQ predicts.

Error 3: Ignoring Education Effects on VAIS

Interpreting low VAIS as "left hemisphere damage" without considering education.
  • Mistake: "VAIS is low in this illiterate farmer, must be left brain damage"
  • Correction: VAIS is heavily education-dependent. Low VAIS in low-education individuals reflects opportunity, not brain damage.
  • Rule: For low-education subjects, weigh PIQ, Memory, and BVMGT more than VAIS.

Error 4: Misinterpreting VIQ-PIQ Discrepancy

Assuming all discrepancies indicate lateralized damage.
  • Mistake: "VIQ = 105, PIQ = 92 (13 points difference) = right hemisphere damage"
  • Correction: A 13-point discrepancy is within normal variation. Need 15+ points for clinical significance, and even then consider base rates and education.
  • Rule: Only interpret VIQ-PIQ discrepancies of 15+ points as clinically significant.

Error 5: Confusing Depression with Brain Damage

Calling pseudodementia "organic dysfunction."
  • Mistake: "All scores are low in this depressed patient — brain damage confirmed"
  • Correction: Severe depression can produce global cognitive slowing mimicking brain damage. Key differentiators: no true organic signs on BVMGT, effort-dependent pattern, improvement with treatment.
  • Rule: Always note psychiatric status. If depression is present, recommend re-assessment after mood improvement.

Error 6: Not Considering Medication Effects

Interpreting slowed performance without considering drug effects.
  • Mistake: "Nahor-Benson is very slow — subcortical pathology"
  • Correction: Sedating medications (benzodiazepines, anticonvulsants, antipsychotics) cause psychomotor slowing independent of brain damage.
  • Rule: Document all medications. Note their potential cognitive effects in the report.

Error 7: Equating Normal Scores with "No Brain Damage"

Concluding brain damage is absent because scores are "normal range."
  • Mistake: "IQ = 95, MQ = 92 — no brain dysfunction"
  • Correction: If premorbid IQ was estimated at 120+ (based on education/occupation), then current IQ of 95 represents significant DECLINE even though it's in "normal range."
  • Rule: Always estimate premorbid level. Compare current performance to EXPECTED level, not just to population norms.

Error 8: Over-Relying on BVMGT Alone

Using Bender Gestalt as the sole indicator of brain damage.
  • Mistake: "BVMGT shows 6 errors — brain damage confirmed"
  • Correction: BVMGT has a hit rate of 75-85%. This means 15-25% false positives/negatives. It must be interpreted within the FULL battery context.
  • Rule: BVMGT is one piece of evidence, not a standalone diagnostic tool.

Golden Rules of PGI-BBD Interpretation

  1. Always compare current scores to ESTIMATED PREMORBID level
  2. MQ must always be compared to IQ (not just to norms)
  3. VIQ-PIQ discrepancy must be 15+ points for clinical significance
  4. Consider education, medications, and psychiatric status before concluding organicity
  5. Look for CONVERGENT evidence across multiple tests
  6. BVMGT is sensitive but not specific alone — integrate with full battery
  7. Use probabilistic language in reports
  8. Always recommend re-assessment if confounding factors present

12.1 Frequently Asked Examiner Questions

Q: What are the component tests of PGI-BBD?
A: (1) Verbal Adult Intelligence Scale (VAIS), (2) Bhatia's Battery of Performance Tests / Short Battery, (3) PGI Memory Scale (PGIMS), (4) Nahor-Benson Test, and (5) Bender Visuo-Motor Gestalt Test.
Q: Who developed PGI-BBD and when?
A: Developed by Dr. Dwarka Pershad and Dr. N.N. Wig at PGIMER, Chandigarh in 1989. It is the most widely used neuropsychological battery in Indian clinical settings.
Q: What age range is PGI-BBD standardized for?
A: Primarily 20-59 years. VAIS can be used from 16+ years. Bhatia's norms are for 11-16 years (original) but extended norms are used in the battery for adults.
Q: How does VIQ-PIQ discrepancy help in lateralization?
A: VIQ > PIQ (by 15+ points) suggests right hemisphere dysfunction (non-dominant hemisphere mediates performance/spatial tasks). PIQ > VIQ (by 15+ points) suggests left hemisphere dysfunction (dominant hemisphere mediates verbal tasks). Equal scores suggest bilateral/diffuse damage or no lateralizing features.
Q: What is the most sensitive subtest of PGIMS for detecting organic brain damage?
A: Retention for Dissimilar Pairs (new learning of arbitrary associations). This is the first to decline in brain dysfunction because it requires active hippocampal consolidation of novel information.
Q: How do you differentiate organic memory impairment from depression-related memory problems?
A: Organic: (1) Disproportionate MQ-IQ gap, (2) Rapid forgetting (poor delayed recall even when immediate recall partially done), (3) Recognition also impaired (storage failure), (4) Ribot's gradient present. Depression: (1) MQ-IQ decline proportionally, (2) Recognition much better than recall (retrieval deficit), (3) Improves with encouragement, (4) No Ribot's gradient, (5) Effort-dependent pattern.
Q: What BVMGT signs are most indicative of brain damage?
A: Perseveration (frontal), Rotation (parietal), Fragmentation (severe/diffuse damage), and Impotence (recognizes error but cannot correct). Five or more of the 12 discriminating errors suggests brain damage.
Q: What is the difference between cortical and subcortical pattern on PGI-BBD?
A: Cortical: specific domain deficits, aphasia possible, constructional apraxia, recognition as impaired as recall. Subcortical: processing speed primarily affected (Nahor-Benson very slow), executive deficits, recognition much better than recall, generalized slowing, no true aphasia but dysarthria possible.
Q: Can PGI-BBD be used to diagnose dementia?
A: PGI-BBD can provide evidence of cognitive decline consistent with dementia, but DIAGNOSIS requires: (1) Clinical interview documenting functional decline, (2) Neuropsychological evidence of impairment in multiple domains, (3) Rule-out of other causes (depression, delirium, medications). PGI-BBD provides the neuropsychological evidence component.
Q: What is the Dysfunction Rating Score system?
A: In PGI-BBD, individual test scores are assigned ratings of 0 (average), 2 (mildly impaired), or 3 (significantly impaired). Combined ratings across all tests provide an overall dysfunction score that can correctly classify approximately two-thirds of difficult-to-diagnose organic cases.
