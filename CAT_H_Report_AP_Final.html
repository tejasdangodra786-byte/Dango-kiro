CHILDREN'S APPERCEPTION TEST — HUMAN VERSION (CAT-H)

Psychodiagnostic Report — Bellak Analysis-Sheet Method

Instrument: The Children's Apperception Test — Human Version (CAT-H; Bellak & Bellak, 1965) Interpretive system: Bellak, L. & Abrams, D. M., The T.A.T., the C.A.T., and the S.A.T. in Clinical Use — the Bellak C.A.T. Blank & Analysis Sheet (10-variable scheme; descriptive → interpretive → diagnostic levels) Cards administered: Card 1 to Card 10 Language of production: Hinglish (transcribed verbatim by the examiner) Patient: AP, 16-year-old male adolescent

A NOTE ON METHOD (per the manual)

Bellak instructs the interpreter to read each story on three levels: the descriptive (what literally happens), the interpretive (a generalised conditional clause, usually framed as "If one…"), and the diagnostic (a definite statement about the person). The interpreter then condenses each record onto the Analysis Sheet's ten variables. Throughout, Bellak warns that fantasy material is "prima facie only the fantasy needs of the subject," that the relation of fantasy needs to behaviour must be checked against clinical/biographical data, and that single signs are never diagnostic in isolation — consistency across stories is what carries weight.

The CAT-H employs human figures in the same stimulus situations as the original animal C.A.T., designed for children and adolescents aged 7–10+ and those with higher intellectual functioning who respond more productively to human stimuli. The interpretation framework remains identical.
