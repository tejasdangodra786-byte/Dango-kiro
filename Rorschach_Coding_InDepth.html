Rorschach — In-Depth Coding Guide (Exner Comprehensive System)

For every coding variable: what it is, how it is calculated, what it tells about thinking / perception / personality, and an example response with its meaning. Study notes in original wording; confirm exact cut-offs against the current manual.

0. R — Total Responses

R (Total number of responses)

Definition: the count of all answers the person gives across the 10 cards.

How to calculate: simply tally every scorable response; norm is roughly 17 to 27 in adults.

What it tells: overall productivity, engagement and energy available to the task; it is the denominator for most percentages, so it must always be considered first.

Thinking / perception: a guarded or depressed person constricts output, while an obsessive or manic person may over-produce; many distortions can be hidden in a long record or exaggerated in a short one.

Example: a record of only 13 responses (R<14) is treated as invalid and re-administered, because too few answers cannot reliably represent the personality.

1. LOCATION — where the response was seen

W — Whole response

Definition: the entire blot is used to form the percept.

How to calculate: coded whenever essentially the whole inkblot is incorporated; tallied as a frequency and entered into the W:D:Dd ratio.

What it tells: the drive to organize the whole field, to integrate and to reach for the big picture; it reflects ambition and the wish to make complete sense of things.

Thinking / perception: accurate W answers indicate strong synthetic / integrative capacity, while many W with poor form quality suggest over-reaching, grandiosity, or aiming beyond actual ability.

Example: "the whole thing is a bat" (Card I) is an economical, accurate W; a strained "the entire blot is one giant complicated machine" with minus form shows over-ambitious, inaccurate integration.

D — Common Detail

Definition: a frequently used, obvious portion of the blot is used.

How to calculate: coded when the area chosen is one statistically common in normative data; entered in W:D:Dd.

What it tells: a practical, economical, conventional way of engaging the environment; the person attends to the obvious and manageable.

Thinking / perception: a healthy predominance of D reflects everyday, reality-oriented, efficient processing that most people share.

Example: "two people" on the common D area of Card III is a typical, grounded use of an obvious detail.

Dd — Unusual Detail

Definition: a small, rarely used, or unusual portion of the blot is used.

How to calculate: coded when the area is statistically uncommon; high frequency (e.g., >3) is interpretively notable.

What it tells: an idiosyncratic, peripheral focus; attention drawn to small or odd features rather than the whole or the obvious.

Thinking / perception: elevations suggest unusual scanning, perfectionistic over-attention to minor detail, guardedness, or avoidance of the larger, more demanding stimulus.

Example: picking a tiny edge projection and calling it "a little hook" while ignoring the obvious figure signals a fussy, peripheral, possibly avoidant approach.

S — White Space

Definition: the white background / space is used, alone or combined with a blot area.

How to calculate: coded as S added to the location (e.g., WS, DS, DdS); the count and any S- are noted.

What it tells: oppositionality, negativism, autonomy striving, or anger; a tendency to look at things in a contrary, back-to-front way.

Thinking / perception: a little S = independence / assertiveness; much S = entrenched anger and defiance; S- ties the negativism directly to misperception (anger distorting reality).

Example: seeing "a jet plane" in the white center of Card II uses space adaptively; a record full of space answers points to a chronically oppositional stance.

2. DEVELOPMENTAL QUALITY (DQ) — sophistication of the percept

DQ+ — Synthesized

Definition: two or more objects are seen in a meaningful relationship, and at least one has a specific form demand.

How to calculate: coded on the response when separate form-demanding objects are integrated; counted toward DQ+ frequency.

What it tells: mature, analytic-synthetic processing — the capacity to relate parts into organized, meaningful wholes.

Thinking / perception: higher DQ+ indicates complexity, abstraction and higher-order conceptual organization of experience.

Example: "two people leaning over a pot, lifting it together" (Card III) integrates figures + object in a relationship = sophisticated processing.

DQo — Ordinary

Definition: a single object with a definite form is reported.

How to calculate: coded when one form-demanding object is seen without integration; the most common DQ code.

What it tells: standard, economical, adequate processing — neither sophisticated nor impoverished.

Thinking / perception: a normal predominance of DQo reflects ordinary, workable perception of discrete objects.

Example: "a bat" (Card V) — one clearly formed object = ordinary developmental quality.

DQv/+ — Synthesized but Vague

Definition: objects are related to one another but at least one has no specific form demand.

How to calculate: coded when integration occurs but a component is formless.

What it tells: a mix of effort to integrate with imprecise, diffuse perceptual quality.

Thinking / perception: shows organizing intent that is undercut by vagueness — partial maturity.

Example: "clouds gathering over some hills" relates elements but the clouds carry no firm form = DQv/+.

DQv — Vague

Definition: an object with no specific form demand is reported.

How to calculate: coded when the percept lacks definite shape; counted toward DQv frequency.

What it tells: immature, impressionistic, diffuse processing; a low-effort or low-capacity way of handling the stimulus.

Thinking / perception: elevated DQv is common in young children, in cognitively limited records, and in distressed or constricted states.

Example: "just some smoke" or "a stain" — no form demand = vague developmental quality.

3. DETERMINANTS — what about the blot made it look that way

F — Pure Form

Definition: the response is based only on the shape/outline of the area, with no colour, movement, or shading.

How to calculate: count pure-F responses; they drive Lambda = F / (R - F).

What it tells: reliance on logic, structure and a matter-of-fact, affect-distancing way of dealing with the world; a control/economy device.

Thinking / perception: moderate F = healthy controlled processing; very high F (high Lambda) = avoidant simplification that screens out emotion and complexity; very low F = over-involvement and vulnerability to flooding.

Example: "a bat, just from the shape" is pure form; a record dominated by such answers signals a person who keeps things simple and keeps feelings at arm's length.

M — Human Movement

Definition: human activity, or animal/object carrying out a human-like action, is attributed to the percept.

How to calculate: count M; enter into EB (Sum M : WSumC) and examine its form quality and active/passive coding.

What it tells: deliberate ideation, imagination, reasoning, planning and the capacity to delay and think things through; the richness of inner mental life.

Thinking / perception: good-form M = sound, flexible reasoning; M- = thinking that loses touch with reality; many passive M = retreat into fantasy; M with no form = serious cognitive slippage.

Example: "two people lifting a basket" (Card III) is well-formed M (healthy ideation); "a man whose body is melting into the sky" with poor form is M- (distorted thinking).

FM — Animal Movement

Definition: an animal is seen in a natural, species-appropriate movement.

How to calculate: count FM; enter into the left side of eb (FM + m).

What it tells: instinctual, need-driven ideation — the "background chatter" of urges, appetites and unmet needs pressing on the mind.

Thinking / perception: ordinary FM = needs felt and managed normally; elevated FM = need states intruding on attention/concentration and disrupting goal-directed thought, often experienced as distractibility.

Example: "a bear climbing a rock" (Card VIII) is typical FM; a flood of such answers suggests a mind repeatedly pulled off-task by inner urges.

m — Inanimate Movement

Definition: movement of inanimate, non-living forces or objects is reported.

How to calculate: count m; enters eb and es, but excess m is removed when computing Adj es (treated as situational).

What it tells: situational stress, tension and a sense of being acted upon by forces outside one's control.

Thinking / perception: elevated m = current, often transient, pressure and helplessness that can intrude on concentration and lower momentary control.

Example: "an explosion blowing apart" or "smoke being blown by wind" = m; a spike suggests the person currently feels under external pressure.

a / p — Active vs Passive Movement

Definition: every movement (M, FM, m) is also coded as active (a) or passive (p).

How to calculate: tally to form a:p (all movement) and Ma:Mp (human movement only).

What it tells: a:p reflects flexibility of attitudes/ideas; Ma:Mp reflects whether thinking is action-oriented or escapist.

Thinking / perception: a markedly one-sided a:p = rigid, fixed values hard to change; Mp dominant = a "Snow White" pattern of passive daydreaming and waiting rather than acting.

Example: "a person resting / lying back" (passive) vs "a person running / fighting" (active); many passive answers point to avoidance of active problem-solving.

FC — Form-Colour

Definition: chromatic colour is used but form is the dominant element of the response.

How to calculate: count FC; weighted 0.5 in WSumC; enters FC:CF+C.

What it tells: modulated, controlled emotional expression — feelings released in a socially appropriate, form-bound way.

Thinking / perception: a healthy predominance of FC indicates mature affect regulation, emotion guided by reason and context.

Example: "a pink flower with clearly shaped petals" (Card X) uses colour within firm form = well-controlled emotion.

CF — Colour-Form

Definition: chromatic colour is the dominant element and form is secondary.

How to calculate: count CF; weighted 1.0 in WSumC; enters FC:CF+C.

What it tells: less-modulated, more reactive emotionality — feelings expressed more freely and with less restraint.

Thinking / perception: some CF is normal spontaneity; a CF/C predominance over FC signals emotion that frequently outruns control.

Example: "blood, just a big red splash with a vague edge" emphasizes colour over shape = under-modulated affect.

C — Pure Colour

Definition: the response is based on colour alone, with no form.

How to calculate: count Pure C; weighted 1.5 in WSumC; a key affect-control marker.

What it tells: raw, unmodulated emotional discharge that bypasses cognitive control — the affective equivalent of impulsivity.

Thinking / perception: one or more Pure C, especially with weak controls, indicates a proneness to emotional outbursts or being flooded by feeling.

Example: "it's just red — fire" or "all this colour is blood" with no shape = raw emotional discharge.

Cn — Colour Naming

Definition: the person names the colours as the response itself ("that's red, that's blue").

How to calculate: rare; counted within the colour determinants.

What it tells: a severe failure to integrate affect into any meaningful percept.

Thinking / perception: typically associated with serious disturbance, organicity, or marked regression.

Example: on Card X, "red, yellow, green" given as the answer rather than seeing an object = colour naming.

FC', C'F, C' — Achromatic Colour

Definition: the black, grey or white is used as colour (not as shape), e.g., "black like coal."

How to calculate: count toward SumC'; enters the right side of eb and the SumC':WSumC constriction ratio.

What it tells: emotion that is suppressed and held inside rather than expressed — painful internalization of feeling.

Thinking / perception: SumC' > 2 signals notable constriction that breeds tension, dysphoria or low mood, since affect is bottled up.

Example: "a dark, black bat — it looks gloomy and heavy" uses the blackness as mood = internalized, dampened affect.

FT, TF, T — Texture (Shading)

Definition: shading is interpreted as a tactile quality (furry, soft, rough, smooth).

How to calculate: count toward SumT; enters eb and the interpersonal cluster.

What it tells: needs for emotional closeness, attachment and contact with others.

Thinking / perception: SumT = 1 is expected/healthy; T = 0 = guardedness, emotional distance, denial of need for closeness; T > 1 = strong unmet loneliness / longing for contact.

Example: "a furry animal skin, looks soft to touch" (Card VI) = texture; absence of any texture across the record suggests a person who keeps others at a distance.

FV, VF, V — Vista (Shading)

Definition: shading is used to convey depth, distance or dimensionality ("looking down into it").

How to calculate: count toward SumV; loads on the Suicide Constellation and self-perception cluster.

What it tells: painful self-examination — looking inward and finding fault; guilt, shame, self-critical rumination.

Thinking / perception: any V is noteworthy; it marks introspection that hurts rather than helps, often tied to depressive self-evaluation.

Example: "a deep canyon seen from above, dropping far down" = vista; in context it reflects distressing self-scrutiny.

FY, YF, Y — Diffuse Shading

Definition: shading is seen as haze, smoke, X-ray, or light/dark gradation without texture or depth.

How to calculate: count toward SumY; excess Y is removed for Adj es (situational).

What it tells: anxiety, apprehension and a sense of helplessness — distress that is felt but not easily acted upon.

Thinking / perception: elevated Y indicates free-floating anxiety and a passive, paralysed quality under stress.

Example: "hazy smoke, you can't see clearly through it" = diffuse shading, reflecting anxious uncertainty.

FD — Form Dimension

Definition: depth or dimensionality is conveyed by the shape/contour (not shading), e.g., perspective.

How to calculate: count FD; used in self-perception and the Suicide Constellation.

What it tells: the capacity for self-inspection and introspective awareness — stepping back to view oneself.

Thinking / perception: one or two FD is adaptive (healthy reflection); absence suggests little capacity for self-examination; excess can reflect uncomfortable self-focus.

Example: "a person seen from far below, so they look towering" conveys dimension by form = introspective awareness.

(2) — Pair

Definition: two identical objects are reported based on the blot's symmetry.

How to calculate: count pairs; feeds the Egocentricity Index [3(Fr+rF) + Sum(2)] / R.

What it tells: a component of self-focus / self-attention.

Thinking / perception: high pair counts contribute to elevated egocentricity (heightened self-preoccupation).

Example: "two identical bears, one on each side" (Card VIII) is a pair; many pairs raise the self-focus index.

Fr, rF — Reflections

Definition: part of the blot is seen as a mirror image / reflection because of symmetry.

How to calculate: count Fr + rF; weighted heavily (x3) in the Egocentricity Index.

What it tells: narcissistic self-focus, inflated or defensive self-regard, and a sense of entitlement.

Thinking / perception: any reflection is significant; it points to an externalizing, self-admiring stance that resists self-criticism.

Example: "a bear looking at its own reflection in the water" = reflection, reflecting an over-valued self-image.

Blends

Definition: a single response containing two or more determinants (e.g., M.CF).

How to calculate: count blends; examine Blends:R for proportion of complex responses.

What it tells: psychological complexity — the richness and layeredness of inner experience.

Thinking / perception: too few blends = psychologically simple / constricted; very many = complex and possibly overwhelmed; the specific mix reveals how thought and feeling combine.

Example: "two people dancing joyfully in red costumes" (M + FC) is a blend integrating ideation and modulated affect.

Colour-Shading Blend

Definition: a blend combining chromatic colour with shading or achromatic colour.

How to calculate: flagged among blends; loads on the Suicide Constellation.

What it tells: ambivalent, confused or painful emotion — pleasant and unpleasant feelings fused together.

Thinking / perception: marks emotional experiences that are hard to resolve, often felt as bittersweet, conflicted, or distressingly mixed.

Example: "a bright sunset, but it looks sad and hazy" combines colour with diffuse shading = ambivalent affect.

Shading Blend

Definition: a blend containing more than one type of shading determinant.

How to calculate: noted among blends.

What it tells: intense, complex and painful distress with several negative affects at once.

Thinking / perception: suggests a person simultaneously contending with anxiety, loneliness and/or self-criticism.

Example: "a cold, misty, deep cave that feels lonely" mixes diffuse shading, vista and texture-like distress.

4. FORM QUALITY (FQ) — accuracy of perception / reality testing

FQ+ (Superior-Overelaborated)

Definition: form is used precisely and is enriched with accurate, well-articulated detail.

How to calculate: assigned per response from the form-quality tables; relatively rare.

What it tells: exact, high-quality perception with care and precision (also a key OBS-index variable).

Thinking / perception: indicates demanding accuracy; in excess (with other signs) it can reflect perfectionism.

Example: a carefully specified, accurately matched "two figures in detailed costume" can earn FQ+.

FQo (Ordinary)

Definition: a common, easily-seen object whose form fits the area well.

How to calculate: assigned when the percept matches the area conventionally and accurately.

What it tells: sound, conventional reality testing — the person sees what is really there as most people do.

Thinking / perception: a healthy share of FQo anchors perception to consensual reality.

Example: "a butterfly" on Card I = ordinary, accurate form quality.

FQu (Unusual)

Definition: an uncommon response whose contours nevertheless fit the area reasonably.

How to calculate: assigned for low-frequency but not contour-violating percepts; feeds Xu%.

What it tells: an individualistic, unconventional way of seeing — different, but not distorted.

Thinking / perception: moderate Xu% = creativity / nonconformity; high Xu% = a person who often departs from convention without losing reality contact.

Example: seeing a specific, plausible-but-rare object where most people see the popular = unusual form quality.

FQ- (Minus)

Definition: the form imposed does not fit the contours of the area; the percept distorts the blot.

How to calculate: assigned when contours are violated; feeds X-% and many indices.

What it tells: the central marker of impaired reality testing — seeing what is not really supported by the stimulus.

Thinking / perception: elevated FQ- / X-% indicates misperception and faulty judgement; very high levels suggest psychotic-range reality testing.

Example: insisting "this whole side is a clear human face" where the outline does not support it = minus.

FQnone

Definition: no form is involved in the response.

How to calculate: assigned to pure colour / pure shading answers with no shape.

What it tells: response governed entirely by colour or shading with no formal control.

Thinking / perception: associated with affective discharge or distress overriding structured perception.

Example: "just blackness, darkness" with no object = form quality none.

XA% / WDA% / X-% / X+% / Xu% (the form-quality percentages)

Definitions & formulas: XA% = (FQ+, o, u) / R (overall appropriate form); WDA% = appropriate-form W+D / (W+D); X-% = FQ- / R; X+% = (FQ+ , o) / R; Xu% = FQu / R.

Norms: XA% > ~.89; WDA% > ~.85; X-% < ~.15; P about 5 to 7.

What they tell: together they quantify reality testing and conventionality — how accurately and how conventionally the person perceives.

Thinking / perception: low WDA% means distortion even in obvious areas (pervasive, serious); high Xu% with low X-% means unconventional but intact; low X+% means reduced conventional perception.

Example: XA% .57, WDA% .60, X-% .43 describes someone misreading nearly half of reality, including obvious cues = severe impairment.

S- (Space minus)

Definition: a white-space response that also has minus form quality.

How to calculate: tally S responses that are FQ-.

What it tells: negativism / anger that is specifically linked to misperception.

Thinking / perception: indicates that when the person becomes oppositional or angry, their reality testing degrades.

Example: an angry, contour-violating space percept shows affect (anger) driving distortion.

5. CONTENTS — what was actually seen

H, (H), Hd, (Hd), Hx — Human contents

Definition: whole real humans (H), fictional/mythical humans ((H)), human parts (Hd), fictional parts ((Hd)), and human experience/emotion (Hx).

How to calculate: tally each; combine for total human content and the H:(H)+Hd+(Hd) ratio; Pure H counted separately.

What it tells: interest in people and the realism of one's view of self and others.

Thinking / perception: good Pure H = realistic, whole-person understanding; predominance of (H)/Hd/(Hd) = self/other concept built on fantasy or fragments; Hx = intellectualized handling of emotion.

Example: "two whole people talking" (Pure H) reflects realistic social perception; only "a mask" and "a giant monster" indicates an unreal, part-based view of people.

A, (A), Ad, (Ad) — Animal contents

Definition: whole real animals, fictional animals, animal parts, fictional animal parts.

How to calculate: tally; A% (animal content / R) is examined.

What it tells: animal content is the easiest, most common content; its proportion indexes conventionality and cognitive economy.

Thinking / perception: very high A% = stereotyped, guarded, simple, economical thinking; very low = unusual or strained processing.

Example: a record that is almost all "bat, butterfly, bug, dog" shows a constricted, stereotyped cognitive style.

An, Xy — Anatomy / X-ray

Definition: internal anatomy (An) or X-ray/skeletal imagery (Xy).

How to calculate: tally; combine as An+Xy for the self-perception cluster.

What it tells: bodily / somatic preoccupation and concern about physical integrity, illness or being damaged inside.

Thinking / perception: elevated An+Xy points to body-focused worry, somatic anxiety, or a sense of internal vulnerability.

Example: repeated "ribs, lungs, a damaged spine" answers reflect a preoccupation with bodily harm or illness.

Bl, Fi, Ex — Blood, Fire, Explosion

Definition: blood, fire, or explosion contents.

How to calculate: tally individually.

What it tells: strong, often poorly controlled affect, aggression, or trauma-linked imagery.

Thinking / perception: when frequent or affect-laden, they signal emotional intensity that may overwhelm controls.

Example: "blood everywhere" and "a huge explosion" together suggest intense, eruptive emotional themes.

Fd — Food

Definition: edible food content.

How to calculate: tally; used in CDI and interpersonal cluster.

What it tells: dependency needs — a wish to be nurtured, cared for, or looked after.

Thinking / perception: presence of Fd suggests reliance on others and a more dependent interpersonal stance.

Example: "ice cream" or "a piece of chicken" = food content, hinting at underlying dependency wishes.

Bt, Cl, Ge, Ls, Na — Isolation-Index contents

Definition: Botany, Clouds, Geography, Landscape, Nature.

How to calculate: feed the Isolation Index = (Bt + 2Cl + Ge + Ls + 2Na) / R.

What it tells: a preponderance is associated with social withdrawal and reduced interpersonal involvement.

Thinking / perception: an elevated index (>.24, and especially .33+) reflects detachment from people and retreat into impersonal imagery.

Example: a record heavy on "clouds, mountains, islands, plants" yields a high Isolation Index = withdrawn.

Art, Ay, Cg, Hh, Sc, Sx, Id, etc. — Other contents

Definition: Art and Anthropology (Ay) feed intellectualization; Clothing (Cg), Household (Hh), Science (Sc), Sex (Sx), Idiographic (Id) and others are tallied as present.

How to calculate: tally each; 2AB+Art+Ay forms the Intellectualization Index, and Cg is an HVI variable.

What it tells: content themes flag preoccupations (e.g., Art/Ay = intellectual distancing; Sx = sexual preoccupation; Sc = mechanical/impersonal focus).

Thinking / perception: elevations in a category point to a specific area of concern or defensive style rather than to a single fixed meaning.

Example: many "paintings, statues, ancient masks" (Art/Ay) suggest intellectualization as a way to neutralize feeling.

6. POPULAR RESPONSES (P)

P — Popular

Definition: one of the conventional answers that occurs in at least 1 of every 3 records.

How to calculate: count responses matching the 13 CS Populars; norm about 5 to 7.

What it tells: the ability and willingness to perceive the world as others conventionally do.

Thinking / perception: low P = idiosyncratic, possibly impaired or oppositional perception; very high P = over-conventional, conforming, plays it safe.

Example: "a bat" (I/V), "two people" (III), "an animal skin" (VI) are Populars; producing very few signals a departure from shared reality.

7. ORGANIZATIONAL ACTIVITY (Z scores)

Zf — Z Frequency

Definition: the number of responses that earn a Z score (i.e., that involve organizing the blot — wholes, or meaningfully relating separate details, or integrating space).

How to calculate: count every response assigned any Z value.

What it tells: the amount of cognitive effort the person invests in organizing and making sense of the field.

Thinking / perception: high Zf = effortful, motivated (or driven) processing; low Zf = an avoidant, economizing, or low-capacity style that skips organizing work.

Example: a person who repeatedly integrates "two figures doing X with an object" earns many Z scores = high processing effort.

ZSum and Zest

Definition: ZSum is the total of the weighted Z values actually earned; Zest is the value expected for that Zf (from a standard table).

How to calculate: sum the Z weights for ZSum; look up Zest for the given Zf.

What it tells: together they allow comparison of how much organizing was achieved versus what is expected.

Thinking / perception: they are intermediate values whose real meaning emerges in the Zd difference score.

Example: Zf 14 with ZSum 42 and Zest 45.5 yields Zd = -3.5 (slightly underincorporative).

Zd — Processing Efficiency

Definition: the difference score Zd = ZSum - Zest, indexing scanning efficiency.

How to calculate: subtract Zest from ZSum; norm is about -3.0 to +3.0.

What it tells: whether the person takes in too much, too little, or about the right amount of information before responding.

Thinking / perception: Zd > +3.0 = overincorporation (cautious, thorough, effortful, can be slow/inefficient, perfectionistic); Zd < -3.0 = underincorporation (hasty, careless scanning that misses cues, leading to faulty input and impulsive errors).

Example: Zd +5.0 describes someone who over-scans and double-checks; Zd -6.0 describes someone who glances and jumps to conclusions, often misreading situations.

PSV — Perseveration

Definition: repetition of the same response in a way that signals an inability to shift set (within-card, content, or mechanical).

How to calculate: count PSV occurrences.

What it tells: cognitive rigidity / inflexibility, or, in some records, a neuropsychological processing difficulty.

Thinking / perception: elevated PSV = difficulty changing mental set, getting "stuck," reduced flexibility of attention and thought.

Example: giving "a bat" to several cards in a row, unable to move on, suggests perseverative rigidity.

8. SPECIAL SCORES — in depth

The first six ("Critical Special Scores") capture disturbances in thinking/logic and are weighted into WSum6; each has a Level 1 (mild) and Level 2 (serious) form except ALOG and CONTAM.

DV — Deviant Verbalization (Levels 1 / 2; weight 1 / 2)

Definition: odd, distorted, or redundant language — a neologism or a needless repetition that conveys faulty word usage.

How to calculate: code DV1 or DV2; add weight (1 or 2) to WSum6.

What it tells: mild slippage at the level of words/expression rather than logic.

Thinking / perception: occasional DV1 is common and benign; DV2 or several DVs suggest looser verbal control.

Example: calling something "a flying-bird-airplane" (neologistic) or "a dead corpse" (redundancy) = DV.

INCOM — Incongruous Combination (1 / 2; weight 2 / 4)

Definition: incompatible features are condensed into a single impossible object.

How to calculate: code INCOM1/2; add weight to WSum6.

What it tells: a condensation error in which perception fuses details that cannot belong together.

Thinking / perception: reflects illogical merging of percept elements; Level 2 indicates more serious cognitive slippage.

Example: "a person with the head of a chicken" or "a pink bear" (wrong colour fused with object) = incongruous combination.

DR — Deviant Response (1 / 2; weight 3 / 6)

Definition: inappropriate phrases or rambling, circumstantial elaboration that drifts from the task.

How to calculate: code DR1/2; add weight to WSum6.

What it tells: loosened, derailing, or self-referential communication and difficulty staying on track.

Thinking / perception: indicates fluid, poorly bounded thinking; DR2 (marked derailment) is a serious sign.

Example: "it's a bat — my uncle once caught a bat, that was the year we moved house..." drifting away = deviant response.

FABCOM — Fabulized Combination (1 / 2; weight 4 / 7)

Definition: an implausible or impossible relationship is asserted between two clearly perceived objects.

How to calculate: code FAB1/2; add weight to WSum6; FAB2 also feeds PTI.

What it tells: illogical linkage of accurately seen parts — reasoning that connects things that cannot be connected.

Thinking / perception: a robust marker of disordered thought; FAB2 is among the more serious cognitive special scores.

Example: "two ants holding hands and praying" or "a butterfly attacking an elephant" = fabulized combination.

ALOG — Inappropriate Logic (weight 5)

Definition: the person uses strained, idiosyncratic reasoning to justify a response, unprompted.

How to calculate: code ALOG; add weight 5 to WSum6.

What it tells: autistic / illogical reasoning in which conclusions follow from faulty premises.

Thinking / perception: indicates a breakdown in the logic of thought, not just its expression.

Example: "it must be the North Pole because it's at the top of the card" = inappropriate logic.

CONTAM — Contamination (weight 7)

Definition: two separate percepts are fused into a single impossible image occupying the same area.

How to calculate: code CONTAM; add weight 7 (the heaviest) to WSum6.

What it tells: the most serious cognitive special score — a breakdown of the boundaries between ideas/percepts.

Thinking / perception: strongly associated with psychotic-level thought disturbance; even a single CONTAM is clinically weighty.

Example: seeing "a bloody island" by fusing "blood" and "island" into one inseparable thing, or a "butterfly-face," = contamination.

Raw Sum6 and WSum6

Definition: Raw Sum6 = the count of the six critical special scores; WSum6 = the sum of (each score's weight x its frequency).

How to calculate: weights are DV 1/2, INCOM 2/4, DR 3/6, FABCOM 4/7, ALOG 5, CONTAM 7.

What it tells: the overall amount and severity of disordered/illogical thinking in the record.

Thinking / perception: nonpatient WSum6 averages roughly 5; markedly elevated WSum6 (and any Level-2 / CONTAM) signals serious thought disturbance.

Example: WSum6 = 15 driven by 2 INCOM + 1 FABCOM + 1 CONTAM indicates genuine, clinically important thinking problems.

AB — Abstraction

Definition: a clear symbolic or abstract representation is given (e.g., a percept stands for an emotion/idea).

How to calculate: count AB; weighted double in the Intellectualization Index (2AB+Art+Ay).

What it tells: use of intellectualization — turning feelings into ideas/symbols to keep them at a distance.

Thinking / perception: moderate AB can reflect capacity for abstraction; high contributes to a defensive, distancing style.

Example: "this red shape represents anger / the conflict between good and evil" = abstraction.

AG — Aggressive Movement

Definition: a movement response in which the action is aggressive (fighting, attacking, threatening).

How to calculate: count AG; used in interpersonal cluster, CDI, HVI logic.

What it tells: a tendency to perceive relationships and interactions as hostile, competitive, or threatening.

Thinking / perception: elevated AG = an aggressive/forceful interpersonal set, expecting or projecting hostility.

Example: "two animals fighting / tearing at each other" (Card II) = aggressive movement.

COP — Cooperative Movement

Definition: a movement response in which two or more objects interact in a clearly positive, cooperative way.

How to calculate: count COP; used in interpersonal cluster, CDI, DEPI.

What it tells: the expectation of positive, collaborative, harmonious interaction with others.

Thinking / perception: presence of COP is generally adaptive (people are seen as approachable); absence suggests reduced expectation of cooperation.

Example: "two people lifting a basket together" or "dancing" = cooperative movement.

CP — Colour Projection

Definition: the person reports chromatic colour on an achromatic (grey/black) area — sees colour that is not there.

How to calculate: count CP; reviewed in the affect cluster.

What it tells: a denial-based defence — replacing unpleasant reality/affect with an artificially pleasant one.

Thinking / perception: reflects a hysteroid tendency to gloss over distress and impose positive feeling, a mild reality-bending in the emotional domain.

Example: calling a clearly grey-black area "a pretty, colourful butterfly" = colour projection.

MOR — Morbid Content

Definition: a percept that is dead, damaged, broken, spoiled, or given a dysphoric attribution.

How to calculate: count MOR; used in both self-perception and ideation clusters; >2 is significant.

What it tells: a damaged, vulnerable self-image and a pessimistic, gloomy cognitive set that colours interpretation.

Thinking / perception: high MOR primes the person to construe events negatively and to see self/world as harmed or doomed.

Example: "a crushed bug," "a torn, dying leaf," "a broken, bleeding heart" = morbid contents.

PER — Personalized Response

Definition: the person justifies a response by referring to personal knowledge or experience ("I know because I have one").

How to calculate: count PER; reviewed in the interpersonal cluster.

What it tells: a defensive authoritarianism — bolstering the self and warding off challenge by appealing to personal authority.

Thinking / perception: low PER is normal; high PER suggests insecurity managed through rigidity, control, and a need to be right, which can strain relationships.

Example: "it's a specific kind of orchid — I know because I grow them" repeatedly = personalized responses.

GHR : PHR — Good vs Poor Human Representation

Definition: each human-related response is classified as a Good (GHR) or Poor (PHR) human representation by a set decision rule.

How to calculate: tally GHR and PHR; compare as a ratio.

What it tells: the quality of internalized object/relationship representations — how adaptively the person conceives of people.

Thinking / perception: GHR > PHR = adaptive, effective interpersonal templates; PHR > GHR = maladaptive, distorted, or off-putting ways of representing and engaging others.

Example: "two friends talking" (GHR) vs "a monster ripping a body apart" (PHR); a 0:2 ratio signals uniformly poor relational templates.

9. CORE RATIOS — Controls & Stress Tolerance

Lambda (L)

Definition: the proportion of pure-form answers, indexing a simplifying/economizing style.

How to calculate: L = F / (R - F); norm about .30 to .99.

What it tells: how much the person narrows the stimulus field and screens out complexity and emotion.

Thinking / perception: L > 0.99 = avoidant style (oversimplifies, dodges affect/ambiguity); very low L = over-incorporates, takes in too much and is easily flooded.

Example: L = 1.0 (half the record pure form) describes a person who keeps life simple and emotionally distant.

EB — Erlebnistypus (coping style)

Definition: the balance between ideational and emotional response tendencies.

How to calculate: EB = Sum M : WSumC; classified Introversive (M side), Extratensive (colour side), or Ambitent (roughly equal). Not interpretable if EA < 4.

What it tells: the preferred problem-solving / coping mode.

Thinking / perception: Introversive = thinks things through internally before acting; Extratensive = uses feelings and trial-and-error; Ambitent = inconsistent, switches modes, less efficient.

Example: EB 7:1.0 = strongly introversive (ideational); EB 2:6.5 = extratensive (affect-driven).

EA — Experience Actual (resources)

Definition: the volume of organized psychological resources available for deliberate coping.

How to calculate: EA = Sum M + WSumC; norm about 7 to 11.

What it tells: how much capacity the person can deploy purposefully to manage demands.

Thinking / perception: high EA = ample coping capital; low EA = limited resources, leaving the person more easily overwhelmed even if outwardly stable.

Example: EA = 2.0 indicates a person running on very little usable resource.

EBPer — EB Pervasiveness

Definition: how rigidly the dominant EB style is used.

How to calculate: EBPer = larger EB side / smaller side (only when a clear style exists); 2.5 or more = pervasive.

What it tells: whether the coping style is flexible or applied inflexibly across situations.

Thinking / perception: EBPer ≥ 2.5 means the person almost always defaults to one mode, losing the flexibility to switch when the situation calls for it.

Example: EBPer 7.0 in an introversive person = thinks through nearly everything, rarely lets feeling guide action, even when it would help.

eb — Experience Base

Definition: the balance of non-deliberate, "felt" pressures.

How to calculate: eb = (FM + m) : (SumC' + SumT + SumV + SumY).

What it tells: the load of internal demand (left side = need/stress ideation) and painful affect (right side = constraint, loneliness, self-criticism, anxiety).

Thinking / perception: a high right side = significant internalized distress; a high left side = pressure from drives and situational stress.

Example: eb 4:5 with the right side elevated points to a person weighed down by suppressed, painful emotion.

es and Adj es

Definition: es is the total current stimulus demand; Adj es removes situational "noise."

How to calculate: es = FM + m + SumC' + SumT + SumV + SumY; Adj es = es minus all but one m and all but one Y.

What it tells: es = how much internal demand is pressing now; Adj es = the more stable, trait-level demand.

Thinking / perception: comparing es with EA shows whether demand exceeds resources; comparing es with Adj es isolates the situational component.

Example: es 9 with Adj es 8 (one excess m removed) shows mostly chronic, with a small situational add-on.

D Score and Adj D Score

Definition: standardized indices of the capacity for control and stress tolerance (current vs baseline).

How to calculate: convert (EA - es) for D and (EA - Adj es) for Adj D via the standard banding (within ±2.5 → 0; ±2.6 to 5.0 → ±1; ±5.1 to 7.5 → ±2; etc.).

What it tells: whether the person can reliably form and direct behaviour under demand.

Thinking / perception: minus = overload and proneness to impulsive lapses; 0 = average; plus = sturdy control. D < Adj D = situational stress is currently lowering control; D = Adj D = the strain is chronic.

Example: D = -2, Adj D = -2 means a chronically overwhelmed control system with standing vulnerability to disorganization.

10. AFFECT CLUSTER RATIOS

Afr — Affective Ratio

Definition: responsiveness to the coloured (emotionally loaded) cards relative to the rest.

How to calculate: Afr = responses to Cards VIII+IX+X / responses to Cards I–VII; norm about .50 to .80.

What it tells: willingness to engage with and process emotional stimulation.

Thinking / perception: low Afr (<.46) = avoids/withdraws from emotion; very high = drawn to or over-aroused by affect.

Example: Afr .27 indicates marked avoidance of emotionally evocative situations.

FC:CF+C and Pure C

Definition: the balance of well-modulated vs poorly-modulated emotional expression.

How to calculate: compare FC against the sum of CF and C; note Pure C separately.

What it tells: how controlled or unrestrained the person's emotional displays are.

Thinking / perception: FC > CF+C = controlled affect; CF+C dominant = under-modulated, intense; any Pure C = raw, impulsive discharge.

Example: FC:CF+C = 3:7 with Pure C = 3 describes someone whose feelings frequently spill out with little restraint.

SumC':WSumC (Constriction Ratio), S, Blends:R, CP

Definition: markers of how much affect is held in vs expressed, plus negativism, complexity and denial.

How to calculate: compare SumC' (held-in) with WSumC (expressed); also note S count, Blends:R, and CP.

What it tells: a left-weighted constriction ratio = painful suppression of feeling; S = anger; Blends:R = emotional complexity; CP = denial.

Thinking / perception: together they describe the texture of emotional life — bottled-up, oppositional, complex, or defensively rose-tinted.

Example: SumC':WSumC = 4:0 = strong internalization with no outlet (a recipe for dysphoria/tension).

11. IDEATION CLUSTER RATIOS

a:p, Ma:Mp, Intellectualization (2AB+Art+Ay), MOR, M-

Definition: the markers of how flexible, active, defended, pessimistic, or distorted the thinking is.

How to calculate: a:p and Ma:Mp from movement coding; 2AB+Art+Ay for intellectualization; count MOR and M-.

What it tells: a:p = flexibility of attitudes; Ma:Mp = active vs escapist thought; intellectualization = distancing defence; MOR = pessimistic set; M- = reality-distorted reasoning.

Thinking / perception: lopsided a:p = rigid views; Mp dominant = retreat into fantasy; index 4–5 = notable intellectualization, 6+ = major defence; M- = judgement that misfires especially about people.

Example: Ma:Mp 0:2 with MOR 8 = a passive, fantasy-leaning thinker dominated by themes of damage.

12. SELF-PERCEPTION RATIOS

Egocentricity Index 3r+(2)/R; Fr+rF; FD; SumV; An+Xy; MOR; H:(H)+Hd+(Hd)

Definition: the cluster describing the amount and tone of self-focus and the realism of self-image.

How to calculate: Egocentricity = [3 x (Fr+rF) + Sum(2)] / R (norm .33 to .45); plus the component variables above.

What it tells: how much attention is on the self, whether self-regard is inflated or deflated, and whether identity rests on reality or fantasy.

Thinking / perception: high index with reflections = narcissistic self-absorption; low index = poor self-worth; V = painful self-criticism; An+Xy = body preoccupation; MOR = damaged self; right-weighted H ratio = identity built on imagination/distortion.

Example: Egocentricity .29, Fr+rF 0, MOR high = low self-esteem with a damaged self-image (not narcissism).

13. INTERPERSONAL RATIOS

COP, AG, GHR:PHR, SumT, Human content / Pure H, a:p, Fd, PER, Isolation Index

Definition: the cluster describing how the person expects, perceives, and engages in relationships.

How to calculate: tallies and ratios above; Isolation Index = (Bt + 2Cl + Ge + Ls + 2Na) / R.

What it tells: expectations (cooperative vs hostile), representational quality, need for closeness, realism about people, dependency, defensiveness, and social withdrawal.

Thinking / perception: COP>AG and GHR>PHR = adaptive relating; T=0 = guarded distance; low Pure H = poor grasp of others; Isolation >.24 = withdrawal; high PER = controlling defensiveness.

Example: COP 0, AG 4, GHR:PHR 2:4, T 0 = a guarded person who expects hostility and relates ineffectively.

14. PROCESSING RATIOS

Zf, W:D:Dd, W:M, Zd, DQ, PSV

Definition: the cluster describing effort, approach, aspiration, efficiency, sophistication, and flexibility of processing.

How to calculate: frequencies and ratios above; W:M compares organizing ambition with ideational delivery.

What it tells: how hard and how well the person organizes incoming information.

Thinking / perception: high Zf = effort; W:M with W far exceeding M = over-aspiration; Zd over/under = efficiency; DQ = sophistication; PSV = rigidity.

Example: Zf 3 with Zd +4.5 = a person who rarely organizes, but over-scans when they do — erratic, inefficient processing.

15. THE SIX INDICES / CONSTELLATIONS

PTI — Perceptual-Thinking Index

Definition: a 0–5 scale flagging combined disturbance of perception and thinking.

Criteria (count met): (1) XA%<.70 & WDA%<.75; (2) X-%>.29; (3) Level-2 >2 & FAB2>0; (4) R≤16 & WSum6>12, or R>16 & WSum6>17; (5) M->1 or X-%>.40.

What it tells: the likelihood and degree of psychotic-spectrum reality-testing / thought problems (continuous; higher is worse, no fixed cutoff).

Thinking / perception: values of 4–5 strongly suggest serious mediational/ideational disturbance; even 3 is clinically meaningful, especially with a CONTAM or very high X-%.

Example: PTI 3 with X-% .45 and a CONTAM points to severe, if circumscribed, reality-testing difficulty.

DEPI — Depression Index

Definition: a 7-condition constellation for depressive organization.

Criteria (≥5 positive): vista/FD; colour-shading blend or S>2; egocentricity (low or high-with-no-reflection); Afr<.46 or Blends<4; shading>FM+m or SumC'>2; MOR>2 or intellectualization>3; COP<2 & Isolation>.24.

What it tells: whether the personality structure carries significant depressive features (affective + cognitive).

Thinking / perception: 5 = potential for affective disruption; 6–7 = a serious, often disabling depressive picture.

Example: DEPI 6 with vista, low Afr, high MOR = a strong, pervasive depressive organization.

CDI — Coping Deficit Index

Definition: a 5-condition constellation for social/coping inadequacy.

Criteria (≥4 positive): EA<6 or AdjD<0; COP<2 & AG<2; WSumC<2.5 or Afr<.46; p>a+1 or PureH<2; SumT>1 or Isolation>.24 or Fd>0.

What it tells: a deficit in coping resources and social effectiveness.

Thinking / perception: a positive CDI marks someone who flounders socially and copes ineffectively; often the affective distress is secondary to relationship failure.

Example: CDI 5 with EA 2 and Pure H 0 = profound coping and interpersonal-skill deficits.

S-CON — Suicide Constellation

Definition: a 12-variable list screening for self-destructive potential (reviewed first).

Criteria (≥8 of 12 = red flag; 7 = caution): FV+VF+V+FD>2; colour-shading blend>0; Ego<.31 or >.44; MOR>3; |Zd|>3.5; es>EA; CF+C>FC; X+%<.70; S>3; P<3 or >8; PureH<2; R<17.

What it tells: the degree to which the record resembles those of people who later harmed themselves.

Thinking / perception: ≥8 warrants urgent risk evaluation; 7 still warrants caution (has predicted near-lethal attempts).

Example: S-CON 8 in a record with vista and high MOR is a serious safety flag requiring direct assessment.

HVI — Hypervigilance Index

Definition: a constellation for a guarded, mistrustful, hyper-alert stance.

Criteria: prerequisite FT+TF+T = 0, PLUS at least 4 of: Zf>12; Zd>+3.5; S>3; H+(H)+Hd+(Hd)>6; (H)+(A)+(Hd)+(Ad)>3; H+A:Hd+Ad <4:1; Cg>3.

What it tells: a chronic, energy-consuming state of preparedness against a world felt to be threatening.

Thinking / perception: when positive, the person is cautious, guarded, preoccupied with personal space, and slow to trust; exacerbations resemble paranoid features.

Example: T=0 with Zf 14, high Hd/Ad and Cg>3 yields a positive HVI = pervasive hypervigilant guardedness.

OBS — Obsessive Style Index

Definition: a constellation for a perfectionistic, over-precise, detail-bound cognitive style.

Criteria (positive via combinations): built from Dd>3; Zf>12; Zd>+3.0; P>7; FQ+>1 — positive if all five, or various subsets together with FQ+>3 and/or X+%>.89.

What it tells: an over-careful, perfectionistic, indecisive style preoccupied with correctness and detail.

Thinking / perception: when positive, the person is meticulous, conforming, and detail-focused, sometimes at the cost of efficiency and spontaneity.

Example: high Dd, high Zf, many Populars and FQ+ with X+% >.89 = an obsessive, perfectionistic processing style.

Golden rules for interpretation: (1) never read a single score alone — interpret variables in combination; (2) compare to norms (deviation principle), then integrate into the whole person; (3) all frequencies depend on R; (4) anchor meanings to the engine — Form = control, Movement = mental activity (M ideation / FM needs / m stress), Colour = emotion expressed, C'/shading = emotion or pain held in, Texture = closeness, Vista = painful self-look, Reflection = self-regard; (5) the Cognitive Triad (Processing → Mediation → Ideation) and the Self/Interpersonal pair are each interpreted together.
