## TESTS ADMINISTERED AND RATIONALE

**Beck's Depression Inventory (BDI):** The Beck's Depression Inventory is a widely used self-report measure that assesses the severity of depressive symptoms such as sadness, hopelessness, fatigue, sleep disturbances, and suicidal ideation. This test was administered to assess the presence and severity of depressive symptoms, including persistent low mood, loss of interest, fatigue, sleep disturbances, and negative self-evaluation, as reported by the patient.

**Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS):** The Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale is a clinician-administered rating scale that assesses the severity of obsessions and compulsions independently of the content of the symptoms, based on the time occupied, interference with functioning, associated distress, resistance offered, and the degree of control the patient has over them. This scale was administered to evaluate the severity of the patient's obsessive thoughts and compulsive behaviours and their impact on daily functioning.

**Beck's Anxiety Inventory (BAI):** The Beck's Anxiety Inventory is a self-report measure that assesses the severity of anxiety symptoms, with an emphasis on somatic manifestations such as numbness, tingling, dizziness, palpitations, and fear, while minimising overlap with depressive symptoms. This scale was administered to assess the presence and severity of the patient's anxiety symptoms, particularly the somatic and subjective components of anxiety.

## YALE–BROWN OBSESSIVE COMPULSIVE SCALE (Y-BOCS)

| Scores Obtained | Level of symptom severity |
|:---:|:---:|
| **24** | **Severe** |

A total score of 24 on the Yale–Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale reflects a *severe* level of obsessive-compulsive symptomatology. The score indicates that obsessions and compulsions together occupy a considerable portion of the patient's day, with substantial time consumed by intrusive thoughts and repetitive behaviours. The symptoms are associated with marked interference in social, occupational, and personal functioning, and with a considerable degree of distress.

Analysis across the two subscales suggests that the patient experiences frequent and distressing obsessions accompanied by compulsive acts performed in an effort to reduce the associated anxiety. The patient's attempts to resist the obsessions and compulsions appear to be limited, and the degree of perceived control over these symptoms is reduced, such that the urges are yielded to with difficulty. This pattern reflects the ego-dystonic and effortful nature of the symptoms and their intrusive impact on the patient's routine.

The findings indicate a **severe level of obsessive-compulsive symptoms**, with significant time occupied by symptoms, notable interference in daily functioning, associated distress, and limited control, warranting active clinical intervention and monitoring.

> **Interpretation of category:** Using the widely applied clinical severity grid for the Y-BOCS (total range 0–40), scores of **0–7** indicate *subclinical*, **8–15** *mild*, **16–23** *moderate*, **24–31** *severe*, and **32–40** *extreme* symptoms. A score of **24** therefore falls at the lower end of the **severe** band.
>
> *Note:* A recent empirical benchmarking study of the Y-BOCS-II proposed alternative ranges (non-/sub-clinical 0–14, mild 15–21, moderate 22–34, severe 35–50), under which a 24 would be classified as *moderate*. The established clinical convention used in most reports, however, places 24 in the **severe** range. (See sources below.)
