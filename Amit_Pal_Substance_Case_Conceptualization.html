Clinical Notes for Both Models
  • Convergence of both models: Both Beck's cognitive model and Marlatt & Gordon's relapse prevention model identify the same core maintaining mechanism in Amit's case — a rigid, emotion-driven coping style that overwhelms cognitive control before coping strategies can be engaged, combined with a damaged self-concept that generates permission-giving beliefs (Beck) or amplifies the Abstinence Violation Effect (Marlatt & Gordon).
  • Key paradox: Amit possesses adequate-to-strong psychological resources (EA=12, Adj D=+1), yet his pervasively extratensive style (EBPer=5.0) prevents their utilization in high-risk moments. The therapeutic leverage point is NOT resource-building but cultivating reflective delay and affect modulation.
  • Rorschach data integrated: EB=2:10 (pervasively extratensive), EA=12.0, EBPer=5.0, FC:CF+C=3:7, Pure C=3, MOR=5, Ego=0.29, Zd=-14.0, Sum T=0, X-%=0.28, P=3, 2AB+Art+Ay=4, Sum V=0, S-CON=7, m=4, Sum Y=1, Afr=0.47, H:(H)+Hd+(Hd)=1:3.
  • Risk alert: S-CON=7 (below formal cutoff of 8 but elevated concern per Fowler et al.) — combined with MOR=5, Pure C=3, damaged self-image, and substance use context — active and direct suicide-risk assessment is warranted.
  • Treatment implications from both models:
    • Affect regulation training (to reduce raw discharge: target Pure C and FC:CF+C imbalance)
    • Reflective delay / mindfulness (to interpose thought between trigger and action: target EBPer rigidity)
    • Cognitive restructuring of core beliefs about self as damaged (target MOR=5, Ego=0.29)
    • Coping skills training for high-risk situations (to activate existing EA=12 resources)
    • Relapse prevention: addressing AVE cognitions and permission-giving beliefs
    • Interpersonal therapy: developing capacity for genuine closeness (target Sum T=0)
    • MBRP (Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention): aligns with need for decentering from emotion and craving tolerance
