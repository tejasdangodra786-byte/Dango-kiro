## Modifying Indices (MCMI-III)

The patient's protocol was found to be valid and interpretable. The Validity Index (V = 0) indicates no evidence of random, inconsistent, or careless responding, confirming the protocol's reliability.

The Disclosure Index (X) raw score of 78 falls within the acceptable and normal range (61-123), suggesting that the patient adopted a relatively open and self-revealing response style. The level of disclosure indicates adequate engagement with the test items without excessive guardedness or over-reporting.

The Desirability Index (Y) score was notably low (BR = 25), indicating a minimal tendency to present oneself in an overly favourable or socially desirable manner. The patient did not attempt to distort responses to appear well-adjusted, psychologically healthy, or free of problems. This low score suggests either genuine openness about difficulties or a lack of motivation to manage social impressions.

The Debasement Index (Z) score was elevated (BR = 72), reflecting a moderate tendency toward negative self-presentation, self-criticism, or emphasis on personal distress. This may indicate genuine subjective suffering, a cry-for-help response style, or some degree of exaggeration of psychological difficulties. Given that Z > Y, the modifying indices adjustment was applied: +1 BR point added to Scales 8A, 8B, C, and S, and -1 BR point subtracted from Scales 4, 5, and 7.

Overall, the response style appears candid and somewhat negatively weighted, suggesting that the patient is willing to disclose concerns but may present himself in a more distressed manner than is objectively warranted. Clinical scale elevations should therefore be interpreted in conjunction with clinical interview data, behavioral observations, and corroborative information.

## Clinical Personality Patterns

No personality scale reached the threshold for pathological level (BR ≥ 85) or trait level (BR ≥ 75). However, several scales show subclinical elevations in the suggestive range (BR 60-74), indicating the presence of personality features that, while not meeting disorder-level criteria, may still influence the patient's interpersonal functioning, coping style, and overall adjustment.

### Scale 3 - Dependent (BR = 70)

The highest personality elevation on the Dependent scale at a subclinical level suggests a tendency toward excessive reliance on others for emotional support, decision-making, and reassurance. The patient may display features of submissiveness, difficulty asserting personal needs, and a preference for others to take the lead in decision-making. There may be an underlying fear of abandonment or loss of support, leading to compliance, self-sacrifice, and difficulty expressing disagreement. While not at a pathological level, these dependent features may contribute to difficulty in autonomous functioning, especially during periods of stress or when support systems are disrupted.

### Scale 6A - Antisocial (BR = 65) & Scale 6B - Sadistic/Aggressive (BR = 65)

The moderate elevations on the Antisocial and Sadistic scales suggest the presence of some features related to impulsivity, resistance to conventional expectations, and possible tendencies toward assertion of power or dominance in interpersonal relationships. At this subclinical level, the patient may display occasional disregard for rules, low frustration tolerance, and difficulty consistently accepting responsibility for actions. There may also be some assertiveness that, under stress, manifests as irritability or confrontational behaviour. These features are not pervasive enough to indicate a personality disorder but may influence interpersonal dynamics, particularly in authority-related contexts.

### Scale 8A - Negativistic/Passive-Aggressive (BR = 66)

The subclinical elevation on the Negativistic scale suggests an intermittent tendency toward indirect expression of dissatisfaction, resistance through procrastination or sullenness, and fluctuation between compliance and opposition. The patient may experience internal conflict between dependency needs and desires for autonomy, sometimes resulting in unpredictable or inconsistent behaviour. This feature may manifest as ambivalence toward responsibilities and relationships, where cooperation alternates with passive resistance.

### Scale 2A - Avoidant (BR = 60) & Scale 2B - Depressive (BR = 60)

The suggestive-level elevations on Avoidant and Depressive scales indicate mild features of social hesitation, sensitivity to criticism, and a tendency toward pessimism and self-doubt. ## Overall Impression

The MCMI-III profile suggests a personality configuration characterized by a predominance of **dependent features** (BR = 70) combined with subclinical elevations across **antisocial/aggressive** (BR = 65), **negativistic/passive-aggressive** (BR = 66), and **paranoid** (BR = 65) dimensions. This pattern is suggestive of an individual who, on one hand, seeks support and guidance from others, but on the other hand, harbours mistrust, resentment, and difficulty with authority, resulting in an ambivalent and at times contradictory interpersonal stance.

The most notable clinical syndrome elevation is on the **Somatoform scale** (BR = 70), indicating a tendency to channel psychological distress into physical complaints. The **substance dependence** elevations (Alcohol = 65, Drug = 65), while subclinical on this measure, are consistent with risk factors for substance misuse and should be explored further through clinical interview and history.

The **Debasement > Desirability** pattern on modifying indices indicates that the patient approaches testing with a willingness to disclose difficulties and may present himself in a somewhat more distressed manner than is typical. This should be considered when interpreting elevated scales, as some inflation of distress-related scores may be present.

Importantly, **no scale reached pathological level (BR ≥ 85)** and **no scale reached trait level (BR ≥ 75)**, indicating that the current profile does not suggest the presence of a diagnosable personality disorder or full clinical syndrome based on MCMI-III criteria alone. The overall pattern is best characterized as a **mixed subclinical profile** with features of dependency, passive-aggression, somatization, and mild substance-related risk, which together may contribute to functional difficulties but do not constitute a distinct personality disorder or severe clinical syndrome.

The profile should be interpreted in conjunction with the clinical interview, behavioral observations, case history, and other psychological test findings for a comprehensive diagnostic formulation.
