1. Introduction

1.1 What is MMPI-3?

The Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory-3 (MMPI-3) is the latest iteration of the most widely used and researched objective personality and psychopathology assessment instrument in clinical psychology. Published in 2020 by Yossef S. Ben-Porath and Auke Tellegen, the MMPI-3 represents a substantial revision of the MMPI-2-RF (Restructured Form).

Key Facts:
  • Authors: Yossef S. Ben-Porath & Auke Tellegen
  • Year of Publication: 2020
  • Publisher: University of Minnesota Press
  • Number of Items: 335 (True/False)
  • Target Population: Adults aged 18 years and older
  • Administration Time: Approximately 25–50 minutes
  • Reading Level: 5th–6th grade English reading level
  • Number of Scales: 52 scales total

1.2 Domains Assessed by MMPI-3

DomainDescription
Emotional DysfunctionDepression, anxiety, anger, low positive emotions
Thought DysfunctionPsychotic experiences, aberrant perceptions, paranoid ideation
Behavioral DysfunctionSubstance abuse, antisocial behavior, impulsivity
Somatic ComplaintsPhysical symptoms, cognitive complaints, neurological complaints
Interpersonal FunctioningSocial avoidance, family problems, relationship issues
Personality TraitsFive-factor personality pathology dimensions (PSY-5)
Response ValidityConsistency, overreporting, underreporting

Critical Differences for Viva (MMPI-3 vs. MMPI-2):
  1. No K-correction — MMPI-2 added K fractions to 5 Clinical Scales
  2. Shorter — 335 items vs. 567 items
  3. Updated normative sample — 2020 census-matched vs. 1980s
  4. Hierarchical interpretation (H-O → RC → SP → PSY-5) instead of code-type
  5. New scales — CRIN, EAT, CMP, SFI
  6. RC Scales replace heterogeneous Clinical Scales
  7. No Fb scale — shorter test eliminates need
  8. Updated content — removed outdated/offensive items

2. When to Use MMPI-3

2.1 Indications

A. Personality & Psychopathology Assessment

  • Comprehensive mapping of dysfunction domains
  • PSY-5 personality trait dimensions
  • Differentiating Axis I vs Axis II pathology

B. Diagnostic Clarification

  • MDD vs. Adjustment Disorder
  • Psychotic spectrum vs. personality pathology
  • Somatic Symptom Disorder vs. medical illness
  • PTSD vs. personality disorder

C. Forensic Evaluation

  • Competency evaluations
  • Personal injury litigation
  • Custody evaluations
  • Malingering detection (F-r, Fp-r, Fs)
  • Defensiveness detection (L-r, K-r)

D. Treatment Planning

  • Target symptoms and severity
  • Treatment engagement prediction
  • Substance abuse risk (RC4, SUB)
  • Suicide risk (SUI scale)
  • Modality selection guidance

3. Administration Overview

Format & Duration

  • 335 True/False items
  • Paper-and-pencil or computer
  • Duration: 25–50 minutes (avg 35 min)
  • No time limit (untimed)

Testing Conditions

  • Quiet, private room
  • Examiner available but not explaining items
  • No aids (dictionary, phone)
  • Observe for fatigue/confusion

Pre-Administration Checklist

  1. Confirm age ≥ 18
  2. Assess reading ability (min 5th grade)
  3. Confirm adequate cognitive functioning
  4. Ensure no acute intoxication
  5. Explain purpose & obtain informed consent
  6. Clarify no “right or wrong” answers
  7. Instruct to answer every item
  8. Answer based on current/recent experiences

4.2 STEP 1: Check Cannot Say (CNS) Items

What are CNS items? Any item where the examinee did NOT choose either True or False (both blank OR both filled).

CNS CountAction
0–5✔ Proceed with scoring — negligible impact
6–14⚠ Proceed with caution — some scales may be attenuated
≥ 15❌ Protocol likely INVALID — do not interpret substantive scales

4.3 STEP 2: Calculate Raw Scores

4.3.1 The Keyed Response System

Each of the 335 items belongs to one or more scales. For each scale, items are “keyed” in a specific direction:

  • Keyed True: If examinee answers TRUE → score 1 point for that scale
  • Keyed False: If examinee answers FALSE → score 1 point for that scale
Example:
Item 38: “I feel sad most of the time” — Keyed True for RCd
• Answers TRUE → Score 1 on RCd
• Answers FALSE → Score 0 on RCd

Item 107: “I enjoy being with other people” — Keyed False for RC2
• Answers FALSE → Score 1 on RC2
• Answers TRUE → Score 0 on RC2

4.3.2 Raw Score Calculation Rules

RuleDescription
1 point per keyed responseEach item endorsed in keyed direction = 1 point
0 for non-keyed responseOpposite to key = 0 points
0 for omitted itemsCNS items score 0 on ALL scales they belong to
No partial creditBinary scoring only (0 or 1)
No reverse scoring stepKey already accounts for direction
No K-correctionUnlike MMPI-2, raw scores NOT adjusted
Items on multiple scalesOne item may contribute to 2–3 scale raw scores
Important: In MMPI-3, there is no separate “reverse scoring” step. If an item is Keyed False, answering FALSE scores a point — it is already “reversed” in the key. The scoring templates handle this automatically.

4.4 STEP 3: Convert Raw Scores to T-Scores

4.4.1 What is a T-Score?

A T-Score is a standardized score allowing comparison to a normative group.

  • Mean = 50 (normative average)
  • SD = 10
  • T = 65 means 1.5 SD above normative mean
  • T = 80 means 3.0 SD above normative mean

Conceptual formula:

T-Score = 50 + 10 × [(Raw Score - Normative Mean) / Normative SD]

In practice, use the normative conversion tables from the MMPI-3 manual.

4.4.2 The Normative Sample (2020)

CharacteristicDetail
Total N1,620 community-dwelling adults
Gender~50% male, ~50% female
Age Range18–80+ years
MatchingU.S. Census 2020 (age, gender, race/ethnicity, education)
ExclusionsThose in psychiatric/substance treatment excluded

4.4.3 Types of T-Score Transformations

Uniform T-Scores

Used for most substantive scales. Ensures percentile equivalence across scales: T=65 ≈ 92nd percentile on ALL scales.

Linear T-Scores

Used for validity scales (VRIN-r, TRIN-r, CRIN, F-r, Fp-r, Fs, FBS-r, L-r, K-r) and Interest Scales (AES, MEC).

4.4.4 Gender-Based Norms

MMPI-3 uses COMBINED-GENDER norms as the PRIMARY norm set.
This is a change from MMPI-2 (separate male/female norms). Gender-specific norms available as supplement.

4.4.5 Worked Example

Case: RCd (Demoralization) — 18 items

Items keyed TRUE: #15, #38, #52, #88, #103, #145, #178, #201, #235, #267, #289, #310
Items keyed FALSE: #22, #67, #130, #156, #198, #244

Examinee endorsed TRUE on: #15, #38, #88, #103, #145, #201, #235, #267, #310 = 9 matches
Examinee endorsed FALSE on: #22, #156, #244 = 3 matches

Raw Score = 9 + 3 = 12
Look up in norm table → T-Score = 68
T ≥ 65 → Clinically significant elevation

  • ≈ 92nd percentile (uniform T)
  • 1.5 SD above normative mean
  • Empirically established threshold for reliable clinical correlates

5.2 Section A: Response Consistency Scales

VRIN-r (Variable Response Inconsistency-Revised)

Purpose: Detects random or careless responding using paired items with similar/opposite content.

T-ScoreInterpretation
T ≥ 80❌ INVALID — Random responding highly likely
T 70–79⚠ Possibly invalid — interpret with extreme caution
T < 70✔ Acceptable consistency

TRIN-r (True Response Inconsistency-Revised)

Purpose: Detects fixed responding (acquiescence or nay-saying).

T-ScoreDirectionInterpretation
T ≥ 80True (T)❌ INVALID — Indiscriminate True responding
T ≥ 80False (F)❌ INVALID — Indiscriminate False responding
T 70–79Either⚠ Possible fixed responding
T < 70—✔ No evidence of fixed responding

CRIN (Combined Response Inconsistency) — NEW IN MMPI-3

Purpose: Single comprehensive index combining both variable and fixed inconsistency. Catches inconsistency missed by VRIN-r or TRIN-r alone.

T-ScoreInterpretation
T ≥ 80❌ INVALID — Pervasive inconsistency
T 70–79⚠ Possible inconsistency
T < 70✔ Consistent responding

5.3 Section B: Overreporting Scales

F-r (Infrequent Responses-Revised)

Purpose: Items endorsed by <10% of normative sample. Detects symptom exaggeration.

T-ScoreInterpretation
T ≥ 120❌ INVALID — Almost certainly non-credible
T 100–119❌ INVALID — Likely non-credible overreporting
T 80–99⚠ Possible overreporting; interpret with caution
T 70–79⚠ Possible genuine severe pathology OR mild overreporting
T < 70✔ No significant overreporting

Fp-r (Infrequent Psychopathology-Revised)

Purpose: Items endorsed by <10% of psychiatric inpatients. Differentiates malingering from genuine illness.

T-ScoreInterpretation
T ≥ 100❌ INVALID — Non-credible responding certain
T 80–99⚠ Likely overreporting
T 70–79⚠ Possible overreporting
T < 70✔ No evidence of overreporting beyond genuine symptoms
Key Clinical Pattern:
F-r ↑, Fp-r normal → Likely genuine severe psychopathology
F-r ↑, Fp-r ↑ → Likely malingering/fabrication
Both normal → No overreporting

Fs (Infrequent Somatic) & FBS-r (Symptom Validity)

Fs: Detects somatic symptom overreporting (items endorsed by <10% of medical patients).

FBS-r: Detects non-credible somatic/cognitive complaints, especially in forensic/litigation contexts.

T-ScoreFs InterpretationFBS-r Interpretation
T ≥ 100❌ Non-credible somatic reporting❌ Non-credible somatic/cognitive
T 80–99⚠ Likely somatic overreporting⚠ Probable non-credible
T 70–79⚠ Possible overreporting⚠ Possible non-credible
T < 70✔ Credible✔ Credible

5.4 Section C: Underreporting Scales

L-r (Uncommon Virtues-Revised)

Purpose: Detects unrealistic claims of moral virtue/perfection.

T-ScoreInterpretation
T ≥ 80❌ INVALID — True pathology likely hidden
T 70–79⚠ Probable underreporting
T 60–69⚠ Possible mild underreporting
T < 60✔ No significant virtue claiming
T < 39Unusually willing to admit faults

K-r (Adjustment Validity-Revised)

Purpose: Detects denial of psychological difficulties; claims superior adjustment.

T-ScoreInterpretation
T ≥ 70❌ Significant underreporting
T 60–69⚠ Possible defensiveness
T 40–59✔ Within normal limits
T < 39Unusually poor self-concept; possible overreporting
L-r vs K-r:
L-r = “I am morally perfect” (virtue claiming)
K-r = “I have no psychological problems” (adjustment claiming)
K-r is NOT used as a correction factor in MMPI-3 (unlike MMPI-2)

6. Hierarchical Scale Structure

The hierarchical model is the DEFINING feature of MMPI-3 interpretation. 
LEVEL 1: Higher-Order (H-O) Scales     → BROADEST (3 scales)
    ↓
LEVEL 2: Restructured Clinical (RC)    → CORE (9 scales)
    ↓
LEVEL 3: Specific Problem (SP)         → DETAILED (26 scales)
    ↓
LEVEL 4: PSY-5 Scales                  → PERSONALITY (5 scales)
    ↓
LEVEL 5: Interest Scales               → INTERESTS (2 scales)

7. Step-by-Step Interpretation Strategy

THIS IS THE CLINICAL WORKFLOW — FOLLOW THIS ORDER EVERY TIME

STEP 1: Check Validity

  1. Record CNS count → If ≥ 15, STOP
  2. Check VRIN-r, TRIN-r, CRIN → If any ≥ 80, STOP (inconsistent)
  3. Check F-r, Fp-r → If ≥ 100, STOP (overreporting/invalid)
  4. Check L-r → If ≥ 80, STOP (underreporting/invalid)
  5. Note any caution-level elevations (T 70–79) for validity modifiers
  6. Write validity statement

Output: “Protocol is VALID / VALID WITH CAUTION / INVALID”

STEP 2: Interpret Higher-Order (H-O) Scales

  1. Examine EID, THD, BXD T-scores
  2. Identify which (if any) are ≥ 65
  3. Determine primary domain(s) of dysfunction
  4. Note relative elevation (which is highest?)

Output: “Primary dysfunction domain is [EID/THD/BXD/Multiple]”

STEP 3: Interpret RC Scales

  1. Within each elevated H-O domain, examine constituent RC scales:
    • EID → RCd, RC1, RC2, RC7
    • THD → RC6, RC8
    • BXD → RC4, RC9
  2. Also examine RC3 (cross-cutting)
  3. Identify all RC scales ≥ 65
  4. Generate interpretive statements for each elevated RC

Output: List of elevated RC scales with clinical meaning

STEP 4: Interpret Specific Problem (SP) Scales

  1. For each elevated RC, examine related SP scales
  2. Determine which SPECIFIC symptoms are driving the RC elevation
  3. Pay special attention to:
    • SUI (any elevation = immediate action)
    • SUB (substance abuse risk)
    • AGG (violence risk)
  4. Note SP elevations without corresponding RC elevation

Relationship Mapping: RC → SP

RC Scale ElevatedLook at These SP Scales

RC1MLS, GIC, HPC, NUC, COG

STEP 5: Interpret PSY-5 Scales

  1. Examine all five PSY-5 scales
  2. Identify elevations (T ≥ 65) and very low scores (T ≤ 39)
  3. Generate personality trait descriptions
  4. Consider personality disorder implications

STEP 6: Look for Patterns and Code-Types

  1. Identify the two highest RC scales (code-type analog)
  2. Look for known patterns (see Section 8)
  3. Note any unusual or contradictory patterns

STEP 7: Integrate Findings

  1. Synthesize all levels into coherent clinical narrative
  2. Answer the referral question
  3. Generate: primary diagnostic impression, comorbidities, severity, risk factors, personality traits, treatment recommendations
  4. Write the psychological report

8. Code-Type Interpretation

Important Note: Unlike MMPI-2, MMPI-3 does NOT primarily use code-type interpretation. The hierarchical model (Section 7) is recommended. However, identifying the two most elevated RC scales provides useful clinical patterns.

8.1 How to Determine Code-Type Equivalent

  1. Identify the two RC scales with the highest T-scores (both must be ≥ 65)
  2. List them in order of elevation (highest first)
  3. The combination provides a clinical pattern

8.2 Common Two-Point RC Scale Patterns

RCd + RC2 (Demoralization + Low Positive Emotions)

Clinical Picture: Severe Depressive Syndrome

  • Pervasive unhappiness (RCd) combined with anhedonia (RC2)
  • Suggests Major Depressive Disorder with melancholic features
  • High hopelessness — assess suicide risk carefully
  • Poor prognosis without intervention; may require medication + psychotherapy
  • Check SUI, HLP for additional risk information

RCd + RC7 (Demoralization + Dysfunctional Negative Emotions)

Clinical Picture: Anxious Depression / Mixed Anxiety-Depressive

  • General distress (RCd) with prominent anxiety and negative emotionality (RC7)
  • Consider: GAD with comorbid depression, Mixed Anxiety-Depressive Disorder
  • Look at STW, AXY for anxiety specifics
  • Treatment: CBT for anxiety + depression; consider SSRI

RCd + RC1 (Demoralization + Somatic Complaints)

Clinical Picture: Somatization with Depression

  • Emotional distress (RCd) expressed through physical symptoms (RC1)
  • Consider: Somatic Symptom Disorder, Depression with somatic features
  • Rule out genuine medical conditions
  • Look at MLS, GIC, HPC, NUC, COG for specifics
  • Treatment: Integrated medical-psychological approach

RC6 + RC8 (Ideas of Persecution + Aberrant Experiences)

Clinical Picture: Active Psychotic Process

  • Paranoid ideation (RC6) with unusual perceptual experiences (RC8)
  • Strongly suggests Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorder
  • If RCd also elevated → Schizoaffective (depressed type)
  • If RC9 also elevated → Schizoaffective (bipolar type) or Mania with psychotic features
  • Treatment: Antipsychotic medication primary; psychological support adjunctive

RC4 + RC9 (Antisocial Behavior + Hypomanic Activation)

Clinical Picture: Antisocial Personality with High Energy/Impulsivity

  • Rule-breaking (RC4) combined with overactivation (RC9)
  • Consider: Antisocial PD, Bipolar (manic), or both
  • High risk for substance abuse, legal problems, interpersonal conflict
  • Check SUB, AGG, JCP for specifics
  • Treatment: Challenging engagement; structured approaches; substance treatment

RC4 + RCd (Antisocial Behavior + Demoralization)

Clinical Picture: Demoralized Antisocial

  • Antisocial history (RC4) now experiencing distress (RCd)
  • Common in forensic/incarcerated settings
  • May be demoralized about consequences rather than genuine remorse
  • Assess motivation for treatment carefully

RC7 + RC1 (Dysfunctional Negative Emotions + Somatic)

Clinical Picture: Anxiety with Somatic Expression

  • Anxiety (RC7) manifesting through physical symptoms (RC1)
  • Consider: Panic Disorder, Health Anxiety, GAD with somatic focus
  • Look at AXY (panic) and somatic SP scales

RC2 + RC9 Low (Low Positive Emotions + Low Activation)

Clinical Picture: Severe Anhedonic Depression with Psychomotor Retardation

  • Complete absence of positive emotions (RC2 high) with no energy (RC9 low)
  • Vegetative depression; “can’t feel anything” and “no energy”
  • May need activating antidepressant or behavioral activation focus

RC6 + RC3 (Ideas of Persecution + Cynicism)

Clinical Picture: Paranoid Personality Style

  • Both paranoid ideation (RC6) and general mistrust (RC3)
  • If RC6 < 80 → Paranoid PD more likely than psychotic disorder
  • If RC6 ≥ 80 → Psychotic-level paranoia
  • Treatment: Very difficult; trust-building essential

RC9 + RC8 (Hypomanic Activation + Aberrant Experiences)

Clinical Picture: Mania with Psychotic Features

  • Overactivation/grandiosity (RC9) with perceptual disturbances (RC8)
  • Strongly suggests Bipolar I, manic with psychotic features
  • Or substance-induced mania with psychosis
  • Treatment: Mood stabilizer + antipsychotic

9. Clinical Inference

9.1 Deriving Diagnostic Impressions

CRITICAL PRINCIPLE: MMPI-3 does NOT provide diagnoses. It provides diagnostic SUPPORT and HYPOTHESIS GENERATION.

Proper language:

  • ✔ “The MMPI-3 profile is consistent with a diagnosis of...”
  • ✔ “The pattern of elevations supports the possibility of...”
  • ✔ “The findings are suggestive of...”
  • ❌ “The MMPI-3 diagnoses the patient with...”
  • ❌ “According to MMPI-3, the diagnosis is...”

9.4 Risk Assessment

9.4.1 Suicide Risk

Primary Indicators:
  • SUI (Suicidal/Death Ideation) — T ≥ 65 = IMMEDIATE concern
  • HLP (Helplessness/Hopelessness) — cognitive vulnerability
  • RCd (Demoralization) — emotional suffering
  • RC2 (Low Positive Emotions) — anhedonia = risk factor
Secondary Indicators:
  • INTR-r elevated (withdrawal, isolation)
  • NFC elevated (feeling unable to cope)
  • Low K-r (acknowledging distress without buffer)
Action Protocol:
  1. SUI T ≥ 65 → Conduct structured suicide risk assessment (C-SSRS)
  2. Document risk level in report
  3. Provide specific safety recommendations
  4. Communicate to referring clinician immediately

9.4.2 Violence/Aggression Risk

Primary Indicators:

  • AGG (Aggression) — T ≥ 65
  • RC9 (Hypomanic Activation) — high energy + impulsivity
  • AGGR-r (Aggressiveness personality trait)
  • RC4 (Antisocial Behavior) — history of rule-breaking

Secondary Indicators:

  • ANP elevated (anger proneness)
  • DISC-r elevated (poor impulse control)
  • RC6 elevated (paranoia may drive reactive aggression)

9.4.3 Substance Abuse Risk

Primary Indicators:

  • SUB (Substance Abuse) — T ≥ 65
  • RC4 (Antisocial Behavior)
  • BXD (Behavioral/Externalizing Dysfunction)
  • DISC-r (Disconstraint)

Secondary:

  • RC9 elevated (impulsivity)
  • JCP elevated (conduct problems history)

9.5 Personality Trait Formulation

Using PSY-5 scales, generate a dimensional personality description:

“[Client] presents with a personality characterized by [high/low AGGR-r], [high/low PSYC-r], [high/low DISC-r], [high/low NEGE-r], and [high/low INTR-r]. This personality configuration is associated with [PD considerations] and suggests that treatment should [implications].”
Example:
“Mr. X presents with a personality characterized by high Negative Emotionality (NEGE-r T=78), indicating pervasive emotional instability, anxiety-proneness, and irritability. Combined with elevated Disconstraint (DISC-r T=68), indicating impulsivity and poor behavioral regulation, and elevated Aggressiveness (AGGR-r T=72), indicating a domineering and instrumentally aggressive interpersonal style, this configuration is consistent with Borderline and Antisocial personality features. Treatment should prioritize emotion regulation skills and impulse control strategies.”

10. Report Writing

10.1 MMPI-3 Psychological Report Structure

Section 1: Identifying Information & Referral

Client Name: [Name]
Age/DOB: [Age]
Date of Testing: [Date]
Referral Source: [Who referred and why]
Referral Question: [Specific question to be answered]
Examiner: [Your name, degree, license]
Test Administered: MMPI-3 (Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory-3)

Section 2: Behavioral Observations

  • Client’s demeanor during testing
  • Level of cooperation and engagement
  • Observable signs of distress, fatigue, confusion
  • Time taken to complete the test
  • Any unusual behaviors noted

Section 3: Validity Statement (ALWAYS FIRST IN RESULTS)

Template — Valid Protocol:
“Review of the MMPI-3 validity indicators reveals a consistent response pattern (VRIN-r T=[X], TRIN-r T=[X], CRIN T=[X]), with no significant evidence of overreporting (F-r T=[X], Fp-r T=[X]) or underreporting (L-r T=[X], K-r T=[X]). [Number] items were left unanswered (CNS=[X]). The protocol is considered valid, and the substantive scales can be interpreted with confidence as an accurate reflection of the client’s self-reported symptoms and personality characteristics.”
Template — Caution:
“Validity indicators suggest a mild tendency toward [overreporting/underreporting]. Specifically, [scale name] was mildly elevated at T=[X], suggesting [interpretation]. While the protocol remains interpretable, the substantive scale scores may [slightly overestimate/underestimate] the actual level of psychopathology. Results should be interpreted with this caveat.”
Template — Invalid:
“The validity indicators indicate that this protocol is invalid due to [specific reason]. [Scale] T=[X] exceeds the threshold for valid interpretation. The substantive scales cannot be meaningfully interpreted. The reasons for this response pattern may include [possible explanations]. Re-administration under different conditions may be considered.”

Section 4: Symptom Domains (Results — Body of Report)

Organize by domain, from most elevated to least:

  1. Emotional/Internalizing Domain (if EID elevated)
    • Demoralization (RCd), emotional symptoms (RC2, RC7), SP specifics (SUI, HLP, SFD, NFC, STW, AXY, ANP)
  2. Thought Dysfunction Domain (if THD elevated)
    • Type of thought dysfunction (RC6, RC8), severity, implications
  3. Behavioral/Externalizing Domain (if BXD elevated)
    • Behavioral problems (RC4, RC9), SP specifics (SUB, AGG, JCP, ACT)
  4. Somatic Domain (if RC1 elevated)
    • Type of somatic complaints (MLS, GIC, HPC, NUC, COG)
  5. Interpersonal Domain (if interpersonal SP scales elevated)
    • Relationship patterns (FML, IPP, SAV, SHY, DSF, SFI)

Section 5: Personality Traits

  • Report PSY-5 findings
  • Link to personality disorder considerations
  • Describe interpersonal style implications

Section 6: Clinical Summary & Diagnostic Impression

“In summary, [Client]’s MMPI-3 profile is characterized by [brief pattern description]. The overall picture is consistent with [diagnostic impressions]. The primary areas of concern include [list]. Risk factors identified include [SUI/AGG/SUB if relevant].”

Diagnostic Impression (supported by MMPI-3):
• Primary: [Diagnosis]
• Secondary: [Diagnosis]
• Personality Features: [Description]

Section 7: Recommendations

  1. Treatment modality recommendations
  2. Medication considerations (if appropriate)
  3. Risk management recommendations
  4. Further assessment needs
  5. Prognosis indicators

10.2 Sample Report Excerpt (Illustrative)

Validity:

“Mr. A responded to the MMPI-3 in a consistent manner (VRIN-r T=48, TRIN-r T=52, CRIN T=50). There is no evidence of overreporting (F-r T=55, Fp-r T=47) or underreporting (L-r T=52, K-r T=45). Zero items were omitted. The protocol is valid and the results can be interpreted with confidence.”

Results:

“Mr. A’s profile is characterized by significant elevations on measures of emotional/internalizing dysfunction (EID T=72). Specifically, he reports marked demoralization (RCd T=70), indicating pervasive unhappiness and a sense of being overwhelmed. He also reports significant anhedonia (RC2 T=68), indicating inability to experience positive emotions. His level of dysfunctional negative emotions is elevated (RC7 T=66), indicating anxiety, worry, and irritability beyond normal levels.”
“Examination of the Specific Problem scales reveals clinically significant hopelessness (HLP T=72), self-doubt (SFD T=67), and stress/worry (STW T=65). The Suicidal/Death Ideation scale (SUI T=70) is elevated, indicating current thoughts related to death or self-harm that require immediate clinical attention.”
“No evidence of thought dysfunction (THD T=48) or behavioral/externalizing problems (BXD T=44) was found.”
“Personality assessment (PSY-5) indicates elevated Negative Emotionality (NEGE-r T=72) and Introversion (INTR-r T=67), suggesting a personality prone to emotional distress and social withdrawal.”

Summary:

“Mr. A’s MMPI-3 profile is consistent with a Major Depressive Episode of moderate-to-severe intensity, characterized by demoralization, anhedonia, anxiety, hopelessness, and suicidal ideation. His personality traits (high negative emotionality, introversion) may predispose him to depressive episodes and make treatment engagement challenging.”

Recommendations:

  1. Immediate suicide risk assessment using structured instrument (C-SSRS)
  2. Psychiatric referral for medication evaluation (antidepressant)
  3. Individual psychotherapy (CBT for depression; behavioral activation for anhedonia)
  4. Monitor treatment response; consider re-administration in 3–6 months
  5. Social engagement interventions to address withdrawal

11.1 Critical Errors to Avoid

2Over-interpreting single scaleOne scale without context is misleadingUse hierarchical approach; interpret in context
