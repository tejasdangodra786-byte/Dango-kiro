## 1. Rachman's Model of OCD — Applied to This Case

**Diagram file:** `rachman_model_case_conceptualization.svg`

| Model Component | Case-Specific Content |
|---|---|
| **Trigger Stimulus** | Conflict involving paternal uncle (~4 years ago, symptom onset); later, exposure to a disturbing visual that he replays "the entire day" |
| **Unwanted intrusive thoughts** | Recurrent thoughts of harm/death befalling family members; intrusive sexual thoughts about girls |

| **Obsessions** | Y-BOCS = 33 (severe range) — persistent, distressing, time-consuming intrusive thoughts |
| **Increase in distress & anxiety** | Severe depressive features (BDI = 30), guilt, shame, impaired social/academic/interpersonal functioning |
| **Neutralization** | Repeated eye rubbing and avoidance of swallowing saliva — rituals that bring only temporary relief and loop back to reinforce further intrusions (maintenance cycle) |

## 2. Salkovskis' Cognitive Model of OCD — Applied to This Case

**Diagram file:** `salkovskis_model_case_conceptualization.svg`

| Model Component | Case-Specific Content |
|---|---|
| **Early experiences (vulnerability)** | Strong emotional dependency on mother; heightened sensitivity to family's safety; guilt-proneness and harsh self-critical stance evident on TAT (superego "harsh and guilt-inducing") |
| **Critical incidents (what started it off)** | Conflict involving the paternal uncle ~4 years ago, which activated repetitive thoughts about harm/negative consequences to others |
| **Assumptions/general beliefs** | "If I think about harm to my family, I am responsible for it"; "A sexual thought means I am a bad/immoral person"; "Not neutralizing is as bad as the harm occurring"; "Better safe than sorry" (rub eyes / hold saliva) |
| **Intrusive thoughts, images, urges, doubts** | Repeated negative thoughts about harm to family; intrusive sexual thoughts about girls; disturbing visual replayed all day — Y-BOCS = 33 (severe) |
| **Misinterpretation of significance — responsibility for actions** | Interprets intrusions as meaning he is personally responsible for preventing harm, and that sexual thoughts reflect his true, "bad" character — generating excessive guilt/disgust rather than normalizing the thoughts |
| **Neutralizing actions (rituals, reassurance, mental argument)** | Repeated eye rubbing to "remove" the thought/image; avoidance of swallowing saliva to prevent thoughts "entering" him — both give only short-lived relief |
| **Attention & reasoning biases (looking for trouble)** | Hypervigilant scanning for disturbing visuals/situations; rumination and overthinking; interpersonal mistrust (SSCT Interpersonal Relationships = 20, significant disturbance) |
| **Counterproductive safety strategies (suppression, impossible criteria, avoidance)** | Attempts to suppress/avoid the thoughts entirely; discomfort and avoidance of interacting with females; social withdrawal, reduced academic engagement |
| **Mood changes (distress, anxiety, depression)** | BAI = 24 (moderate anxiety), BDI = 30 (severe depression); chronic guilt, hopelessness, low self-esteem (SSCT Self-Concept = 16); loneliness/emotional deprivation themes on TAT |

**Maintenance loop:** Safety behaviors and negative mood states feed back into and intensify further intrusive thoughts (dashed red arrows in diagram), sustaining the OCD cycle over the reported 4-year course.

## Clinical Notes for Both Models

- Both formulations converge on the same core maintenance mechanism: appraisal of normal intrusive thoughts as highly significant/threatening (inflated responsibility, thought–action fusion), followed by rituals/neutralization that provide only short-term relief while preventing disconfirmation of the catastrophic beliefs — thereby perpetuating the cycle.
- Test data integrated: Y-BOCS = 33 (severe OCD), BDI = 30 (severe depression), BAI = 24 (moderate anxiety), SSCT (Family = 11, Sex = 09, Interpersonal = 20, Self-Concept = 16), and TAT themes of guilt, rejection, and harsh superego functioning.
- These conceptualizations directly support the report's recommended interventions: cognitive restructuring of responsibility/thought-action fusion beliefs, and Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP) targeting the eye-rubbing and saliva-avoidance rituals.
