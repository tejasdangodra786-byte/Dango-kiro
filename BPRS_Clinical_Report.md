## Interpretive Cautions

- The BPRS is a clinician-rated, observer-dependent instrument; scores reflect the rater's clinical judgment at a specific point in time and may vary with the patient's cooperation, rapport, and the rater's training.
- A total score of 30 places the presentation in the mild range globally, but domain-specific elevations (particularly Depressive Mood and Somatic Concern at moderate levels) carry clinical significance that the total score alone may obscure.
- The BPRS was originally designed for inpatient populations with severe mental illness; its sensitivity to mild-to-moderate depressive and somatic presentations in outpatient settings may be limited.
- All conclusions derive from the BPRS alone and require integration with clinical history, mental status examination, collateral information, and supplementary psychological/medical assessment before any diagnostic determination.
- The absence of a symptom on the BPRS does not confirm its absence in the patient's experience; it reflects only that the symptom was not observed or reported at the threshold of clinical significance at the time of rating.
