Clinical Case History – Child
Detailed Interview Questions

A section-by-section bank of questions to ask the child and the parents / informant while taking a detailed child case history. Organized in the exact order of the Clinical Case History – Child proforma.

Ask the Parent / Informant Ask the Child Ask Both
Made by Tejas Dangodra

Sections (proforma order)

  1. Socio-Demographic Data
  2. Chief Complaints
  3. Family History
  4. Personal History
  5. Schooling History
  6. General Temperament & Personality
  7. History of Present Illness
  8. Treatment & Negative History
  9. Family Perception & Functional Impairment
  10. Mental Status Examination (child)
  11. General Physical Examination
How to use. Ask open questions first, then probe for detail (when it started, how often, where, what makes it better/worse, how it affects daily life). Colour tags show who to direct each question to. Not every question is needed in every case — select by the child's age and presenting problem.

1Socio-Demographic Data

Basic identifying details as listed on the proforma. Confirm with records where available.

  • What is the child's full name? Parent
  • What are the parents' names? Parent
  • What is the child's date of birth, and current age? Parent
  • What is the child's gender (as registered in the clinical setting)? Parent
  • What is the child's religion / cultural background (if relevant)? Parent
  • What is the child's educational status — is the child in school, and in which class? Parent
  • What is the family's socio-economic status / main source of income? Parent
  • What is the place of residence (urban / rural) and full address? Parent
  • What are the contact number, UHID number, and Aadhar number (for records)? Parent
  • What is the date of admission / assessment? Parent
  • Who has accompanied the child today, and what is the source of referral (self / school / doctor / other)? Parent

Informant & reliability

  • What is your relationship with the child? Informant
  • Do you live with the child? Informant
  • How much time do you spend with the child each day? Informant
  • How long have you known the child (duration of acquaintance)? Informant
  • How well do you know the child's developmental, behavioural and school history? Informant
  • Is there another caregiver (other parent, grandparent, teacher) who could add to or confirm this history? Informant

2Chief Complaints

Elicit the presenting problems in the informant's own words, then probe each for onset and duration.

  • What brought you here today? Parent
  • What problems or changes have you noticed in the child? Parent
  • Which of these concerns you the most? Parent
  • When did you first notice this problem? Parent
  • How long has each problem been present? Parent
  • In what order did the problems appear (which came first)? Parent
  • What is the reason you decided to seek consultation now? Parent
  • (To the child, age-appropriately) Do you know why you have come here today? Is there anything that is bothering you? Child

3Family History

Covers the genogram, family history of illness, living arrangements, caregivers, parents, home and neighbourhood, family relationships, and child-rearing practices.

Family history of illness

  • Has anyone in the family (parents, siblings, grandparents, uncles/aunts) had any psychiatric or mental illness? Parent
  • Is there any family history of epilepsy or fits? Parent
  • Is there any family history of developmental problems, intellectual disability, or learning difficulties? Parent
  • Has anyone in the family had a substance-use problem (alcohol, tobacco, drugs)? Parent
  • Is there any family history of suicide or attempted suicide? Parent
  • Is there any significant chronic medical or genetic illness in the family? Parent
  • Are the parents related by blood (consanguinity)? Parent

Family structure & living arrangements

  • Who all live at home with the child? Parent
  • Is it a nuclear, joint, or extended family? Parent
  • How many siblings does the child have, and what is the child's birth order? Parent
  • Who is the child's primary caregiver? Parent
  • Has the child ever lived away from the parents or with other relatives? Parent

Parents, home & relationships

  • Can you describe each parent — age, education, occupation, health, and personality? Parent
  • How would you describe the relationship between the parents? Parent
  • How does the child get along with each parent and with siblings? Both
  • What is the home atmosphere generally like — calm, tense, supportive? Parent
  • What is the neighbourhood like where the family lives? Parent
  • Have there been any recent family stressors — separation, loss, illness, financial difficulty, or major conflict? Parent

Child-rearing practices

  • Who mainly looks after the child's daily needs? Parent
  • How is the child usually disciplined when he/she misbehaves? Parent
  • Do the caregivers agree on how to raise and discipline the child? Parent
  • How affectionate and involved are the parents in the child's daily life? Parent
  • Is there any other information about the family that you feel is important? Parent

4Personal History

Prenatal, perinatal, postnatal, birth and early development, developmental milestones, current developmental status, behaviour, and physical illness. Ask the mother where possible.

Prenatal (pregnancy) history

  • Was the pregnancy planned? Mother
  • How old were you (mother) at the time of this pregnancy? Mother
  • Did you have regular antenatal check-ups? Mother
  • Did you have any illness during pregnancy — fever, infections, high blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid problems, or fits? Mother
  • Did you take any medicines during pregnancy? If so, which? Mother
  • Was there any exposure to tobacco, alcohol, or other substances during pregnancy? Mother
  • Were there any nutritional problems or significant weight issues during pregnancy? Mother
  • Was there any significant emotional stress, injury, or trauma during pregnancy? Mother
  • Was there any bleeding or threatened abortion during the pregnancy? Mother
  • Was there any exposure to radiation or toxins (where relevant)? Mother

Perinatal & neonatal (birth) history

  • Was the baby born on time (full-term) or early (preterm)? Mother
  • Was it a normal delivery or a caesarean? If assisted, what was the reason? Mother
  • What was the baby's birth weight? Mother
  • Did the baby cry immediately after birth (birth cry present or absent)? Mother
  • Was any resuscitation, oxygen, or special care needed at birth? Mother
  • Was the baby admitted to the NICU? If so, why and for how long? Mother
  • Were there any fits, yellowness (jaundice), bluish colour, or breathing problems in the first days? Mother
  • Were there any feeding problems or infections in the newborn period? Mother

Postnatal & early development

  • How was the baby's health in the first months of life? Parent
  • Was breastfeeding established, and were there any feeding difficulties? Parent
  • Was the baby easy to soothe, or unusually irritable / difficult to settle? Parent

Developmental milestones — motor

  • At what age did the child gain head control? Parent
  • At what age did the child sit without support? Parent
  • At what age did the child crawl and stand? Parent
  • At what age did the child start walking independently? Parent
  • At what age did the child start using hands to pick up small objects (fine motor)? Parent

Developmental milestones — language

  • At what age did the child start babbling? Parent
  • At what age did the child say his/her first meaningful word? Parent
  • At what age did the child join two words together? Parent
  • At what age did the child speak in short sentences and hold a conversation? Parent
  • Is the child's speech clear and understandable for the age? Parent

Developmental milestones — social & adaptive

  • At what age did the child first smile back at people (social smile)? Parent
  • Did the child point to show you things and share attention? At what age? Parent
  • At what age did the child start playing pretend games and playing with other children? Parent
  • At what age could the child feed himself/herself? Parent
  • At what age was the child toilet-trained (day and night)? Parent
  • At what age could the child dress and manage self-care with / without help? Parent

Developmental regression

  • Did the child ever learn a skill and then lose it (words, play, social interaction, toileting, or school skills)? Parent
  • If yes, which skill was lost, at what age, and how quickly? Parent
  • Was there any illness, fever, or fit around the time the skill was lost? Parent

Current developmental status & behaviour

  • Compared with other children of the same age, how is the child's overall development? Parent
  • How is the child's general intelligence, speech, and language now? Parent
  • How independent is the child in self-help skills (eating, dressing, toileting, bathing)? Parent
  • How would you describe the child's behaviour — generally easy-going, or with frequent temper tantrums? Parent
  • Has the child had any significant physical illness, hospitalization, injury, or surgery? Parent
  • Does the child have any long-term medical condition, allergy, or regular medication? Parent
  • Are the child's hearing and vision normal? Parent

5Schooling History

Follows the proforma's detailed schooling items (a–n) plus habits/interests and sexual history. Combine parent, child, and teacher accounts where possible.

School entry & adjustment

  • At what age did the child start school? Parent
  • How did the child adjust when starting school? Parent
  • Has there been any change of school, medium of instruction, or syllabus? Why? Parent
  • Do you like going to school? What do you like and dislike about it? Child

Academic performance

  • How has the child performed academically overall? Parent
  • How is the child doing in reading, writing, and arithmetic specifically? Both
  • Which subjects are easy for you, and which are difficult? Child
  • What are your (the parents') expectations about the child's performance? Parent
  • Are you satisfied with the child's current performance? Parent
  • How does the child manage homework and examinations? Parent
  • Has the child ever repeated a class? Parent

Behaviour, peers & teacher's view

  • How does the child behave in the classroom? Parent
  • How is the child's attention and concentration in class? Both
  • How does the child get along with classmates and friends at school? Child
  • Does the child take part in extra-curricular activities? Both
  • What does the teacher say about the child (perceptions and concerns)? Parent
  • Has the child been bullied, or bullied others? Child
  • Does the child ever refuse to go to school? If so, why? Parent
  • Are there any other school-related problems? Parent
  • If schooling was discontinued, when and why? Parent
  • Is a school report available, and what does it show? Parent

Habits, interests & sexual history

  • What are the child's habits, interests, hobbies, and talents? Both
  • What do you enjoy doing in your free time? Child
  • (Where age-appropriate) Has puberty started — any body changes? For girls, has menstruation begun and is it regular? Parent
  • (Only where clinically indicated, privately) Is there any concern about age-inappropriate sexual behaviour or exposure to sexual content? Parent

6General Temperament & Personality Attributes

Captures the child's usual style — emotionality, attention, activity, aggression, sociability, rhythmicity, motivation, dependability, moral standards, adaptability, and strengths.

  • How would you describe the child's usual emotional nature? Parent
  • How is the child's attention and concentration in daily activities? Parent
  • Is the child generally very active, average, or calm? Parent
  • How does the child show anger — is there any aggression? Parent
  • Is the child sociable and outgoing, or shy and withdrawn? Parent
  • Are the child's sleep and meal routines regular (rhythmicity)? Parent
  • How motivated is the child to try and complete tasks? Parent
  • Is the child trustworthy and dependable for age-appropriate responsibilities? Parent
  • How would you describe the child's sense of right and wrong (moral standards)? Parent
  • How does the child cope with change or new situations (adaptability)? Parent
  • What are the child's particular strengths or good qualities? Parent

7History of Present Illness

Establishes total duration, onset, course, progress, and predisposing / precipitating / perpetuating factors, plus biological and occupational functioning. Open with the anchor: "The child was apparently well until…"

Onset, course & progression

  • Until when was the child apparently well and behaving as expected? Parent
  • What was the very first change you noticed? Parent
  • How old was the child when it started? Parent
  • Did the problem start suddenly or gradually (abrupt / acute / subacute / insidious)? Parent
  • Was anything happening in the child's life around that time (a possible trigger)? Parent
  • Since it began, does it come and go, or is it present all the time (episodic / chronic / chronic with fluctuation)? Parent
  • Overall, is the problem staying the same, getting worse, or improving (static / deteriorating / improved)? Parent
  • How often does it happen, and how severe is it? Parent

Detailed probing (for each main complaint)

  • What usually happens immediately before the problem/episode? Parent
  • What exactly happens during it? Parent
  • How does the child behave afterward? Parent
  • Does it happen at home, at school, or in both places? Parent
  • Does anything make it better or worse? Parent
  • Are there any other symptoms that occur along with it? Parent
  • (To the child, age-appropriately) Can you tell me what happens when this occurs? How do you feel? Child

Predisposing / precipitating / perpetuating factors

  • Was there anything about the child or the family that may have made this more likely (predisposing)? Parent
  • Was there a specific event that seemed to trigger it (precipitating)? Parent
  • Is anything keeping the problem going or making it continue (perpetuating)? Parent

Biological & occupational functioning

  • Since the problem began, how has the child's sleep been? Parent
  • How has the child's appetite and eating been? Parent
  • How is the child managing self-care since the problem started? Parent
  • Is there any change in bowel or bladder control? Parent
  • How has school attendance and daily routine been affected (occupational functioning)? Parent

Associated symptom screen (ask as relevant)

  • Is the child overactive, restless, or unable to sit still? Parent
  • Does the child have difficulty paying attention, listening, or finishing tasks? Parent
  • Is the child impulsive — interrupting, unable to wait, acting without thinking? Parent
  • Is the child argumentative, defiant, or having temper outbursts? Parent
  • Have there been problems like aggression, lying, stealing, or staying away from home/school? Parent
  • Does the child worry a lot, have specific fears, or become very distressed on separation? Both
  • Has the child seemed sad, irritable, or lost interest in play and activities? Both
  • Are there repetitive thoughts the child can't stop, or actions the child feels must be done a certain way? Child
  • (Gently, age-appropriately) Does the child ever hear or see things others don't, or hold unusual beliefs? Both
  • How is the child's sleep — bedtime, night awakenings, nightmares, snoring, daytime sleepiness? Parent
  • Any unusual eating, very restricted eating, or eating of non-food items? Parent
  • Any bedwetting, soiling, or constipation? Parent
  • Is the child unusually sensitive to sounds, textures, lights, smells, or tastes? Parent
  • How much screen time does the child have, and is it affecting sleep or schoolwork? Parent
  • (Adolescents, privately) Have you come across or tried tobacco, alcohol, or any other substances? Child
  • Has the child been through any frightening or very upsetting experience (only ask sensitively)? Both
  • (Screen for safety) Does the child ever talk about not wanting to live, or about hurting himself/herself or others? Both

8Treatment History & Negative History

Treatment history (till date)

  • Have you consulted anyone for this problem before (doctor, psychologist, special educator, therapist)? Parent
  • Was any diagnosis given to you earlier? Parent
  • Was any medication started? If so, which, at what dose, and for how long? Parent
  • Did the child take the treatment regularly (adherence)? Parent
  • Were there any side effects? Parent
  • Has the child received any psychological therapy, counselling, special education, occupational therapy, speech therapy, or behavioural intervention? Parent
  • Did any of these treatments help — and in what way? Parent

Negative history (points to check and rule out)

  • Has the child ever had seizures/fits, fainting, or loss of consciousness? Parent
  • Has there been any significant head injury or brain infection? Parent
  • Any staring spells, unusual movements, self-talk, or self-smiling? Parent
  • Any history suggesting elevated mood, over-talkativeness, or excessive energy? Parent
  • Any suggestion of hearing or seeing things that are not there? Parent
  • Any previous self-harm or suicide attempts? Parent
  • Any substance use by the child? Parent
Reminder. Only record a negative finding for items you have actually asked about — do not assume absence.

9Family Perception & Functional Impairment

  • What do you (the family) think is causing the child's problem? Parent
  • How has the family been responding to the problem? Parent
  • How has the problem affected the child's daily life at home? Parent
  • How has it affected the child's academic and school activities? Parent
  • How has it affected the child's friendships and social life? Both
  • Is the child able to manage age-appropriate self-care and routine? Parent
  • Compared with before the problem started, what can the child no longer do as well? Parent

10Mental Status Examination (child)

Questions to put directly to the child during the interview, adapted to age. General appearance and behaviour are observed; the items below are the ones you actually ask.

Orientation & general

  • What is your name and how old are you? Child
  • Do you know where we are right now? Child
  • Who came with you here today? Child
  • Do you know what day / time of day it is? (older child) Child

Attention, memory & thinking

  • Can you repeat these words / numbers after me? (immediate attention) Child
  • A little later — do you remember the words I told you earlier? (delayed recall) Child
  • Can you count / say the days of the week? (age-appropriate) Child
  • How are an apple and a banana alike? (abstract thinking, older child) Child

Mood, feelings & worries

  • How have you been feeling lately — happy, okay, or sad most days? Child
  • Are the things you used to enjoy still fun for you? Child
  • Is there anything that makes you feel scared or worried a lot? Child
  • Do you ever feel angry or upset — and what happens then? Child

Child's own view & projective questions

  • Why do you think you came to see me today (child's version of the problem)? Child
  • If you could have any three wishes, what would they be? (3-wish test) Child
  • Who do you like to spend time with, and who do you not like being with? Child
  • (Gently, age-appropriately) Do you ever hear voices or see things when no one is around? Child
Also observe (not asked): appearance, hygiene, eye contact, rapport, behaviour during interview, response to separation, parent–child interaction, activity level, motor behaviour (tics/mannerisms), speech & language, affect, and play-room behaviour.

11General Physical Examination

Recorded by the clinician / staff nurse. Ask/confirm the following before or alongside examination.

  • Has the child's height and weight been measured recently, and are they appropriate for age? Parent
  • Any concern with the child's hearing? Parent
  • Any birth marks or skin patches present since birth? Parent
  • What is the child's pubertal status (where age-appropriate)? Parent
  • Confirm consent for examination of vitals, thyroid, lymph nodes, eyes (retinal), chest, heart, abdomen, and nervous system. Parent
Educational use. This is a clinical-interviewing question bank for M.Phil. Clinical Psychology trainees, organized on the Clinical Case History – Child proforma. Select questions by the child's age and presenting problem; sensitive topics (trauma, self-harm, sexuality, substance use) must be asked only in a safe, private and developmentally suitable way.
