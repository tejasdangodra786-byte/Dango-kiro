M.Phil. Clinical Psychology

Clinical Case History – Child
Detailed Interview Questions

A detailed, section-by-section bank of questions to ask the child and the parents / informant while taking a child case history. Difficult or technical terms are followed by a simple meaning in brackets. Organized in the exact order of the Clinical Case History – Child proforma (proforma = the standard printed case-history form).

Ask the Parent / Informant Ask the Child Ask Both
Made by Tejas Dangodra

Sections (proforma order)

  1. Socio-Demographic Data
  2. Chief Complaints
  3. Family History
  4. Personal History
  5. Schooling History
  6. General Temperament & Personality
  7. History of Present Illness
  8. Treatment & Negative History
  9. Family Perception & Functional Impairment
  10. Mental Status Examination (child)
  11. General Physical Examination
How to use. Ask an open question first, then probe for detail (probe = ask a follow-up question to get more information): when it started, how often, where it happens, what makes it better or worse, and how it affects daily life. The colour tags show who to direct each question to. Difficult terms are explained in brackets. Not every question is needed in every case — choose according to the child's age and the presenting problem (the main reason the family came).

1Socio-Demographic Data (basic background details of the child and family)

Identifying details as listed on the proforma. Confirm with hospital records where available.

  • What is the child's full name? Parent
  • What are the names of the child's parents? Parent
  • What is the child's date of birth, and current age? Parent
  • What is the child's gender (as registered in the hospital)? Parent
  • What is the child's religion and cultural background (ask only if relevant to care)? Parent
  • What is the child's educational status (is the child in school, and how far studied) — and in which class? Parent
  • What is the family's socio-economic status (the family's money/income level — low, middle, or high)? Parent
  • What is the main source of income in the family, and who earns? Parent
  • What is the place of residence (where the family lives — town/village) and the full address? Parent
  • What are the contact number, UHID number (the unique hospital ID number), and Aadhar number, for records? Parent
  • What is the date of admission / assessment (the date of today's evaluation)? Parent
  • What is the source of referral (who sent you here — self, school, doctor, or someone else)? Parent
  • Who worked up the case (the trainee/clinician recording the history)? Parent

Informant & reliability (informant = the person giving the history; reliability = how trustworthy that information is)

  • What is your relationship with the child? Informant
  • Do you live in the same house as the child? Informant
  • How much time do you spend with the child each day? Informant
  • How long have you known the child (duration of acquaintance = for how long you have known the child)? Informant
  • How well do you know the child's early development, behaviour, and school history? Informant
  • Was there a time you were not living with the child, so you may not know some details? Informant
  • Is there another caregiver (another person who looks after the child) — like the other parent, a grandparent, or the teacher — who could add to or confirm this history? Informant

2Chief Complaints (the main problems that brought the family in)

Get the presenting problems in the informant's own words, then ask when each started and how long it has lasted.

  • What brought you here today? Parent
  • What problems or changes have you noticed in the child? Parent
  • Can you tell me about each problem in your own words? Parent
  • Which of these problems worries you the most? Parent
  • When did you first notice each problem? Parent
  • How long has each problem been present (its duration)? Parent
  • In what order did the problems appear (which one came first)? Parent
  • Why did you decide to seek help now, rather than earlier or later? Parent
  • (To the child, in simple words) Do you know why you have come here today? Child
  • (To the child) Is there anything that is troubling you or that you would like help with? Child

3Family History

Covers the genogram (a family tree diagram showing members, ages, and illnesses), family history of illness, living arrangements, caregivers, description of parents, home and neighbourhood, family relationships, and child-rearing practices (how the child is being brought up).

Family history of illness

  • Has anyone in the family (parents, brothers/sisters, grandparents, uncles/aunts) had any mental or psychiatric illness (illness affecting mood, thinking, or behaviour)? Parent
  • Is there any family history of epilepsy or fits (seizures — sudden bursts of electrical activity in the brain causing shaking or blackouts)? Parent
  • Is there any family history of developmental problems (slow development compared with other children), intellectual disability (significantly below-average learning and reasoning ability), or learning difficulties? Parent
  • Has anyone in the family had a problem with alcohol, tobacco, or drugs (substance use)? Parent
  • Is there any family history of suicide (ending one's own life) or attempts at it? Parent
  • Is there any long-term (chronic) medical illness or genetic disorder (a condition passed down in families through genes) in the family? Parent
  • Are the child's parents related by blood (consanguinity = parents who are blood relatives, e.g. cousins)? Parent

Family structure & living arrangements

  • Who all live in the house with the child? Parent
  • Is it a nuclear family (parents and children only), joint family (several related families living together), or extended family? Parent
  • How many brothers and sisters does the child have? Parent
  • What is the child's birth order (whether first-born, second, youngest, etc.)? Parent
  • Who is the child's primary caregiver (the main person who looks after the child)? Parent
  • Has the child ever lived away from the parents or with other relatives? For how long, and why? Parent

Description of parents, home & relationships

  • Can you describe each parent — age, education, occupation, health, and general nature/personality? Parent
  • How would you describe the relationship between the two parents? Parent
  • How does the child get along with the mother? Both
  • How does the child get along with the father? Both
  • How does the child get along with brothers and sisters? Both
  • What is the home atmosphere usually like — calm, tense, loving, or full of arguments? Parent
  • What is the neighbourhood like where the family lives (safe, crowded, supportive)? Parent
  • Have there been any recent family stressors (difficult events causing worry — separation, death, illness, money problems, or major fights)? Parent
  • (Where relevant, ask sensitively) Is there any violence or frequent conflict at home? Parent

Child-rearing practices

  • Who mainly takes care of the child's daily needs (food, bath, sleep, school)? Parent
  • How is the child usually disciplined (corrected or taught right behaviour) when he/she misbehaves? Parent
  • Do the caregivers agree on how to bring up and discipline the child, or do they differ? Parent
  • How affectionate and involved are the parents in the child's daily life? Parent
  • How much time does the family spend together with the child each day? Parent
  • Is there any other information about the family that you feel is important? Parent

4Personal History (the child's own life story — from pregnancy to now)

Covers pregnancy, birth, early growth, developmental milestones, current development, behaviour, and physical illness. Ask the mother where possible.

Prenatal history (prenatal = the time during pregnancy, before birth)

  • Was this pregnancy planned (wanted and expected) or unplanned? Mother
  • How old were you (mother) when you had this pregnancy? Mother
  • Did you have regular antenatal check-ups (doctor visits during pregnancy)? Mother
  • Did you have any illness during pregnancy — high fever, infections, high blood pressure (hypertension), diabetes (high blood sugar), thyroid problems (a gland in the neck that controls energy and growth), or fits? Mother
  • Did you take any medicines during pregnancy? If yes, which ones? Mother
  • Was there any use of tobacco, alcohol, or other substances during pregnancy? Mother
  • Were there any nutrition problems (not getting enough healthy food) during pregnancy? Mother
  • Was there any major emotional stress, injury, or accident during pregnancy? Mother
  • Was there any bleeding, or a threatened abortion (a scare of losing the pregnancy early)? Mother
  • Was there any exposure to X-rays/radiation or harmful chemicals (where relevant)? Mother

Perinatal & neonatal history (perinatal = around the time of birth; neonatal = the first few weeks after birth)

  • Was the baby born on time (full-term) or early (preterm = born before the expected date)? Mother
  • Was it a normal delivery or a caesarean (operation to deliver the baby through the tummy)? Mother
  • If it was an assisted or operative delivery, what was the reason? Mother
  • What was the baby's weight at birth (birth weight)? Mother
  • Did the baby cry immediately after birth (birth cry present or absent)? Mother
  • Did the baby need any resuscitation (emergency help to start breathing) or oxygen at birth? Mother
  • Was the baby admitted to the NICU (Neonatal Intensive Care Unit — special hospital care for newborns)? If yes, why and for how long? Mother
  • Were there any fits/seizures in the newborn period? Mother
  • Was there any jaundice (yellow colour of skin and eyes), and did it need treatment? Mother
  • Was there any bluish colour (cyanosis = bluish skin from low oxygen) or breathing problem? Mother
  • Were there any feeding problems or infections in the first weeks? Mother

Postnatal & early development (postnatal = the period after birth)

  • How was the baby's health in the first months of life? Parent
  • Was breastfeeding started easily, and were there any feeding difficulties? Parent
  • Was the baby easy to comfort, or unusually irritable (cried a lot / hard to settle)? Parent

Developmental milestones — motor (milestones = skills a child gains at certain ages; motor = movement and body control)

  • At what age did the child hold the head steady (head control)? Parent
  • At what age did the child sit without support? Parent
  • At what age did the child crawl and then stand? Parent
  • At what age did the child start walking on his/her own? Parent
  • At what age could the child pick up small things using finger and thumb (fine motor = small, precise hand movements)? Parent
  • At what age did the child start scribbling or drawing? Parent

Developmental milestones — language (speech)

  • At what age did the child start babbling (making baby sounds like "ba-ba", "da-da")? Parent
  • At what age did the child say the first meaningful word (a word with real meaning, like "mama")? Parent
  • At what age did the child join two words together (like "want water")? Parent
  • At what age did the child speak in short sentences? Parent
  • At what age could the child hold a simple conversation? Parent
  • Is the child's speech clear and easy to understand for the age? Parent

Developmental milestones — social & adaptive (social = getting along with people; adaptive = self-care skills)

  • At what age did the child first smile back at people (social smile)? Parent
  • At what age did the child show stranger anxiety (becoming shy or upset with unfamiliar people)? Parent
  • Did the child point at things to show you and share attention (joint attention = looking at the same thing together and enjoying it)? At what age? Parent
  • At what age did the child start pretend play (make-believe play, like feeding a doll)? Parent
  • At what age did the child start playing with other children? Parent
  • At what age could the child feed himself/herself? Parent
  • At what age was the child toilet-trained (learned to use the toilet) during the day and at night? Parent
  • At what age could the child dress and manage bathing/grooming with or without help? Parent

Developmental regression (regression = losing a skill the child had already learned)

  • Did the child ever learn a skill and then lose it — for example words he/she used to say, ways of playing or mixing with others, being toilet-trained, or things learned at school? Parent
  • If yes, which skill was lost, at what age, and did it happen slowly or quickly? Parent
  • Was there any illness, high fever, or fit around the time the skill was lost? Parent

Current developmental status, behaviour & physical illness

  • Compared with other children of the same age, how is the child's overall development now? Parent
  • How is the child's general intelligence (overall thinking and learning ability), speech, and language at present? Parent
  • How independent is the child in self-help skills (eating, dressing, toileting, bathing)? Parent
  • How would you describe the child's usual behaviour — mostly easy-going (calm and cooperative), or with frequent temper tantrums (sudden outbursts of anger/crying)? Parent
  • Has the child had any serious physical illness, hospital stay, injury, or operation? Parent
  • Any head injury, brain infection (like meningitis = infection of the coverings of the brain), or high fever with fits? Parent
  • Does the child have any long-term (chronic) illness, allergy, or regular medicine? Parent
  • Are the child's hearing and eyesight normal? Parent

5Schooling History

Follows the proforma's detailed schooling items, plus habits/interests and sexual history. Combine the accounts of the parent, the child, and the teacher where possible.

School entry & adjustment

  • At what age did the child start school (age of initiation)? Parent
  • How did the child settle in / adjust when starting school? Parent
  • Has there been any change of school, medium of instruction (the language teaching is done in), or syllabus? If yes, why? Parent
  • Do you like going to school? Child
  • What do you like most and least about school? Child

Academic performance (how well the child does in studies)

  • How has the child performed in studies overall (scholastic performance)? Parent
  • How does the child manage reading specifically? Both
  • How does the child manage writing and spelling? Both
  • How does the child manage arithmetic (basic maths — counting and calculations)? Both
  • Which subjects are easy for you, and which are hard? Child
  • What are your expectations about the child's performance? Parent
  • Are you satisfied with how the child is doing at present? Parent
  • Are your expectations in line with the child's ability (appropriate expectations)? Parent
  • How does the child cope with homework and examinations? Parent
  • Has the child ever repeated a class (stayed in the same class an extra year)? Parent

Behaviour, peers & teacher's view

  • How does the child behave in the classroom? Parent
  • How is the child's attention and concentration (ability to focus and keep focusing) in class? Both
  • How does the child get along with classmates (peer relationship = relationships with children of the same age)? Child
  • Does the child take part in extra-curricular activities (activities besides studies — sports, drawing, music)? Both
  • What does the teacher say about the child (the teacher's perceptions and concerns)? Parent
  • Has the child been bullied (repeatedly teased, threatened, or hurt by others), or bullied other children? Child
  • How is the child's school attendance? Parent
  • Does the child ever refuse to go to school (school refusal)? If yes, why? Parent
  • Are there any other problems related to school? Parent
  • If schooling was stopped (discontinued), when did it stop and why? Parent
  • Is a school report card available, and what does it show? Parent
  • Is the child getting any special education (teaching designed for children with extra learning needs) or remedial teaching (extra help to catch up in weak areas)? Parent

Habits, interests & sexual history

  • What are the child's habits, interests, hobbies, and special talents? Both
  • What do you like to do in your free time? Child
  • (Where age-appropriate) Have there been any signs of puberty (the body changes of growing up)? Parent
  • (For girls, where relevant) Has menstruation started (menarche = the first monthly period), and is it regular? Parent
  • (Only if clinically needed, asked privately and sensitively) Is there any concern about age-inappropriate sexual behaviour, or exposure to sexual content? Parent

6General Temperament & Personality Attributes (temperament = the child's inborn, usual nature and mood style)

Captures the child's usual style across situations.

  • How would you describe the child's usual emotional nature (emotionality = how strongly and easily the child shows feelings)? Parent
  • How is the child's attention and concentration in everyday activities? Parent
  • Is the child generally very active, average, or calm (activity level)? Parent
  • How does the child show anger — is there any aggression (hitting, hurting, or attacking others)? Parent
  • Is the child sociable and outgoing, or shy and withdrawn (sociability)? Parent
  • Are the child's sleep and meal timings regular (rhythmicity = how regular and predictable daily routines are)? Parent
  • How motivated (keen and willing) is the child to try and finish tasks? Parent
  • Is the child trustworthy and dependable (reliable — can be counted on) for age-appropriate responsibilities? Parent
  • How is the child's sense of right and wrong (moral standards)? Parent
  • How does the child cope with change or new situations (adaptability = how easily the child adjusts)? Parent
  • How does the child react to new people or places — approach happily or hold back (approach/withdrawal)? Parent
  • What are the child's particular strengths or good qualities? Parent

7History of Present Illness (the full story of the current problem, from start to now)

Builds up the total duration, onset, course, progress, and the factors around the problem, plus how basic functions are affected. Begin with the anchor line: "The child was apparently well until…"

Onset, course & progression

  • Until when was the child apparently well and behaving as expected? Parent
  • What is the total duration (how long the problem has been present altogether)? Parent
  • What was the very first change you noticed? Parent
  • How old was the child when it started? Parent
  • Did the problem start suddenly or slowly — was the onset (the way it began) abrupt/acute (sudden), subacute, or insidious (very gradual, hard to notice at first)? Parent
  • Was anything happening in the child's life around that time (a possible trigger)? Parent
  • Since it began, does it come and go, or is it there all the time — is the course (the pattern over time) episodic (in on-and-off spells), chronic (long-lasting and continuous), or chronic with fluctuation (continuous but going up and down)? Parent
  • Overall, is the problem staying the same, getting worse, or improving — is the progress static (unchanged), deteriorating (getting worse), or improved? Parent
  • How often does it happen (frequency)? Parent
  • How severe is it — mild, moderate, or severe? Parent

Detailed probing for each main complaint

  • What usually happens just before the problem or episode (a single occurrence of the behaviour/symptom)? Parent
  • What exactly happens during it? Parent
  • How does the child behave afterward? Parent
  • Does it happen at home, at school, or in both places (situational variation = whether it changes with the situation)? Parent
  • Does anything make it better? Parent
  • Does anything make it worse? Parent
  • Are there any other symptoms happening along with it (associated symptoms)? Parent
  • (To the child, in simple words) Can you tell me what happens when this occurs, and how you feel? Child

Predisposing, precipitating & perpetuating factors

  • Was there anything about the child or family that may have made this more likely (predisposing factor = a background factor that increases the risk)? Parent
  • Was there a specific event that seemed to set it off (precipitating factor = the trigger that started it)? Parent
  • Is anything keeping the problem going or making it continue (perpetuating factor = what maintains the problem)? Parent

Biological & occupational functioning (biological = basic body functions; occupational for a child = school and daily activities)

  • Since the problem began, how has the child's sleep been? Parent
  • How has the child's appetite (desire to eat) and eating been? Parent
  • How is the child managing self-care since the problem started? Parent
  • Is there any change in bowel or bladder control (passing stool/urine)? Parent
  • How has school attendance and the daily routine been affected? Parent

Associated symptom screen (a quick check for other symptoms — ask those relevant to the case)

  • Is the child overactive or restless — unable to sit still (hyperactivity)? Parent
  • Does the child have trouble paying attention, listening, or finishing tasks (inattention)? Parent
  • Is the child impulsive (acts quickly without thinking — interrupts, can't wait for a turn)? Parent
  • Is the child argumentative, defiant (refuses to obey), or having frequent temper outbursts? Parent
  • Have there been problems like aggression, lying, stealing, or staying away from home or school (conduct problems)? Parent
  • Does the child worry a lot, have specific fears, or get very upset when away from you (separation anxiety = distress at being apart from parents)? Both
  • Has the child seemed sad, irritable, or lost interest in play and activities (low mood)? Both
  • Are there repetitive thoughts the child cannot stop (obsessions = unwanted thoughts that keep coming), or actions the child feels must be done again and again (compulsions = repeated acts like washing or checking)? Child
  • (Gently, in simple words) Does the child ever hear voices or see things that others do not, or hold very unusual beliefs? Both
  • How is the child's sleep — bedtime, waking at night, nightmares, sleepwalking, snoring, or daytime sleepiness? Parent
  • Is there any unusual eating, very fussy/restricted eating, or eating of non-food items (pica = eating things like chalk, mud, or paper)? Parent
  • Any bedwetting (enuresis = wetting the bed/clothes beyond the usual age), soiling (encopresis = passing stool in clothes), or constipation? Parent
  • Is the child unusually sensitive to sounds, textures, lights, smells, or tastes (sensory sensitivity)? Parent
  • How much screen time (time on phone/TV/tablet/games) does the child have, and is it affecting sleep or studies? Parent
  • (Adolescents, asked privately) Have you come across or tried tobacco, alcohol, or any other substances? Child
  • (Only asked sensitively) Has the child been through any frightening or very upsetting experience (trauma = a deeply distressing event)? Both
  • (Safety check) Does the child ever talk about not wanting to live, or about hurting himself/herself or others? Both

8Treatment History & Negative History

Treatment history (till date) (any help or treatment taken so far)

  • Have you consulted anyone for this problem before — a doctor, psychologist, special educator, or therapist? Parent
  • Were you given any diagnosis (a name/label for the condition) earlier? Parent
  • Was any medicine started? If yes, which medicine, what dose, and for how long? Parent
  • Did the child take the treatment regularly (adherence = how well the treatment was followed)? Parent
  • Were there any side effects (unwanted effects of the medicine)? Parent
  • Has the child had any psychological therapy or counselling (talking-based treatment)? Parent
  • Has the child had special education, occupational therapy (therapy to build daily-living and hand skills), speech therapy (therapy to improve talking/language), or behavioural intervention (a plan to change specific behaviours)? Parent
  • Did any of these treatments help — and in what way? Parent

Negative history (important problems you check for and confirm are absent)

  • Has the child ever had seizures/fits, fainting, or loss of consciousness (blacking out / becoming unaware)? Parent
  • Has there been any significant head injury or brain infection? Parent
  • Any staring spells (brief episodes of blank staring), unusual movements, talking to self, or smiling to self? Parent
  • Any period of over-happy or over-excited mood, over-talkativeness, or unusually high energy? Parent
  • Any sign of hearing or seeing things that are not there (hallucinations = sensing things that are not actually present)? Parent
  • Any previous self-harm (deliberately hurting oneself) or suicide attempts? Parent
  • Any use of substances (tobacco, alcohol, drugs) by the child? Parent
Reminder. Only mark a problem as "absent" if you have actually asked about it — do not assume.

9Family Perception & Functional Impairment (perception = what the family thinks/feels; functional impairment = how much the problem affects daily life)

  • What do you (the family) think is causing the child's problem? Parent
  • How has the family been reacting to the problem? Parent
  • How has the problem affected the child's daily life at home? Parent
  • How has it affected the child's studies and school activities? Parent
  • How has it affected the child's friendships and social life? Both
  • Is the child able to manage age-appropriate self-care and daily routine? Parent
  • Compared with before the problem started, what can the child no longer do as well? Parent

10Mental Status Examination (child) (MSE = a check of the child's current thinking, mood, and behaviour during the interview)

Questions to put directly to the child, adapted to age. General appearance and behaviour are observed; the items below are the ones you actually ask.

Orientation (orientation = awareness of who/where/when one is) & general

  • What is your name, and how old are you? Child
  • Do you know where we are right now? Child
  • Who came with you here today? Child
  • (Older child) Do you know what day it is, or the time of day? Child

Attention, memory & thinking

  • Can you repeat these words / numbers after me? (immediate attention = holding information for a moment) Child
  • A little later — do you remember the words I told you earlier? (delayed recall = remembering after a gap) Child
  • Can you count for me, or say the days of the week? (age-appropriate) Child
  • (Older child) How are two things alike — for example, an apple and a banana? (abstract thinking = seeing the general idea behind things) Child

Mood, feelings & worries

  • How have you been feeling lately — happy, okay, or sad most days? Child
  • Are the things you used to enjoy still fun for you? Child
  • Is there anything that makes you feel scared or worried a lot? Child
  • Do you ever feel very angry or upset — and what happens then? Child

Child's own view & projective questions (projective = open questions that let the child reveal inner feelings)

  • Why do you think you came to see me today? (child's version of the problem) Child
  • If you could have any three wishes, what would they be? (the 3-wish test) Child
  • Who do you most like to spend time with, and who do you not like being with? Child
  • (Gently, in simple words) Do you ever hear voices, or see things, when no one else is around? Child
Also observe (not asked aloud): appearance and grooming (neatness), eye contact, rapport (the comfortable connection between child and examiner), behaviour during the interview, response to separation from the parent, parent–child interaction, activity level, motor behaviour (any tics = sudden repeated movements/sounds or mannerisms = odd repeated habits), speech and language, affect (the emotion shown on the face and in manner), and behaviour in the play room.

11General Physical Examination (a head-to-toe body check by the clinician/nurse)

Recorded by the clinician / staff nurse. Ask or confirm the following before or during the examination.

  • Have the child's height and weight been measured recently, and are they right for the age? Parent
  • Is there any concern about the child's hearing? Parent
  • Are there any birth marks or skin patches present since birth? Parent
  • What is the child's pubertal status (stage of physical growing-up), where age-appropriate? Parent
  • Do you consent to a check of vitals (pulse, temperature, blood pressure), thyroid, lymph nodes (small glands that swell with infection), eyes, chest, heart, abdomen (tummy), and nervous system? Parent
Educational use. This is a clinical-interviewing question bank for M.Phil. Clinical Psychology trainees, organized on the Clinical Case History – Child proforma. Choose questions by the child's age and the presenting problem; sensitive topics (trauma, self-harm, sexuality, substance use) must be asked only in a safe, private, and developmentally suitable way.
