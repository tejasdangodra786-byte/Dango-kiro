## 1. INTRODUCTION & HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

### Developer
- **Dr. Joseph M. Sacks** and **Dr. Sidney Levy** (1950)
- Developed at the **New York Veterans Administration Mental Hygiene Service**

### Historical Evolution of Sentence Completion Methods
| Year | Contributor | Contribution |
|------|------------|--------------|
| 1897 | Hermann Ebbinghaus | First sentence completion test (intelligence testing for children) |
| 1916 | Carl Jung | Word associations to assess personality |
| 1928 | Arthur Payne | Applied SCT for guidance in career counseling |
| 1930s | Alexander Tendler | Emotional reaction tests using "I" stems |
| 1950 | Sacks & Levy | Developed the SSCT with 60 items across 4 domains |
| 1950 | Rotter & Rafferty | Developed the Rotter Incomplete Sentences Blank (RISB) |
| 1957 | Amanda Rhode | Created first validated personality SCT |

### Original Reference
Sacks, J. M., & Levy, S. (1950). The Sentence Completion Test. In L. E. Abt & L. Bellak (Eds.), *Projective Psychology: Clinical Approaches to the Total Personality* (pp. 357–402). Alfred A. Knopf.

## 2. NATURE OF THE TEST

### Classification
- **Type:** Projective Personality Test (Semi-structured)
- **Category:** Sentence Completion Method
- **Approach:** Qualitative and Quantitative (Rating Scale)
- **Format:** Paper-and-pencil / Written response
- **Items:** 60 incomplete sentence stems
- **Time:** 20–40 minutes (administration); No strict time limit
- **Age Group:** Adolescents and Adults (typically 12+ years)
- **Administration:** Individual or Group
- **Language:** English (Original); Hindi adaptations available in India

### Theoretical Basis
The SSCT is rooted in the **psychodynamic/projective tradition**. It operates on the hypothesis that when a person completes an incomplete sentence, they reveal conscious and unconscious attitudes, desires, fears, and conflicts. The ambiguous stimulus (incomplete sentence) allows the subject to "project" inner psychological material.

### How It Differs from Other SCTs
| Feature | SSCT (Sacks) | RISB (Rotter) |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Items | 60 | 40 |
| Scoring | 0-2 disturbance scale | 7-point conflict-positive scale |
| Areas | 15 attitudes in 4 domains | General adjustment |
| Approach | Qualitative + Quantitative | Primarily Quantitative |

## 3. PURPOSE & APPLICATIONS

### Primary Purpose
To explore significant areas of emotional adjustment and obtain clinically meaningful material about an individual's personality structure, attitudes, and interpersonal dynamics.

### Specific Aims
1. Assess personality traits and dominant personality trends
2. Identify areas of emotional disturbance/conflict
3. Explore attitudes towards significant relationships (parents, family, peers, authority)
4. Understand self-concept and self-perception
5. Evaluate sexual adjustment and attitudes
6. Identify fears, guilt, goals, and ambitions
7. Provide leads for therapeutic intervention

### Clinical Applications
- **Clinical Psychology:** Personality assessment, treatment planning
- **Counseling:** Identifying problem areas, understanding client dynamics
- **Psychiatry:** Supplement to diagnostic interviews
- **Forensic Psychology:** Personality evaluation in legal cases
- **Industrial/Organizational Psychology:** Personnel selection, screening
- **Educational Settings:** Understanding student adjustment issues
- **Research:** Studying attitudes, personality dynamics across populations

### Two Uses of the Test (as described by Sacks & Levy)
1. **As an independent source of information** — providing clinical data on its own
2. **As an instrument of comparison** — used alongside interviews, other tests to validate/supplement findings

## 7. ADMINISTRATION PROCEDURE

### Materials Required
- SSCT questionnaire (printed form with 60 items)
- Pen/pencil
- Recording sheet (for examiner)
- Stopwatch (optional, for noting time taken)
- Quiet, distraction-free environment

### Pre-Administration
1. **Establish Rapport:** Build comfortable relationship with the subject
2. **Record Demographic Details:** Name, Age, Sex, Education, Occupation, Date, Time Began/Finished
3. **Explain Purpose:** "This is a test to understand your feelings and attitudes about different things in life"

### Instructions to Subject (Read Verbatim):
> "Below are sixty partly completed sentences. Read each one and finish it by writing the first thing that comes to your mind. Work as quickly as you can. If you cannot complete an item, circle the number and return to it later."

### Hindi Instructions (हिंदी निर्देश):
> "नीचे साठ अधूरे वाक्य दिए गए हैं। प्रत्येक वाक्य को पढ़ें और जो पहली बात आपके मन में आए उसे लिखकर वाक्य पूरा करें। जितनी तेज़ी से हो सके काम करें। यदि किसी वाक्य को पूरा न कर सकें तो उसके नंबर पर गोला लगाएँ और बाद में वापस आएँ।"

### Administration Modes

#### Individual Administration:
- Preferred in clinical settings
- Allows observation of non-verbal behavior, hesitations, emotional reactions
- Examiner can note latency (time taken per item)
- Can be given orally to those unable to write (examiner records responses)

#### Group Administration:
- Can be given to groups (screening, research)
- Less observation of individual reactions
- Requires minimal supervision
- Useful in organizational/educational settings

### Important Observations During Administration
Record the following:
- **Hesitations:** Which items caused pauses?
- **Omissions:** Which items were left blank or circled?
- **Erasures/Corrections:** Changes in responses
- **Emotional reactions:** Laughter, sighing, discomfort, anger
- **Sequence of completion:** Did subject skip around?
- **Total time taken:** Note time began and finished
- **Quality of handwriting:** Changes in pressure or legibility

### Special Considerations
- No time limit, but encourage quick responses
- If subject asks for clarification, say: "Whatever it means to you"
- If subject refuses an item, do not force; note refusal
- For illiterate/semi-literate subjects: Read items aloud, record responses verbatim
- Maintain confidentiality and ethical standards

## 8. SCORING SYSTEM

### Rating Scale (0–2 Point Scale)

Each of the 15 attitude areas receives a **disturbance rating** based on analysis of the 4 items belonging to that area:

| Rating | Label | Description |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| **2** | **Severely Disturbed** | Appears to require therapeutic aid in handling emotional conflicts in this area |
| **1** | **Mildly Disturbed** | Has emotional conflicts in this area, but appears able to handle them without therapeutic aid |
| **0** | **No Significant Disturbance** | No significant disturbance noted in this area |
| **X** | **Unknown/Insufficient** | Insufficient evidence to rate (items left blank or responses too vague) |

### Step-by-Step Scoring Procedure

#### Step 1: Organize Responses by Attitude Area
Group the 4 responses belonging to each of the 15 attitude areas together.

**Example — Attitude toward Father (Items 1, 16, 31, 46):**
- Item 1: "I feel that my father seldom... *shows affection*"
- Item 16: "If my father would only... *listen to me*"
- Item 31: "I wish my father... *was more understanding*"
- Item 46: "I feel that my father is... *a strict man*"

#### Step 2: Evaluate Content of Each Group
Read all 4 responses together and determine:
- What attitude does the subject express?
- Is there emotional conflict or disturbance?
- How severe is the disturbance?

#### Step 3: Assign Rating (0, 1, or 2)

### Scoring Examples for Each Rating Level:

#### Rating = 2 (Severely Disturbed):
**Attitude toward Father:**
- "I feel that my father seldom... *cared about me, he ruined my life*"
- "If my father would only... *die, I would be free*"
- "I wish my father... *had never been born*"
- "I feel that my father is... *a cruel monster who destroyed our family*"
→ **Score: 2** — Extreme hostility, deep unresolved conflict requiring therapeutic intervention

#### Rating = 1 (Mildly Disturbed):
**Attitude toward Father:**
- "I feel that my father seldom... *had time for us*"
- "If my father would only... *be more open-minded*"
- "I wish my father... *spent more time at home*"
- "I feel that my father is... *a good man but distant*"
→ **Score: 1** — Some dissatisfaction/conflict, but manageable without therapy

#### Rating = 0 (No Disturbance):
**Attitude toward Father:**
- "I feel that my father seldom... *gets angry without reason*"
- "If my father would only... *take better care of his health*"
- "I wish my father... *all the happiness in the world*"
- "I feel that my father is... *a wonderful person and my role model*"
→ **Score: 0** — Positive, warm, no conflict noted

### Total Score Calculation
- **Maximum possible score per area:** 4 items × 2 = 8 (for each major domain with multiple attitudes)
- **Maximum total score:** 15 attitudes × 2 = 30
- **Cutoff for conflict (per attitude):** Score of 4 (across the 4 items combined) may indicate presence of conflict in some scoring variants
- **General interpretation:** Higher total score = more areas of emotional disturbance

### Additional Quantitative Indicators
- **Number of areas with score 2** — Indicates breadth of severe disturbance
- **Number of areas with score 1** — Indicates mild conflict areas
- **Domain totals** — Which major area (Family/Sex/Interpersonal/Self) is most disturbed?

## 9. INTERPRETATION GUIDELINES

### General Principles of Interpretation

1. **Holistic Approach:** Never interpret a single response in isolation; always consider the pattern across all 4 items in an attitude area
2. **Content Analysis:** Focus on themes, emotional tone, recurring patterns
3. **Cross-Area Comparison:** Look at relationships between different attitude areas
4. **Contextual Understanding:** Consider the subject's demographic background, life circumstances
5. **Behavioral Observation:** Integrate test responses with behavioral observations during administration

### Three Levels of Interpretation (Sacks & Levy, 1950)

#### Level 1: Attitudes Showing Disturbance
- Identify which of the 15 areas show ratings of 1 or 2
- Describe the nature of the conflict in each disturbed area
- Note the specific content/themes expressed

#### Level 2: Interrelationships Between Attitudes
- How do disturbed areas relate to each other?
- Example: Disturbed attitude toward father + disturbed attitude toward superiors = possible authority conflict
- Example: Disturbed attitude toward women + disturbed sexual attitudes = possible intimacy issues

#### Level 3: Personality Structure and Dynamics
- What does the overall pattern suggest about the person's:
  - Defense mechanisms
  - Coping style
  - Object relations
  - Core conflicts
  - Ego strength

### Qualitative Analysis Framework

#### A. Content Themes to Look For:
- **Aggression/Hostility:** Expressions of anger, resentment, desire to harm
- **Anxiety/Fear:** Worries, phobias, insecurities
- **Depression:** Hopelessness, sadness, worthlessness
- **Dependency:** Need for approval, inability to be independent
- **Withdrawal:** Avoidance, isolation, distancing
- **Ambivalence:** Contradictory feelings toward same object
- **Idealization:** Unrealistic positive views
- **Devaluation:** Excessive negative views

#### B. Response Style Analysis:
- **Brevity vs. Elaboration:** Very short responses may indicate guardedness; lengthy responses may indicate anxiety or need to explain
- **Humor/Sarcasm:** May indicate defense mechanism
- **Stereotyped/Clichéd responses:** May indicate superficiality or avoidance
- **Omissions/Refusals:** May indicate strong conflict in that area
- **Third-person responses:** May indicate distancing/projection
- **Qualified responses:** "Sometimes," "maybe" — may indicate ambivalence

#### C. Pattern Analysis Across Domains:

| Pattern | Possible Interpretation |
|---------|----------------------|
| All family areas disturbed | Pervasive family conflict |
| Father + Superiors disturbed | Authority conflict pattern |
| Mother + Women + Sex disturbed | Issues with femininity/intimacy |
| Fears + Guilt + Past disturbed | Neurotic pattern, unresolved past |
| Goals + Future + Abilities disturbed | Low self-efficacy, poor self-concept |
| All interpersonal areas disturbed | Social maladjustment |

### Specific Interpretation Guidelines Per Area

#### 1. Attitude toward Mother (Items 14, 29, 44, 59)
- Look for: warmth vs. hostility, dependence vs. independence, idealization vs. devaluation
- Disturbed pattern: "My mother... never understood me," "I like my mother but... she is controlling"

#### 2. Attitude toward Father (Items 1, 16, 31, 46)
- Look for: respect vs. resentment, closeness vs. distance, acceptance vs. rejection
- Disturbed pattern: "I wish my father... was never around," "I feel my father is... a failure"

#### 3. Attitude toward Family Unit (Items 12, 27, 42, 57)
- Look for: cohesion vs. fragmentation, belonging vs. alienation, support vs. neglect
- Disturbed pattern: "My family treats me like... an outsider," "Compared with most families, mine... is broken"

#### 4. Attitude toward Women/Men (Items 10, 25, 40, 55)
- Look for: respect vs. contempt, trust vs. suspicion, realistic vs. distorted views
- Disturbed pattern: "I think most girls... are manipulative," "What I like least about women... everything"

#### 5. Attitude toward Heterosexual Relationships (Items 11, 26, 41, 56)
- Look for: comfort vs. anxiety, positive vs. negative views of marriage/intimacy
- Disturbed pattern: "My sex life... is non-existent and miserable," "My feeling about married life is... it's a trap"

#### 6. Attitude toward Friends (Items 8, 23, 38, 53)
- Look for: trust vs. suspicion, sociability vs. isolation, satisfaction vs. disappointment
- Disturbed pattern: "I don't like people who... everyone basically," "When I'm not around, my friends... talk about me"

#### 7. Attitude toward Superiors (Items 6, 21, 36, 51)
- Look for: respect vs. resentment, compliance vs. rebellion, comfort vs. anxiety
- Disturbed pattern: "The people over me... make my life miserable," "When I see the boss coming... I hide"

#### 8. Attitude toward People Supervised (Items 4, 19, 34, 49)
- Look for: empathy vs. domination, fairness vs. exploitation, comfort with authority
- Disturbed pattern: "When I give orders, I... feel guilty and confused"

#### 9. Attitude toward Colleagues (Items 13, 28, 43, 58)
- Look for: cooperation vs. competition, trust vs. suspicion, belonging vs. alienation
- Disturbed pattern: "Those I work with are... against me," "People who work with me usually... avoid me"

#### 10. Fears (Items 7, 22, 37, 52)
- Look for: nature of fears (realistic vs. irrational), impact on functioning, awareness
- Disturbed pattern: "My fears sometimes force me to... stay in bed all day"

#### 11. Guilt Feelings (Items 15, 30, 45, 60)
- Look for: severity, content of guilt, ability to resolve, self-forgiveness
- Disturbed pattern: "The worst thing I ever did... cannot be forgiven"

#### 12. Goals (Items 3, 18, 33, 48)
- Look for: clarity, realism, motivation, alignment with abilities
- Disturbed pattern: "I could be perfectly happy if... I were someone else entirely"

#### 13. Attitude toward Own Abilities (Items 2, 17, 32, 47)
- Look for: confidence vs. self-doubt, realistic self-appraisal
- Disturbed pattern: "My greatest weakness is... everything about me"

#### 14. Attitude toward Past (Items 9, 24, 39, 54)
- Look for: nostalgia vs. trauma, integration vs. fixation, acceptance vs. regret
- Disturbed pattern: "My most vivid childhood memory... is being beaten"

#### 15. Attitude toward Future (Items 5, 20, 35, 50)
- Look for: optimism vs. pessimism, clarity vs. uncertainty, motivation vs. hopelessness
- Disturbed pattern: "To me the future looks... dark and hopeless"

## 12. PSYCHOMETRIC PROPERTIES

### Validity

#### Content Validity
- Items were developed by a team of psychologists at the New York VA Mental Hygiene Service
- Items systematically cover 15 attitude areas across 4 major domains
- Each area has 4 items to ensure even one reveals significant material, even with guarded subjects

#### Construct Validity
- The test is based on the projective hypothesis: ambiguous stimuli reveal underlying personality dynamics
- Supported by decades of clinical use confirming its utility in revealing attitudes and conflicts
- Research shows concordance between SSCT findings and clinical interview data

#### Concurrent Validity
- Sacks & Levy (1950) reported agreement between SSCT ratings and independent clinical evaluations
- In their validation study, clinicians' ratings from SSCT were compared with therapists' judgments:
  - Agreement rates varied across attitude areas
  - Overall satisfactory concordance for clinical use

#### Criticism
- As a projective test, formal statistical validation is challenging
- Some researchers question whether self-report completions truly access unconscious material
- Cultural bias in item content (originally American context)

### Reliability

#### Inter-Rater Reliability
- Sacks & Levy reported inter-rater reliability studies
- When trained raters use the scoring guidelines, agreement is generally satisfactory
- Greater consistency achieved when raters are trained together
- Subjective nature of scoring means reliability depends heavily on rater training

#### Test-Retest Reliability
- Limited formal test-retest data available
- Responses may vary based on:
  - Current emotional state
  - Relationship with examiner
  - Testing context and purpose

### Norms
- The SSCT is **not a standardized test** in the traditional psychometric sense
- There are no formal normative tables
- Interpretation relies on clinical judgment rather than normative comparison
- This is consistent with the projective test tradition

## 13. ADVANTAGES & LIMITATIONS

### Advantages

1. **Simple Administration:** Can be administered individually or in groups with minimal supervision
2. **Time-Efficient:** Takes only 20-40 minutes for completion
3. **Wide Coverage:** Covers 15 attitude areas across 4 major life domains
4. **Structured Projective Test:** More structured than TAT or Rorschach, easier to score
5. **Flexibility:** Can be adapted for different populations and languages
6. **Rich Clinical Data:** Provides both quantitative ratings and qualitative material
7. **Less Threatening:** Subjects often find it less anxiety-provoking than inkblot tests
8. **Comparison Tool:** Useful alongside other assessment tools for validation
9. **Minimal Training Required:** Can be administered by trained psychometrists
10. **Cost-Effective:** Does not require expensive materials
11. **Follow-up Utility:** Can be re-administered to assess therapeutic progress
12. **Group Screening:** Useful for screening large groups in organizational settings

### Limitations

1. **Subjectivity in Scoring:** Interpretation depends on clinical judgment of the examiner
2. **Lack of Formal Norms:** No standardized normative data available
3. **Cultural Bias:** Some items may not translate well across cultures
4. **Faking Possible:** Subject can give socially desirable or guarded responses
5. **Limited Validity Data:** Formal psychometric validation is limited
6. **Literacy Required:** Requires basic reading and writing skills
7. **Not Diagnostic:** Cannot be used to make formal diagnoses alone
8. **Examiner Bias:** Interpretation may be influenced by examiner's theoretical orientation
9. **Inter-Rater Variability:** Different raters may assign different scores
10. **Limited Depth:** Provides leads rather than deep personality exploration (compared to Rorschach)
11. **Outdated Items:** Some stems (e.g., "Before the war") need cultural adaptation
12. **Not Suitable for All Populations:** Difficult with severely disturbed, cognitively impaired, or very young subjects

## APPENDIX B: SCORING KEY REMINDERS

**Remember:**
- Score each ATTITUDE AREA (not individual items)
- Read ALL 4 items of an area together before scoring
- Consider both CONTENT (what is said) and MANNER (how it is said)
- When in doubt between 1 and 2, consider: "Does this person need therapy for this issue?"
  - If YES → Score 2
  - If they can manage on their own → Score 1
- When in doubt between 0 and 1, consider: "Is there ANY emotional conflict here?"
  - If YES → Score 1
  - If NO → Score 0

## APPENDIX D: IMPORTANT CLINICAL TIPS

### Do's:
1. Always read ALL items before scoring any area
2. Consider cultural context when interpreting responses
3. Use behavioral observations to supplement test data
4. Cross-validate findings with other assessment tools
5. Maintain professional objectivity in interpretation
6. Document the exact responses (verbatim) in the file
7. Note response latency and omissions
8. Consider the subject's educational level when evaluating responses
9. Report both strengths and areas of concern

### Don'ts:
1. Don't interpret single items in isolation
2. Don't over-pathologize culturally normative responses
3. Don't use SSCT as the sole basis for diagnosis
4. Don't share raw responses with unauthorized persons
5. Don't ignore the context (e.g., recent life events) when interpreting
6. Don't force subjects to complete items they refuse
7. Don't interpret without adequate training in projective assessment
8. Don't use outdated or poorly translated versions without adaptation
