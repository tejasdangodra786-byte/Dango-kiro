# Worksheet 07 — Special Issues Modules

**Phase 5 — Special Issues (interwoven as indicated)**
*Source basis: Harway (Ed.), Handbook of Couples Therapy — Ch. 15 (Illness/medical), Ch. 16 (Sexual abuse history), Ch. 17 (Physical aggression), Ch. 18 (Addiction), Ch. 19 (Infidelity), Ch. 20 (Religious/spiritual), Ch. 21 (Same-sex couples), Ch. 22 (Socioeconomic factors), Ch. 23 (Divorce).*

---

## How to use this worksheet (clinician instructions)

- **Purpose:** The *Handbook* devotes all of Section III to special presentations that require specific clinical adaptations. These modules are **not** meant to replace the session arc — they are **add-ons** or **sequence modifiers** to Worksheets 01–06.
- **When to use:** When the safety screen (Worksheet 01, Part A) or individual sessions (Worksheet 02) identify any of these issues.
- **Format:** Select the relevant module(s); integrate its assessment and intervention prompts into the treatment plan (Worksheet 03, Part D). Some modules (aggression, addiction) alter the standard sequence — follow the sequencing notes carefully.
- **Scope:** These modules are brief assessment-and-planning aids, not comprehensive treatments. Consult the corresponding *Handbook* chapter and the relevant primary clinical literature for depth.

## Module A — Couples Facing Illness / Medical Concerns (Ch. 15)

**Core framework:** The interaction between biological and emotional — illness disrupts the couple's roles, intimacy, and emotional regulation.

**Assessment questions:**
1. What is the medical condition / diagnosis? ___________________________
2. Who is the patient and who is the caregiver? ________________________
3. How has illness changed your daily roles and routines? _________________
4. How has illness affected your emotional connection and intimacy? _______
5. On a 0–10 scale, how well are you managing the emotional roller coaster? Partner A: ___ Partner B: ___

**Intervention prompts:**
- Help the couple develop shared understanding of the illness and its practical demands
- Address caregiver burnout and the patient's guilt/loss of autonomy
- Use emotional reactivity management techniques (Worksheet 04, Tool 6)
- Normalize grief for the "lost" relationship/life before the illness
- Support practical problem-solving for medical demands (Worksheet 06)

## Module B — Sexual-Abuse History (Ch. 16)

**Core framework:** A history of sexual abuse in one partner "may affect the overall couple's functioning and may lead to difficult therapeutic concerns."

**Safety-first sequencing:**
> "If sexual abuse is ruled out at intake, the standard clinical assessment protocol for couples may be implemented." If abuse IS present: individual trauma assessment and stabilization come first or alongside.

**Assessment questions (with the individual partner — Worksheet 02):**
1. Has the survivor received or is currently in individual trauma therapy? ☐ Y ☐ N
2. Is the partner aware of the abuse history? ☐ Y ☐ N ☐ Partially
3. How does the abuse history show up in the couple's dynamic? (triggers, avoidance, sexual difficulties, trust issues) __________
4. Does the partner feel they "walk on eggshells" or feel shut out? __________

**Intervention focus:**
- Psychoeducation: both partners learn how trauma affects the nervous system and relational behavior
- Pacing: go slow with emotional vulnerability (Worksheet 05) — stay within the survivor's window of tolerance
- Address the non-abused partner's feelings (helplessness, frustration) without centering them
- Refer for individual trauma work if not already in place
- Build a safety plan for triggered moments (link to Worksheet 04, Time-Out)

## Module C — Physical Aggression / Domestic Violence (Ch. 17)

**Safety-first sequencing:**
> Conjoint communication work can be **contraindicated** when active violence is present. This decision must be made at intake and revisited.

**Level-of-risk assessment (clinician completes):**

| Factor | Partner A → B | Partner B → A |
|--------|---------------|---------------|
| Type of aggression (physical, psychological, sexual, financial, technology) | | |
| Frequency and recency | | |
| Severity / injury | | |
| Fear level of the partner (scale 0–10) | | |
| Use of coercive control? | ☐ Y ☐ N | ☐ Y ☐ N |
| History of escalation? | ☐ Y ☐ N | ☐ Y ☐ N |
| Weapons involvement? | ☐ Y ☐ N | ☐ Y ☐ N |

**Decision tree:**
- **Active high-risk violence or coercive control →** Conjoint therapy is not appropriate at this time. Individual safety planning, refer to DV services, potentially batterer-intervention program.
- **Situational couple violence (low severity, mutual, no fear/coercion) →** Conjoint may proceed with modifications: Time-Out plan (Worksheet 04) must be in place first; topics are carefully titrated; aggression is monitored every session.

**Safety plan (always complete when any aggression history):**
- Emergency numbers (local DV hotline, crisis line): ________________________
- Safe person to call: __________
- Safe place to go: __________
- How to signal the therapist between sessions: __________

## Module D — Addiction (Ch. 18)

**Core framework:** Key elements of couples therapy integrated with treatment of addictive behaviors.

**Assessment:**
1. Substance(s) or behavior(s) of concern: _____________________________
2. Current use level and pattern: _____________________________________
3. Is the individual currently in treatment for the addiction? ☐ Y ☐ N
4. Stage of change (precontemplation / contemplation / preparation / action / maintenance): _____
5. How does the addiction affect the couple's functioning? __________________
6. Enabling / co-dependent patterns? __________________________________

**Sequencing note:** Active untreated addiction may need to be addressed (at minimum harm-reduction or individual addiction treatment) before deep couples work can be effective — the cycle cannot be interrupted if substances are fueling it.

**Intervention focus:**
- Psychoeducation: addiction as a biopsychosocial pattern affecting the couple system
- Map the cycle with addiction included (who uses when, what triggers it, how partner responds, how that response feeds the cycle)
- Rebuild trust (overlaps with Module F)
- Relapse-prevention planning that includes the couple (Worksheet 08)

## Module E — Infidelity (Ch. 19)

**Core framework:** Infidelity "affects a large number of couples and presents particular challenges." Ch. 19 proposes an effective model for intervention.

**Assessment:**
1. Nature of the infidelity (emotional, physical, online, ongoing/ended): __________________
2. How was it discovered? __________
3. When? __________ How recently? __________
4. Has the affair/contact ended? ☐ Y ☐ N ☐ Unclear
5. Disclosure status: ☐ Full disclosure ☐ Partial ☐ Still emerging
6. Impact on the betrayed partner (0–10 distress): _____
7. Remorse level of the involved partner (0–10): _____

**Phase-specific work:**
- **Phase 1 — Crisis stabilization:** Contain the acute emotional flooding. No major decisions yet. Rule out safety concerns.
- **Phase 2 — Understanding:** Why did it happen? (not to excuse, but to understand the relational context). Map it onto the cycle (Worksheet 03).
- **Phase 3 — Rebuilding trust:** Transparency, accountability, addressing the attachment injury (Worksheet 05, Tool 4). "Taking responsibility for transgressions, repairing damages, and expressing regret" (PAIRS).
- **Phase 4 — Decision:** Can the relationship be repaired? New commitment or transition to divorce (Module G)?

## Module F — Religious / Spiritual Issues (Ch. 20)

**Assessment:**
1. Spiritual/religious affiliation of each partner: Partner A _________ Partner B _________
2. Importance of spirituality to each (0–10): A ____ B ____
3. Points of harmony: __________
4. Points of conflict: __________
5. Are spiritual differences contributing to couple distress? __________

**Intervention focus:**
- Create a safe space to discuss spiritual beliefs without judgment
- Acknowledge spiritual differences as one form of cultural difference
- Explore how spiritual values can be a resource for healing
- Respect that therapy should not impose the therapist's beliefs

## Module G — Divorce / Dissolution (Ch. 23)

**Decision check-in:**
- Is one or both partners here to end the relationship rather than repair it? ☐ A ☐ B ☐ Both ☐ Neither
- Are they open to exploring the relationship first? ☐ Y ☐ N

**If proceeding toward dissolution:**
- Shift goals to a smooth, respectful transition
- Address co-parenting plan if children are involved
- Manage the emotional process of grief and loss
- Refer for legal/financial resources as needed
- Prevent adversarial dynamics that harm children or each other
