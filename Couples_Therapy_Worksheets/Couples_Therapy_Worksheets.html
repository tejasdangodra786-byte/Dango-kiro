Couples Therapy Worksheets — Clinician's Guide (How to Use)

Based on: Handbook of Couples Therapy, edited by Michele Harway (John Wiley & Sons, 2005).

This worksheet series translates the clinical frameworks from the Handbook of Couples Therapy into a usable session-by-session toolkit. It integrates the book's three organizing perspectives — developmental (life-cycle stages), theoretical (CBCT, EFT, Narrative, Strategic/Solution-Focused, Object Relations, PAIRS, Bowen, Feminist/Contextual), and situational (illness, abuse, aggression, addiction, infidelity, divorce). Content paraphrased and reorganized for clinical use.

1. Who this is for

Licensed or supervised mental-health professionals (MFTs, psychologists, counselors, social workers) conducting conjoint couples therapy.

2. The arc of treatment at a glance

A typical course runs 12–20 sessions; brief work compresses it, complex cases extend it.

3. How to use the worksheets with couples

  1. Lead with the alliance, not the forms.
  2. Stay non-blaming and multidirectionally partial.
  3. Assess before you intervene.
  4. Screen for safety first.
  5. Weave culture and context through everything.
  6. Match the worksheet to the model you practice. CBCT → Worksheet 06; EFT → Worksheet 05; Solution-Focused → Worksheets 03 & 15.

Practical mechanics:

  • Give each partner their own copy of individual-reflection worksheets.
  • Use joint worksheets as a shared in-session artifact.
  • Assign homework from Worksheet 15 every session.
  • Keep completed worksheets in the clinical file for the termination review (Worksheet 08).

5. Clinical notes & limitations

  • These are clinical tools, not a treatment manual or a substitute for training/supervision.
  • Safety overrides sequence. If violence, addiction, or acute risk is identified, pause the standard arc and follow Worksheet 07.
  • Document informed consent and your no-secrets policy before the individual sessions (Worksheet 02).

Worksheet 01 — First Session: Goals, Intake, Safety Screen & Alliance

Phase 1 — Assessment & Alliance · Session 1 Source basis: Harway (Ed.), Handbook of Couples Therapy — Ch. 1 (Setting the Stage), Ch. 2 (PAIRS premarital assessment), Ch. 3 (first years of commitment), Ch. 8 (CBCT functional assessment), Ch. 11 (EFT Stage 1 / Step 1 assessment & alliance), Ch. 16–17 (safety screening).

How to use this worksheet (clinician instructions)

  • Purpose of Session 1: establish the alliance, understand the couple's shared story, run a safety screen, surface the presenting problems, and set clear goals and expectations — not to solve problems or teach skills yet.
  • Format: Conjoint (both partners present). Book a longer first session (90–120 min) if possible. The PAIRS chapter stresses that the standard 50-minute hour "cannot build the trust, connectedness, and collaboration" an initial inquiry needs.
  • EFT alignment (Step 1): "In EFT the client is the relationship." Initiate and maintain a collaborative alliance in which each partner feels "sensitively listened to, understood, empathized with, and not blamed."
  • Stance: Warm, curious, multidirectionally partial (both partners feel joined). Reframe "who's at fault" into "what's the pattern."
  • Fill it in together on a shared surface for Parts C–F; complete Part B (safety) using your clinical interview and, if needed, brief separate check-ins.
  • End the session by setting up the individual sessions (Worksheet 02) and explaining your no-secrets / confidentiality policy.

Part A — The Explicit Goals of the First Session (clinician checklist)

Work through — and tick off — each of these first-session goals before you close:

  • ☐ Build a working alliance with both partners (neither feels you've sided with the other)
  • ☐ Create emotional safety so each partner can speak honestly
  • ☐ Hear the presenting problem from each partner in their own words
  • ☐ Understand the relationship story and context (how they got here)
  • ☐ Screen for safety (violence, abuse, addiction, acute risk) — see Part B
  • ☐ Assess suitability for conjoint couples therapy right now
  • ☐ Begin to observe the negative interaction cycle (don't interpret aloud yet)
  • ☐ Instill hope — name a strength and normalize that change is possible
  • ☐ Set preliminary goals and expectations for therapy
  • ☐ Establish the frame — confidentiality, no-secrets policy, fees, logistics
  • ☐ Agree on next steps (individual sessions, homework)

Worksheet 02 — Individual History Sessions

Phase 1 — Assessment & Alliance · Sessions 2–3 (one per partner) Source basis: Harway (Ed.), Handbook of Couples Therapy — Ch. 2 (PAIRS individual-interview format), Ch. 7 (Bowen: family-of-origin, differentiation), Ch. 9 (Object Relations: attachment patterns), Ch. 11 (EFT: attachment fears/needs), Ch. 14 (Feminist/Contextual: gendered expectations).

How to use this worksheet (clinician instructions)

  • Purpose: Gather each partner's individual social and emotional history without the other's presence. The PAIRS chapter describes two-hour individual interviews that "open windows to new understandings that have not previously been realized or considered."
  • Format: Individual session, one partner at a time. Aim for 60–120 min.
  • Stance: Empathic, curious, non-judgmental. This is an opportunity for the individual to disclose what might be uncomfortable to share in a joint session.
  • Confidentiality: Begin by restating your no-secrets policy (decided in Session 1). Make clear how information shared here will and won't be used in conjoint sessions.
  • What you're assessing: Family conditioning, attachment styles, models from the parental marriage, invisible loyalties, expectations of the relationship, sexual/abuse history, personal hopes and fears, and individual contributing factors.
  • Give each partner their own copy. They may fill in some answers before the session or you may complete it interview-style.
  • Safety check: This is the best time to check for domestic violence indicators privately — ask directly.

Worksheet 03 — Formulation, Feedback & Treatment Plan

Phase 2 — Formulation & Goals · Session 3–4 (joint feedback session) Source basis: Harway (Ed.), Handbook of Couples Therapy — Ch. 2 (PAIRS feedback session), Ch. 8 (CBCT functional assessment ➜ treatment plan), Ch. 11 (EFT cycle de-escalation formulation), Ch. 12 (Strategic/Solution-Focused: miracle question & scaling).

How to use this worksheet (clinician instructions)

  • Purpose: Share with the couple your understanding of their pattern ("the cycle"), set collaborative goals, and establish the treatment plan. This is the bridge between assessment and active intervention.
  • Format: Joint session, ideally 90 min.
  • Stance: Present the formulation as a shared cycle — not either partner's fault. The PAIRS chapter models "relabeling and reframing many past behaviors and intentions, pointing out how blame is often not the issue." The EFT chapter describes mapping the negative cycle so both partners can "see through the smoke" and recognize they are each caught in it.
  • How to deliver: Walk through each section aloud; write or draw the cycle diagram together on paper or whiteboard. Ask the couple to correct, add, or adjust — this is collaborative, not the therapist "announcing a verdict."
  • Important: Only share individual-session material that a partner has agreed may be shared (per your no-secrets policy). Summarize themes, not verbatim disclosures.

Worksheet 04 — Communication & De-escalation Skills

Phase 3 — De-escalation & Communication · Sessions 4–8 (and reinforced throughout) Source basis: Harway (Ed.), Handbook of Couples Therapy — Ch. 2 (PAIRS Dialogue Guide, "complaining without blaming," I-messages), Ch. 8 (CBCT communication: Satir's placator/blamer/distractor/computer; Gordon's I-messages; speaker–listener), Ch. 11 (EFT de-escalation of the negative cycle), Ch. 15 (managing emotional reactivity).

How to use this worksheet (clinician instructions)

  • Purpose: First active-change phase. Interrupt the negative cycle and build core communication skills: speaking without blame, listening with empathy, and de-escalating reactivity. The CBCT chapter names communication, problem-solving, and behavior exchange as the "lynchpins" of effective couples intervention — this worksheet covers communication.
  • Format: Joint sessions. Teach a skill, then have the couple practice it live in the room (enactment) while you coach. Do not just explain — the EFT and Strategic chapters both emphasize in-session enactments over didactics.
  • Prerequisite: Complete Phase 1–2 and the safety screen. If physical aggression is present, see Worksheet 07 before doing hot-topic communication work; teach the Time-Out tool first and keep topics low-intensity.
  • Pacing: Start with low-conflict topics; raise intensity only as the couple demonstrates skill. The therapist uses "shifts in intensity" (slowing tone, volume, pacing) to keep the room workable (Minuchin/EFT principle).

Tool 2 — I-Messages / Complaining Without Blaming (PAIRS Dialogue Guide)

The PAIRS Dialogue Guide is "a communication tool for complaining without blaming." Convert blame-statements into I-messages.

Formula:

"When ______ (specific situation/behavior), I felt ______ (emotion), because ______ (meaning/need). What I'd like is ______ (request)."

Practice conversions:

Blame statement ("you" message)I-message rewrite
"You never help around here."
"You always ignore me."
"You're so controlling."
(couple's own example)

Rule from PAIRS: "Express important hurt, fear, or irritation directly to each other in words, asking to be heard and understood with empathy. What is left unsaid is often more harmful than what is said."

Tool 3 — Speaker–Listener Technique (CBCT)

A structured turn-taking format that slows reactivity.

Speaker rules:

  • Speak for yourself (use I-messages)
  • Keep it brief — a few sentences, then pause
  • Don't mind-read or accuse

Listener rules:

  • Don't rebut, defend, or problem-solve
  • Paraphrase back what you heard: "What I hear you saying is…"
  • Check: "Did I get that right?"
  • Only then does the Speaker continue or hand over the floor

Tool 4 — Empathic Listening Checklist

For the Listener (adapted from PAIRS listening skills):

  • ☐ I made eye contact / turned toward my partner
  • ☐ I did not interrupt
  • ☐ I paraphrased what I heard before responding
  • ☐ I checked whether I understood correctly
  • ☐ I acknowledged my partner's feeling, even if I disagree with the content

Worksheet 05 — Emotion & Attachment Restructuring (EFT-informed)

Phase 4 — Restructuring the Bond · Sessions 8–14 Source basis: Harway (Ed.), Handbook of Couples Therapy — Ch. 11 (EFT: emotion, attachment, enactments, softening, attachment injuries), Ch. 9 (Object Relations: projective identification, internalized models), Ch. 15 (emotional reactivity).

How to use this worksheet (clinician instructions)

  • Purpose: Move from de-escalating the cycle (Phase 3) to restructuring the emotional bond. EFT frames couple distress as insecurity in the attachment bond: rigid interaction patterns organized by "absorbing affect states." The task is to access the softer, underlying emotions and attachment needs that drive the surface behavior, and to create new bonding interactions.
  • When to use: After the couple has achieved some de-escalation (they can talk about the cycle without the cycle taking over). EFT describes this as moving beyond "critical pursuit followed by distance and defensiveness."
  • Format: Joint sessions. Heavy use of in-session enactments — you direct partners to turn and speak the newly accessed emotion to each other, not to you.
  • Stance: Clients are the experts on their own experience; do not pathologize or label. "They are viewed as struggling with problems arising from a particular social context." Validate, reflect, and heighten emotion gently.
  • Caution: Deep emotional work can surface trauma or attachment injuries (including infidelity or abuse). Have Worksheet 07 ready; pace to the couple's window of tolerance.

Tool 4 — Attachment Injury Repair

An "attachment injury" is a moment of betrayal or abandonment at a critical time (e.g., not being there during a loss, an affair, a broken promise) that becomes a recurring wound blocking trust. EFT describes specific work on these injuries.

Identify the injury:

  • What happened, and when? _______________________________________
  • Why was this moment so critical for the injured partner? ________________
  • What did it come to mean about the relationship / your safety? ___________

Repair steps (guide the couple through, in order):

  1. Injured partner stays with and expresses the pain and its attachment meaning (not just the facts).
  2. Other partner stays present, hears the impact without defending, and acknowledges the hurt they caused.
  3. Other partner expresses genuine remorse and understanding of the injury's significance.
  4. Injured partner begins to risk asking for what they need now to feel safe.
  5. The couple creates a new, corrective bonding moment.
Links to PAIRS competency: "Seek forgiveness for hurts by taking responsibility, repairing damages, and expressing regret. Let go of grudges and choose to forgive."

Worksheet 12 — First-Session Goals & Expectations (Deep Dive)

Phase 1 — Assessment & Alliance · Session 1 (extended) Source basis: Harway (Ed.), Handbook of Couples Therapy — Ch. 1 (Setting the Stage), Ch. 2 (PAIRS format), Ch. 3 (nature of marriage/commitment), Ch. 11 (EFT alliance & assessment), Ch. 12 (Solution-Focused exception/scaling/miracle), Ch. 14 (Feminist/Contextual).

How to use this worksheet (clinician instructions)

  • Purpose: The first session must accomplish many things simultaneously. This worksheet provides a detailed walk-through of every goal to ensure nothing is missed and both partners leave with clarity, hope, and buy-in. It is a companion to Worksheet 01 — where 01 is the data-gathering form, 12 is the procedural guide for the clinician.
  • Format: Use as a session plan. Walk through sections in order; tick them off as you go. Add your clinical notes.
  • Time management: Budget time roughly as shown.

Goal 1 — Building the Alliance (15–20 min)

What the book says: "Initiating and maintaining a collaborative alliance in which each spouse experiences being sensitively listened to, understood, empathized with, and not blamed is essential." (EFT, Ch. 11)

How to do it:

  • ☐ Welcome both partners warmly; normalize nervousness
  • ☐ Explain your role: "I'm not a judge — I'm here to help both of you"
  • ☐ Brief overview of how couples therapy works (active, skills-based, both are expected to work)
  • ☐ "What questions or concerns do you have before we begin?"
  • ☐ Maintain balance: roughly equal eye contact, time, and curiosity for each

Clinical note: In the first 5 minutes, partners are scanning for "is this therapist going to side with them?" Explicit multidirectional partiality reassures: "I'm on the side of your relationship, not one of you against the other."

Goal 2 — Setting the Frame (10 min)

  • ☐ Confidentiality and its limits (duty to report, etc.)
  • ☐ No-secrets policy: What happens if one partner discloses something individually? (State your policy: ☐ No secrets — anything told individually may be brought into conjoint work ☐ Limited confidentiality — I will encourage but not force disclosure ☐ Other: ______)
  • ☐ Attendance expectation: both partners attend together unless individual sessions are scheduled
  • ☐ Session frequency, expected length of treatment, fees/cancellation
  • ☐ How to handle crises between sessions
  • ☐ Consent form signed ☐

Goal 3 — Understanding the Relationship Story (15–20 min)

Use the prompts from Worksheet 01, Part C. Key information to gather:

  • ☐ How they met and what attracted them
  • ☐ Decision to commit
  • ☐ High points and turning points
  • ☐ Current life-cycle stage and stressors
  • ☐ Family-of-origin context (brief; detailed in Worksheet 02 and 13)

Clinician note: Nichols (Ch. 3) stresses that "the first year or so has been identified as the most difficult time of adjustment in marriage" and "manifold needs are expected to be satisfied in marriage" (citing Lewin). Understand the mismatch between expectations and reality.

Goal 4 — Hearing the Presenting Problem (15 min)

  • ☐ Each partner states their concerns uninterrupted (therapist manages the floor)
  • ☐ Use the Presenting Problems Checklist (Worksheet 10) to ensure breadth
  • ☐ Note convergences and divergences in their reports
  • ☐ Map the presenting problem to one of Haley's three entry points: ☐ Individual symptom ☐ Child's problem ☐ Direct couples issue

Goal 5 — Conducting the Safety Screen (5–10 min)

  • ☐ Use Worksheet 01, Part B
  • ☐ Ask directly about violence, fear, substance use, and acute risk
  • ☐ If flags arise, document and modify plan immediately (see Worksheet 07)
  • ☐ Do not skip this — violence is often undisclosed in conjoint sessions

Goal 6 — Observing the Cycle (continuous throughout)

You don't interpret this aloud yet — you observe and note for the formulation (Worksheet 03).

What to observe:

  • Who speaks first? Who defers?
  • What happens when one raises a complaint? (Does the other defend, withdraw, counterattack, placate?)
  • Nonverbal: eye contact, body orientation, sighing, eye-rolling, touching, pulling away
  • Satir styles (Worksheet 04): who placates, blames, distracts, computes, or levels?
  • Emotional temperature: who is hot/flooding, who is shut down?

Goal 7 — Instilling Hope (5 min)

Why it matters: Couples come in demoralized. Naming strengths and normalizing struggle gives them a reason to return.

  • ☐ Name at least one specific strength you observed: "I notice that even with all the pain, you both showed up. That says something about your commitment."
  • ☐ Normalize: "Many couples face exactly these kinds of struggles. They're addressable."
  • ☐ Briefly preview: "In the next sessions we'll understand the pattern and build specific tools."

Goal 8 — Setting Preliminary Goals & Expectations (5–10 min)

  • ☐ "If therapy goes well, what would be different?" (each partner answers)
  • ☐ Scaling: "On a 0–10, where's the relationship now?" (each answers)
  • ☐ "What would one point higher look like?"
  • ☐ Set realistic expectations: "This takes active work — from both of you, between sessions too."
  • ☐ Clarify: this is a first pass; full goals come after assessment (Worksheet 03)

Goal 9 — Closing & Next Steps (5 min)

  • ☐ Summarize briefly what you heard (show you listened)
  • ☐ Schedule individual sessions (Worksheet 02, 13)
  • ☐ Assign small homework from Worksheet 15 (e.g., notice one positive thing daily)
  • ☐ Check in: "How was this for each of you?"
  • ☐ Session rating (Worksheet 15)
