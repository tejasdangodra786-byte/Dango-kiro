# Worksheet 04 — Communication & De-escalation Skills

**Phase 3 — De-escalation & Communication · Sessions 4–8 (and reinforced throughout)**
*Source basis: Harway (Ed.), Handbook of Couples Therapy — Ch. 2 (PAIRS Dialogue Guide, "complaining without blaming," I-messages), Ch. 8 (CBCT communication: Satir's placator/blamer/distractor/computer; Gordon's I-messages; speaker–listener), Ch. 11 (EFT de-escalation of the negative cycle), Ch. 15 (managing emotional reactivity).*

---

## How to use this worksheet (clinician instructions)

- **Purpose:** First active-change phase. Interrupt the negative cycle and build core communication skills: speaking without blame, listening with empathy, and de-escalating reactivity. The CBCT chapter names communication, problem-solving, and behavior exchange as the "lynchpins" of effective couples intervention — this worksheet covers communication.
- **Format:** Joint sessions. Teach a skill, then **have the couple practice it live in the room (enactment)** while you coach. Do not just explain — the EFT and Strategic chapters both emphasize in-session enactments over didactics.
- **Prerequisite:** Complete Phase 1–2 and the safety screen. If physical aggression is present, see Worksheet 07 **before** doing hot-topic communication work; teach the Time-Out tool first and keep topics low-intensity.
- **Pacing:** Start with low-conflict topics; raise intensity only as the couple demonstrates skill. The therapist uses "shifts in intensity" (slowing tone, volume, pacing) to keep the room workable (Minuchin/EFT principle).

## Tool 1 — Identify Your Communication Style Under Stress (Satir)

Each partner circles the style they fall into most under stress (CBCT, Ch. 8, citing Satir):

| Style | What it sounds like |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Placator** | "It's fine, whatever you want, I'm sorry" — appeasing, self-erasing |
| **Blamer** | "You always… you never…" — accusing, fault-finding |
| **Distractor** | Changes subject, jokes, goes off-topic to avoid |
| **Computer** | Over-rational, cold, detached, "just the facts" |
| **Leveler** (goal) | Honest, direct, congruent — words match feelings |

- Partner A's usual style: ___________ Partner B's usual style: ___________
- **Goal:** move toward *leveling* — congruent, honest, non-blaming communication.

## Tool 2 — I-Messages / Complaining Without Blaming (PAIRS Dialogue Guide)

The PAIRS Dialogue Guide is "a communication tool for complaining without blaming." Convert blame-statements into I-messages.

**Formula:**
> "When ______ (specific situation/behavior), I felt ______ (emotion), because ______ (meaning/need). What I'd like is ______ (request)."

**Practice conversions:**

| Blame statement ("you" message) | I-message rewrite |
|----------------------------------|-------------------|
| "You never help around here." | |
| "You always ignore me." | |
| "You're so controlling." | |
| *(couple's own example)* | |

**Rule from PAIRS:** "Express important hurt, fear, or irritation directly to each other in words, asking to be heard and understood with empathy. What is left unsaid is often more harmful than what is said."

## Tool 3 — Speaker–Listener Technique (CBCT)

A structured turn-taking format that slows reactivity.

**Speaker rules:**
- Speak for yourself (use I-messages)
- Keep it brief — a few sentences, then pause
- Don't mind-read or accuse

**Listener rules:**
- Don't rebut, defend, or problem-solve
- Paraphrase back what you heard: *"What I hear you saying is…"*
- Check: *"Did I get that right?"*
- Only then does the Speaker continue or hand over the floor

**In-session practice log:**

## Tool 4 — Empathic Listening Checklist

For the Listener (adapted from PAIRS listening skills):

- [ ] I made eye contact / turned toward my partner
- [ ] I did not interrupt
- [ ] I paraphrased what I heard before responding
- [ ] I checked whether I understood correctly
- [ ] I acknowledged my partner's feeling, even if I disagree with the content
- [ ] I set aside my rebuttal to truly hear them first

> **PAIRS competency:** "Give up being right. Invite and express diversity. Welcome differences as sources of vigor, perspective, and healthy growth."

## Tool 5 — Uncovering "Love Knots" / Hidden Expectations (PAIRS)

"Love Knots" are hidden expectations and mind-reading assumptions — e.g., *"If you really loved me, you would know…"*

Each partner lists hidden expectations they've been holding:

| "If you really loved me, you would…" | Have you actually told your partner this? |
|---------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| | ☐ Y ☐ N |
| | ☐ Y ☐ N |
| | ☐ Y ☐ N |

**Discussion:** How might unspoken expectations be fueling disappointment? Practice stating the expectation directly as a request.

## Tool 6 — The Time-Out / De-escalation Plan (managing emotional reactivity, Ch. 15)

When reactivity spikes, the couple needs an agreed exit ramp. (Essential when aggression history is present — see Worksheet 07.)

**Our Time-Out agreement:**
- Our signal word/gesture to call a time-out: __________________
- Either partner can call it — no questions asked.
- During a time-out we will: (go to separate rooms · take a walk · breathe · no following/pursuing)
- We will come back and resume within ______ minutes/hours.
- Self-soothing I will do during the break (Partner A): __________________
- Self-soothing I will do during the break (Partner B): __________________

**Physiological self-regulation:** notice the body's early warning signs (racing heart, heat, tight chest). Name them before escalation: Partner A's signs ____________ Partner B's signs ____________
