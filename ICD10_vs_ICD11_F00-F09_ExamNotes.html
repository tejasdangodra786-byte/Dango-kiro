The ONE big idea you must say in the exam

ICD-11 ICD-10 put everything with a physical brain/body cause under one word — "organic" (meaning: caused by a diagnosable brain or body disease, not "made up") — in the block F00–F09.

ICD-11 drops the word "organic" (because all mental disorders have a biological basis) and splits the block into two:

  • Neurocognitive disorders (problems where thinking/memory is the MAIN issue) → delirium, mild neurocognitive disorder, amnestic disorder, the dementias (codes 6D70–6D86).
  • Secondary mental/behavioural syndromes ("secondary" = caused by another medical illness) → mood, psychosis, anxiety, catatonia, personality change due to a physical illness (codes 6E60–6E69).
🧭 One-line answer: "ICD-11 replaced the ICD-10 'organic' block (F00–F09) with two clearer groups — Neurocognitive disorders (cognition-led) and Secondary mental syndromes (symptom-led, caused by a medical illness) — kept the same diseases, added severity codes and behaviour codes, and still needs two codes (the syndrome + the cause)."

Dementia — the general idea (F00–F03 / 6D80–6D85)

Simple definition: a slow, long-lasting drop in thinking abilities bad enough to make daily life hard, while the person stays awake and alert (consciousness is clear — this is what separates it from delirium).

ICD-10 says dementia hits many higher brain functions — memory, thinking, orientation (knowing the time, place and who you are), understanding, calculation, learning, language and judgement — and consciousness is not clouded (not drowsy/confused in level of alertness).

ICD-11 says the same but adds numbers: decline in 2 or more cognitive domains (thinking areas: memory, attention, planning/"executive function", language, social behaviour, speed, seeing-and-space skills), enough to reduce independence in ADLs (activities of daily living — washing, dressing, eating, toilet).

Exam trap: if memory/thinking problems happen only during low mood, do not call it dementia — think depression first ("pseudodementia" = depression pretending to be dementia). ICD-11
🧭 Dementia signature: "Acquired (came on later in life, not born with it), lasting, many-area thinking decline, with clear alertness, that steals daily independence."

F00 · Dementia in Alzheimer's disease → 6D80

ICD-10 F00 (.0 early, .1 late, .2 mixed, .9 unspecified)
ICD-11 6D80 (.0 early, .1 late, .2 mixed w/ stroke, .3 mixed w/ other, .Z unknown)
Status Kept (renamed slightly)
Change Added "mixed with stroke" type; severity + behaviour coded separately.

Simple definition: the commonest dementia — a slow brain-wasting disease that eats away memory first, then other thinking. Insidious onset (starts so slowly nobody notices the exact day) and slowly gets worse over years. ICD-10

What's happening inside: sticky amyloid plaques (clumps of protein between brain cells) and neurofibrillary tangles (twisted "tau" protein threads inside brain cells) build up, brain cells die (especially in the hippocampus — the brain's memory-saving centre), and there is a big drop in acetylcholine (a memory chemical messenger — this is why we give "cholinesterase inhibitor" drugs that raise it). K&S

Criteria in simple words

Must haveMeaning
1. Dementia presentMeets the general dementia rule above.
2. Slow, sneaky start; slow worseningNo sudden jump; declines smoothly over months–years.
3. No other causeTests rule out other causes (thyroid, B12, stroke, fluid on the brain, drugs).
4. No early focal signs (one-sided weakness/numbness, etc.)Early one-sided body signs point to stroke instead.

Sub-types: F00.0 early-onset (starts before 65, faster, early aphasia — trouble with language, apraxia — can't do learned movements like dressing); F00.1 late-onset (after 65, slower, memory the main problem). ICD-10

⭐ UNIQUE CLUES — "see this → think Alzheimer"

  • ★ Memory-FIRST decline (forgets recent talks, repeats questions) with a smooth slow slide over years.
  • ★ Anosognosia (the patient does not realise/denies they have a memory problem) — family is worried, patient is not.
  • ★ Gets lost on familiar routes; loses recent memory but keeps old memories longer.
  • ★ MRI shows hippocampus/temporal-lobe shrinkage.
  • ★ Down syndrome patients almost all develop Alzheimer changes early (extra chromosome 21 carries the amyloid gene). K&S
🧭 Signature: slow + memory-first + alert + no early one-sided signs = Alzheimer.

Treatment (simple)

  • Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine — raise the memory chemical acetylcholine) for mild–moderate; memantine (calms over-active "glutamate" signals) for moderate–severe.
  • For behaviour problems: treat the cause first; use antipsychotics only if serious risk and briefly (they raise stroke/death risk in dementia). K&S
  • Non-drug: routine, orientation, safety, carer education/support.

Prognosis: irreversible (cannot be cured/reversed) and progressive; drugs slow symptoms, not the disease. ICD-10

F01 · Vascular dementia → 6D81

ICD-10 F01 (.0 acute, .1 multi-infarct, .2 subcortical, .3 mixed, .8/.9)
ICD-11 6D81 Dementia due to cerebrovascular disease
Status Renamed / reorganised
Change Renamed; old sub-types not kept as separate codes.

Simple definition: dementia caused by cerebrovascular disease (disease of the brain's blood vessels — strokes or long-term small-vessel damage). Brain tissue dies where blood supply is cut, so thinking drops. ICD-10

Criteria in simple words

Must haveMeaning
1. Dementia presentGeneral dementia rule met.
2. Patchy / uneven deficits (some thinking areas hit hard, others spared)Not smooth all-over decline like Alzheimer.
3. Focal brain signs (one-sided weakness, brisk reflexes, upgoing toe, pseudobulbar palsy)Pseudobulbar palsy = poor control of face/throat muscles with sudden crying/laughing.
4. Evidence of vascular diseaseStroke history, high BP, or scan showing infarcts/white-matter damage.

⭐ UNIQUE CLUES — "see this → think Vascular dementia"

  • ★ Stepwise decline (drops suddenly, stays flat, then drops again — like going down stairs), OR starts right after a stroke.
  • ★ Focal signs early (one-sided weakness) + gait/walking problems early.
  • ★ Planning & speed of thinking hit before memory (memory can be okay early — opposite of Alzheimer).
  • ★ Strong vascular risk factors (high BP, diabetes, smoking, irregular heartbeat/AF).
  • ★ Emotional lability / "emotional incontinence" (crying or laughing that they can't control).
🧭 Signature: stepwise or post-stroke + focal signs + vascular risks = vascular dementia.

Treatment (simple)

  • Control the risk factors — this is the key: blood pressure, sugar, cholesterol, antiplatelets/anticoagulants (blood-thinning drugs to prevent more strokes). K&S
  • Treat mood; physiotherapy for walking; carer support. Controlling risks can slow it.

F02 · Dementia in other diseases → 6D82 (Lewy), 6D83 (FTD), 6D85 (others)

ICD-10 F02 (.0 Pick, .1 CJD, .2 Huntington, .3 Parkinson, .4 HIV, .8 other)
ICD-11 6D82 Lewy body · 6D83 Frontotemporal · 6D85 other diseases
Status Split / reorganised
Change Lewy body got its own code; Pick's widened to frontotemporal.

Simple definition: dementias that are a part/result of a named disease diagnosed elsewhere. You must give two codes — the dementia + the disease causing it. ICD-10

The high-yield members — each with its own unique clue

⭐ Dementia with Lewy bodies (6D82) — the "3 core" disease

Caused by Lewy bodies (clumps of "alpha-synuclein" protein inside brain cells). Remember the trio:

  • ★ Fluctuating thinking/alertness (good hour, then very confused hour).
  • ★ Detailed visual hallucinations (clearly seeing people/animals that aren't there) — often small figures/children.
  • ★ Parkinsonism (slowness, stiffness, tremor — like Parkinson's) that starts around the same time as the dementia.
  • ★ REM-sleep behaviour disorder (acting out dreams — kicking/shouting in sleep), often years before.
  • ★★ DANGER: neuroleptic (antipsychotic) sensitivity (antipsychotic drugs cause severe, even fatal, reactions — AVOID them). K&S

⭐ Frontotemporal dementia (6D83) — "personality first, memory later"

  • ★ Younger patient (50s–60s).
  • ★ Behaviour/personality change FIRST — disinhibition (rude, tactless, socially inappropriate acts), apathy (no motivation/interest), loss of empathy.
  • ★ Memory relatively okay early (opposite of Alzheimer).
  • ★ Language forms: progressive non-fluent aphasia (speech becomes slow/effortful) or semantic dementia (loses the meaning of words).
  • ★ MRI: frontal + temporal shrinkage. (ICD-11 needs a 2nd code 8A23.)

⭐ Other 6D85 causes — one clue each

Disease (code)The unique "give-away" clue
Prion / CJD (6D85.5)VERY rapid dementia (weeks–months) + myoclonus (sudden muscle jerks); startle jerks; fatal fast. (prion = infectious mis-folded protein)
Huntington (6D85.1)Chorea (quick, dance-like involuntary movements) + family history (passed parent-to-child) + early psychiatric/behaviour change.
Parkinson (6D85.0)Established Parkinson's (tremor, stiffness, slow movement) for years first, then dementia comes.
HIV (6D85.3)Subcortical pattern (slowed thinking & movement, apathy — deep-brain type); improves with anti-HIV drugs.
Normal-pressure hydrocephalus (6D85.6)Triad: "wet, wacky, wobbly" = urine incontinence + dementia + odd wide "magnetic" walk; can be REVERSED with a shunt (a tube to drain excess brain fluid).
Pellagra (6D85.8)3 Ds = Dementia + Diarrhoea + Dermatitis (skin rash); from niacin (vitamin B3) deficiency. Reversible with vitamin.
Down syndrome (6D85.9)Early Alzheimer-type dementia in a person with Down syndrome.

F03 · Unspecified dementia → 6D8Z

Simple definition: dementia is definitely there, but the exact cause is not yet known or doesn't fit a named type. It's a temporary label — keep looking for the cause, especially reversible ones. ICD-10 ICD-11

⭐ Exam point

★ "Unspecified" should never end the workup — always screen for treatable causes (thyroid, B12, NPH, depression, drugs) before accepting it.

F04 · Organic amnesic syndrome → 6D72 Amnestic disorder

ICD-10 F04
ICD-11 6D72 (.10 alcohol, .11 sedatives, .12 other, .13 inhalants)
Status Renamed
Change "organic" dropped; alcohol/drug memory loss now sub-codes here.

Simple definition: a striking, memory-ONLY problem — the person can't form new memories, while attention and general cleverness stay normal. ICD-10

Criteria in simple words

Must haveMeaning
1. Anterograde amnesia (can't make NEW memories after the illness started)Forgets things minutes later; can't learn a new list.
+ often retrograde amnesia (loses OLD memories from before the illness)Usually recent past lost more than distant past.
2. Immediate recall preserved (can repeat a phone number right away)Shows attention is intact — separates it from delirium.
3. No general thinking decline, alertNot dementia (only memory hit) and not delirium (alert).
4. A real physical cause founde.g. thiamine lack, low oxygen, head injury, brain infection.

⭐ UNIQUE CLUE — the classic exam link you asked for

★★ Alcohol stopping / long alcohol use → thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency → Wernicke → Korsakoff.

  • Wernicke encephalopathy (the acute, reversible emergency) = triad of confusion + ataxia (unsteady walking) + ophthalmoplegia/nystagmus (abnormal eye movements). Give thiamine FAST.
  • If untreated it becomes Korsakoff syndrome (the chronic memory-loss stage) — the classic amnestic syndrome.
  • ★ Confabulation (making up false memories to fill the gaps — NOT lying on purpose) is the give-away of Korsakoff.
  • Damage is to mammillary bodies & thalamus (deep memory-relay parts of the brain). K&S

Note on coding: in ICD-10 the alcohol form sits in F10; in ICD-11 it is coded here as 6D72.10.

🧭 Signature: can't learn anything new + makes up stories + normal attention/IQ + alcohol/thiamine history = Korsakoff amnestic syndrome. Give thiamine.

F05 · Delirium → 6D70

ICD-10 F05 (.0 not on dementia, .1 on dementia, .8/.9)
ICD-11 6D70 (.0 disease, .1 substance, .2 multiple, .Y/.Z)
Status Renamed / reorganised
Change Sorted by CAUSE now, not by "with/without dementia".

Simple definition: a sudden, come-and-go "brain failure" — over hours the person's attention (ability to focus) and awareness (knowing what's around them) break down, caused by a physical illness. It is a medical emergency and usually reversible if the cause is fixed. ICD-11

Criteria in simple words (ICD-10 wants all areas)

AreaMeaning
1. Attention + alertness disturbedCan't focus, hold or shift attention; alertness goes up and down.
2. Thinking/perception disturbedIllusions/hallucinations (seeing/hearing wrong things — often VISUAL); muddled thinking; poor recent memory; disorientation (loses track of time/place).
3. Psychomotor change (activity level)Either too restless or too slow; shifts between the two.
4. Sleep–wake upsetAwake at night, drowsy by day; worse in evening.
5. Emotion upsetFear, irritability, low mood, or confusion of feelings.
6. Onset + courseRapid start, fluctuates through the day, usually < 6 months. ICD-10

⭐ UNIQUE CLUES — "see this → think Delirium"

  • ★ Attention is the hallmark — bedside test: say the months backwards; a delirious patient fails it.
  • ★ Fluctuates within a day and sundowning (clearly worse in the evening/night).
  • ★ Hypoactive type is the trap (quiet, drowsy, withdrawn — often mistaken for depression and MISSED) — carries worse outcomes.
  • ★ Hallucinations are usually VISUAL (unlike schizophrenia which is usually voices).
  • ★ Special link: alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal → "delirium tremens" (severe withdrawal delirium with shakes, sweating, fast heart) — this is the ONE delirium where benzodiazepines are the treatment, not the enemy.
🧭 Signature: sudden + fluctuating + attention gone + a sick body = delirium (find & fix the cause).

Treatment (simple)

  • Treat the cause (infection, drugs, low oxygen, electrolytes) — this is the real cure.
  • Non-drug first: reorient, familiar faces, glasses/hearing aids, light by day/dark by night, fluids, avoid restraints and avoid deliriogenic drugs (medicines that worsen delirium — e.g. anticholinergics).
  • Drugs only for severe distress/danger — small dose antipsychotic short-term; benzodiazepines only for alcohol/sedative withdrawal. K&S

F06 · Other mental disorders due to brain/body disease → 6E6x (secondary) + 6D71

ICD-10 F06 (.0–.7)
ICD-11 6E61/6E62/6E63/6E65/6E67 + 6D71
Status Moved / reorganised
Change Psychiatric symptoms caused by illness become "secondary syndromes"; mild cognitive disorder becomes 6D71.

Simple definition: here the MAIN problem is a psychiatric symptom (hallucinations, delusions, mood, anxiety, catatonia), but it is caused by a physical illness or drug, not a primary mental illness — and thinking is fairly normal. ICD-11 calls these secondary syndromes ("secondary" = a symptom that is the result of another medical condition). ICD-11

ICD-10ICD-11Simple meaning + unique clue
F06.0/.2 hallucinosis/delusional6E61 secondary psychoticHallucinations/delusions from illness. ⭐ Clue: VISUAL hallucinations + clear mind + a medical cause → think secondary, not schizophrenia.
F06.1 catatonic6E67 secondary catatoniaCatatonia (frozen/mute or wildly over-active movement) from a medical cause.
F06.3 mood6E62 secondary mood⭐ Clue: first-ever mania in an older adult, or depression appearing exactly with a new illness/steroid → think secondary mood syndrome.
F06.4 anxiety6E63 secondary anxiety⭐ Clue: sudden panic + racing heart from hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid) or phaeochromocytoma (an adrenaline-releasing tumour).
F06.5 dissociative6E65 secondary dissociativeDissociation from a medical cause.
F06.6 emotionally labile (asthenic)— (no exact match)Emotional lability (easily tearful/uncontrolled emotion) + tiredness, often after stroke.
F06.7 mild cognitive disorder6D71 mild neurocognitive disorderEarly small thinking decline, daily life still independent (not yet dementia). ⭐ Some progress to dementia, some stay stable, some reverse.

⭐ Master rule: when should you suspect a "secondary/organic" cause instead of a primary mental illness?

  • ★ Wrong age (first psychosis or first mania in an older adult).
  • ★ Visual hallucinations (rather than voices).
  • ★ Symptoms started exactly with a new illness, drug, or lab abnormality.
  • ★ Abnormal physical exam/tests, fluctuating thinking, or poor response to usual psychiatric treatment.

Treatment idea: treat the physical cause (fix thyroid, stop the offending drug) — the psychiatric symptoms often melt away; add symptom treatment as needed. K&S

F07 · Personality & behaviour change due to brain disease → 6E68

ICD-10 F07 (.0 personality, .1 postencephalitic, .2 postconcussional)
ICD-11 6E68 secondary personality change (.1/.2 no exact match)
Status Moved / renamed
Change "Organic personality disorder" → "secondary personality change".

Simple definition (F07.0 → 6E68): after a brain injury/disease the person's personality changes from how they used to be — usually from frontal-lobe damage (front of the brain — controls judgement, impulse, planning). Thinking/memory can test near-normal, but they are "a different person". ICD-10

⭐ UNIQUE CLUES — "see this → think organic/secondary personality change"

  • ★ A clear change from a previous baseline AFTER a brain event (NOT lifelong like a primary personality disorder).
  • ★ Disinhibition (rude, impulsive, socially/sexually inappropriate) OR apathy (flat, no drive).
  • ★ Poor executive function (planning, self-control, judgement) on testing, with fairly intact memory.
  • ★ Classic exam story: frontal-lobe head injury or frontal tumour → "same intellect, different person".

The two post-injury syndromes

  • F07.1 postencephalitic syndrome — leftover behaviour/mood change after recovering from encephalitis (brain infection/inflammation); often improves.
  • F07.2 postconcussional syndrome — after a head injury: headache, dizziness, tiredness, poor concentration, irritability, poor sleep. ⭐ In ICD-11 these have no exact code — handled in the neurology chapter + secondary syndromes.

Mnemonics

Reversible causes of confusion/dementia — "DEMENTIA-R"
Drugs · Endocrine (thyroid) · Metabolic/electrolytes · Emotional (depression) · Nutrition (B12, thiamine) · Tumour/structural · Infection · Alcohol · R = normal-pressure hydRocephalus.

Use: always run this before accepting an "untreatable" dementia — these can be fixed.

Wernicke triad — "COAT"
COnfusion · Ataxia (unsteady walk) · T = eye signs (ophthalmoplegia/nystagmus). Untreated → Korsakoff (confabulation + amnesia). Give thiamine.
Lewy body core — "FAV-R"
Fluctuation · Antipsychotic sensitivity (avoid!) · Visual hallucinations · REM-sleep behaviour disorder + parkinsonism.
NPH triad — "Wet, Wacky, Wobbly"
Urine incontinence (wet) · dementia (wacky) · odd gait (wobbly). Reversible with a shunt.
Delirium management — "TIME"
Think of it (test attention) · Investigate + treat the cause · Manage without drugs first · Engage family & reassess.
ICD-11 codes — "70-71-72, then 80-to-86"
6D70 Delirium · 6D71 Mild NCD · 6D72 Amnestic · 6D80 Alzheimer · 6D81 Vascular · 6D82 Lewy · 6D83 Frontotemporal · 6D84 Substance · 6D85 Other diseases · 6D86 BPSD.
